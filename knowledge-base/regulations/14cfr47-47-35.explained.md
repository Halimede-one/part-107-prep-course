This section covers re-registering an aircraft that was registered in the United States before. The owner may register it under this part if the owner complies with §§ 47.3, 47.7, 47.8, 47.9, 47.11, 47.13, 47.15, and 47.17, as applicable (see those sections for what each requires), and submits with the Aircraft Registration Application, AC Form 8050-1, an Aircraft Bill of Sale, AC Form 8050-2, signed by the seller, or an equivalent conveyance, or other evidence of ownership authorized by § 47.11. The ownership paperwork has to connect back to the last registered owner. If you bought the aircraft directly from the last registered owner, the conveyance runs from that owner to you. If you did not, you must submit conveyances or other instruments showing consecutive transactions from the last registered owner, through each intervening owner, to you — an unbroken chain with no missing links. Paragraph (b) provides a fallback: if for good reason you cannot produce that evidence, you submit other evidence satisfactory to the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA). That may be an affidavit stating why you cannot produce the required evidence, along with whatever further evidence is available to prove the transaction.

Illustration: You buy a large unmanned aircraft that was previously registered in the United States. The person selling it to you is not the name on the last registration — he bought it from a company that had bought it from the registered owner. A bill of sale from your seller alone will not satisfy this section. You need conveyances showing each step: registered owner to the company, company to your seller, your seller to you. You contact the company, obtain a copy of its signed bill of sale to your seller, and file all three documents with your AC Form 8050-1. The chain is now consecutive from the last registered owner to you, so the application can go forward.

Common misreading: People read paragraph (b) as an easy way around a missing document. It is not. It requires a good reason why the evidence cannot be produced, and the substitute evidence must be satisfactory to the FAA. An affidavit is offered as an example of what that other evidence may be, and the section says it should come accompanied by whatever further evidence is available — so a bare statement that you cannot find the paperwork is not the same thing as satisfying this section.
