This section covers what happens when the air pressure gets unusually high. Barometric pressure is the weight of the atmosphere, measured here in inches of mercury. When any information shows that pressure along the route of flight already exceeds, or is expected to exceed, 31 inches of mercury, the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) Administrator can set special flight requirements and publish them in a Notice to Airmen (NOTAM). Once that notice is issued, no person may operate an aircraft or start a flight in a way that goes against those published requirements. Part 107 brings this section in by reference, so a remote pilot is a person operating an aircraft for this purpose. Paragraph (b) gives the Administrator authority to waive any restriction issued under paragraph (a), but only for a narrow purpose: to let emergency supply, transport, or medical services reach isolated communities, and only where the operation can be conducted with an acceptable level of safety.

Illustration: A remote pilot in North Dakota plans a morning mapping flight during a severe cold snap. During the preflight check of notices, a NOTAM issued under this section is in effect because barometric pressure in the area has climbed past 31 inches of mercury, and it sets specific requirements for operations in that airspace. The pilot cannot simply launch and ignore it. The flight has to be conducted in line with whatever that NOTAM requires, because this section says no person may operate an aircraft or initiate a flight contrary to those published requirements. If the pilot cannot meet them, the flight does not go.

Common misreading: Two things trip people up. First, 31 inches of mercury is not itself a flight prohibition. The high pressure is the trigger that lets the Administrator publish requirements in a NOTAM; what binds you is the NOTAM, so you have to read it. Second, the waiver in paragraph (b) is not a general-purpose option a remote pilot can request for a routine job. The chunk limits it to emergency supply, transport, or medical services being delivered to isolated communities.
