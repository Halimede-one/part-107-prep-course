This is the step-by-step list for applying for a remote pilot certificate by the route that uses the free online course rather than the in-person knowledge test. Step 1: take the online Part 107 Small Unmanned Aircraft Systems (small UAS) training on the FAA Safety Team (FAASTeam) site at https://www.faasafety.gov and get a completion certificate. Step 2: fill out FAA Form 8710-13, the Remote Pilot Certificate and/or Rating Application. The chunk gives two ways to do this. Option 1 is online through IACRA, the Integrated Airman Certification and Rating Application system at https://iacra.faa.gov/iacra/, and the chunk says this is how it should be done in almost all cases; proof that you finished the online training or passed an initial aeronautical knowledge test must be included, and the official document or documents get uploaded into IACRA by either the applicant or the certifying official. Option 2 is paper, and the chunk warns that processing time will be longer. On paper, the online training completion certificate must be included with the application. The chunk also notes a part 61 pilot who meets the requirements of § 61.56 may instead take the knowledge test for initial certification, and if so must include the knowledge test report with the paper application. Step 3: make an appointment with a certifying official to verify who you are. The chunk lists a local Flight Standards District Office (FSDO), an FAA Designated Pilot Examiner (DPE), an Airman Certification Representative (ACR), or a certificated flight instructor (CFI). You bring the completed Form 8710-13, the online training completion certificate or knowledge test report as applicable, and proof that you meet the flight review requirements of § 61.56 — see that section for what those are. You sign the application after the official examines your photo identification and verifies your identity, then the FAA representative signs it. The identification has to show your photograph, your signature, and your actual residential address if that differs from your mailing address, and the chunk says this information may come from more than one document. Acceptable forms include, but are not limited to, U.S. drivers' licenses, government identification cards, passports, and military identification cards, with more detail in Appendix D. Under the paper or IACRA method, a FSDO representative, a DPE, or an ACR will issue a Temporary Airman Certificate. The note adds that a CFI is not authorized to issue a temporary certificate; in that case the applicant prints their own Temporary Airman Certificate after the FAA emails to say it is available. The FSDO signs and mails the application to the Airmen Certification Branch, which issues the permanent certificate. Flight instructors are pointed to AC 61-141.

Illustration: A private pilot with a current flight review wants to start flying a drone for her employer. She logs into faasafety.gov, completes the online Part 107 small UAS course, and saves the completion certificate. She then opens IACRA and fills out Form 8710-13, uploading the completion certificate. Because the local FSDO appointment calendar is full, she schedules with a CFI at her flying club instead. She brings her driver's license, which shows her photograph, signature, and residential address, plus her logbook endorsement showing she meets § 61.56. The CFI checks her ID, she signs, and the CFI signs. The CFI cannot hand her a temporary certificate, so she waits for the FAA email and then prints her own Temporary Airman Certificate. She chose IACRA over paper because the chunk says processing time increases with a paper application.

Common misreading: Two things get mixed up here. First, people assume any certifying official can hand them a temporary certificate on the spot — the note says a CFI cannot, and in that case the applicant prints their own after an FAA email. Second, the mention of the knowledge test in Option 2 is sometimes read as a second requirement on top of the online course; the chunk presents it as an alternative a part 61 pilot who meets § 61.56 may choose, with the knowledge test report included instead.
