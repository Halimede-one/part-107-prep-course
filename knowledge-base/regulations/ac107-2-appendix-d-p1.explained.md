Appendix D is written mainly for the people who take in a Remote Pilot Certificate application, not for the applicant, but it tells you what happens on the other side of the counter. The appendix itself does not list knowledge requirements; it points you to 14 CFR part 107 subpart C for those, and to 14 CFR 107.61 for eligibility requirements. Who can accept your application: a certificated flight instructor (CFI), a designated pilot examiner (DPE), an FAA aviation safety inspector (ASI) or aviation safety technician (AST), or an Airman Certification Representative (ACR) tied to a 14 CFR part 141 pilot school. However you apply, the certificate itself is issued by the Civil Aviation Registry Division (AFB-700) on high-quality plastic card stock with tamper- and counterfeit-resistant features. The application can be taken either through the FAA's Integrated Airman Certification and Rating Application (IACRA) system, a web-based system at https://iacra.faa.gov/iacra/ that walks the user through the process, or on the paper FAA Form 8710-13, Remote Pilot Certificate and/or Rating Application. IACRA is encouraged. A CFI who wants to accept a remote pilot application must be registered in IACRA under the role 'Recommending Instructor,' and the CFI's signature shows up in the 'Instructor Action' section of Form 8710-13 even though the CFI is acting as a certifying officer. Before processing an application, the certifying official has three jobs: confirm the applicant meets the eligibility requirements of 107.61, confirm the flight review requirements of 14 CFR 61.56 are met, and verify the applicant's identity. For the English language piece, the official follows AC 60-28, FAA English Language Standard for an FAA Certificate Issued Under 14 CFR Parts 61, 63, 65, and 107, and may consult the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) website at http://cfapp.icao.int/rssta/RSSTA.cfm, to assess FAA Aviation English Language Proficiency (AELP). If the applicant does not meet the standard: in IACRA, the official answers the AELP questions and the system handles it, with no extra paperwork mailed anywhere; on paper, the official checks the 'Application Rejected' box in the Submitting Official's Report, states the reason, checks 'No' after the AELP statement, and still forwards the completed application to the responsible Flight Standards office for data entry and processing. Flight Standards office contact information is at https://www.faa.gov/about/office_org/field_offices/fsdo/. One more note in the chunk: if an applicant has a known medical issue that would require a limitation on the certificate, the applicant is referred to the responsible Flight Standards office so the application is processed with the correct limitations.

Illustration: You already hold a part 61 pilot certificate and have a current flight review, so instead of taking the initial aeronautical knowledge test you complete the online training and go to your local CFI to finish the application. The CFI checks IACRA and finds she is not registered as a 'Recommending Instructor,' so she cannot sign your application yet. She registers in that role, then processes your application in IACRA, where she confirms you meet 107.61 eligibility, confirms your 61.56 flight review, and checks your photo identification to verify who you are. Her signature lands in the 'Instructor Action' section. Your plastic certificate later arrives from AFB-700, because that division issues all Remote Pilot Certificates no matter who accepted the application. Had she preferred paper, the same work would have been recorded on FAA Form 8710-13 instead.

Common misreading: People read 'Application Rejected' as 'the paperwork stops here.' It does not. The appendix says to check that box, write the reason, and still forward the complete application to the responsible Flight Standards office for data entry and processing. 'Accept' and 'reject' are simply how AFB-700 is told whether the applicant met the requirements for issuance. The flip side matters too: the 'Application Accepted' box is checked only after the official determines the applicant meets the eligibility requirements, never as a default.
