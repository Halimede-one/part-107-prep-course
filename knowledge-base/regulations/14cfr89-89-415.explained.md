A "means of compliance" is the technical method a designer or producer uses to show that an unmanned aircraft or broadcast module meets the remote identification requirements. This section says the FAA does not accept a means of compliance once and then leave it alone. The Administrator keeps reviewing accepted means of compliance on an ongoing basis. If the Administrator decides a means of compliance does not meet any or all of the requirements of subpart D or subpart E of this part, the Administrator may rescind that acceptance. When acceptance is rescinded, the FAA publishes a notice of rescission in the Federal Register. Paragraph (b) adds a procedural point: part 13, subpart D, of this chapter does not apply to the rescission procedures in paragraph (a). This chunk does not say what part 13, subpart D, contains or what steps replace it — see those sections.

Illustration: You follow drone industry news and see a Federal Register notice that the FAA has rescinded acceptance of a means of compliance that a manufacturer used for one of its broadcast modules. You wonder how you learned about it at all, and the answer is in this section: publication in the Federal Register is the required way the FAA announces a rescission. You also note that the manufacturer cannot point to that means of compliance going forward, because the Administrator determined it did not meet the subpart D or E requirements and withdrew acceptance.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, this section is about the FAA withdrawing acceptance of a technical method, not about revoking a remote pilot certificate or a specific aircraft's airworthiness. Second, this chunk states only that rescission can happen and that notice is published — it says nothing about what happens to aircraft or modules already built or flown under that means of compliance, so do not assume a rescission notice automatically grounds existing equipment. Any such effect would have to come from another section.
