This section says that if your small unmanned aircraft is registered under Part 48, you may not fly it unless it displays a unique identifier, marked the way § 48.205 requires (see that section for how and where the marking must appear). The section then defines what counts as a unique identifier. It can be either the registration number — the number the Registry issues to an individual or to the aircraft when registration is completed — or, if the Administrator authorizes it, the aircraft's serial number as given on the application for a Certificate of Aircraft Registration under § 48.110(a). So the identifier is not free-form text you choose; it is one of those two specific numbers.

Illustration: You register a new quadcopter and the Registry issues you a registration number. The aircraft arrives from the factory with a serial number printed on a sticker under the battery door, so you figure you are covered and head out to fly. You are not covered: nothing is displayed in accordance with § 48.205, and using the serial number instead of the registration number is only an option if the Administrator has authorized it. Before flying, you mark the aircraft with the registration number issued to you, following the display requirements in § 48.205.

Common misreading: People read paragraph (b)(2) as a free choice between the registration number and the serial number. It is not — the serial number option applies only if authorized by the Administrator. Another misreading is treating the factory-printed serial number as already satisfying the rule; the identifier still has to be displayed as § 48.205 requires.
