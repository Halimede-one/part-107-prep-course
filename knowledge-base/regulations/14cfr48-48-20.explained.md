This section says who is allowed to register a small unmanned aircraft (a drone) in the United States under Part 48. Two things must be true. First, the aircraft must not already be registered under the laws of a foreign country. Second, the aircraft must fall into one of four ownership categories: owned by a U.S. citizen; owned by an individual who is a citizen of a foreign country but has been lawfully admitted for permanent residence in the United States; owned by a corporation that is not a U.S. citizen, if that corporation is organized and doing business under the laws of the United States or of a State, and the aircraft is based and primarily used in the United States; or the aircraft belongs to the United States Government, or to a State, the District of Columbia, a territory or possession of the United States, or a political subdivision of a State, territory, or possession. The registration authority cited here is 49 U.S.C. 44103 together with this part. Note that this section covers eligibility only, not the process or the cost of registering.

Illustration: A German engineering firm opens a subsidiary that is incorporated in Delaware and operates out of an office in Ohio. The subsidiary buys a small drone for inspecting its own facilities, and the drone stays in Ohio. The parent company is not a U.S. citizen, but paragraph (c) still allows registration: the owning corporation is organized and doing business under the laws of a State, and the aircraft is based and primarily used in the United States. The firm also confirms the drone carries no foreign registration. Both conditions met, so it is eligible. If instead the subsidiary shipped that same drone to Germany to be flown there most of the year, the "based and primarily used in the United States" condition would fail.

Common misreading: People read the permanent-resident line in paragraph (b) as covering any lawful presence in the United States. It does not say that. It specifies a citizen of a foreign country lawfully admitted for permanent residence — not someone here on a temporary visa. The other common slip is treating the foreign-registration bar as optional: an aircraft already registered under another country's laws cannot be registered under this part, no matter which ownership category it fits.
