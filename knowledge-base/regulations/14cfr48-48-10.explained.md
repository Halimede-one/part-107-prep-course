This section is the vocabulary list for Part 48, the rule that covers registering and marking small unmanned aircraft. It does not require you to do anything; it just fixes the meaning of three terms used elsewhere in the part.

"Citizen of the United States" (or "U.S. citizen") covers three kinds of people or entities. First, an individual who is a citizen of the United States or one of its possessions. Second, a partnership in which every partner is an individual who is a U.S. citizen. Third, a corporation or association organized under the laws of the United States, a State, the District of Columbia, or a U.S. territory or possession, where the president and at least two-thirds of the board of directors and other managing officers are U.S. citizens, the entity is under the actual control of U.S. citizens, and at least 75 percent of the voting interest is owned or controlled by U.S. citizens.

"Registry" is shorthand for a specific FAA office: the FAA, Civil Aviation Registry, Aircraft Registration Branch. When another section in Part 48 tells you to send something to the Registry, that is the office it means.

"Resident alien" means an individual who is a citizen of a foreign country and has been lawfully admitted for permanent residence in the United States as an immigrant, in conformity with the Department of Homeland Security regulations in 8 CFR Chapter 1.

Illustration: Three friends form a partnership to fly real-estate photography missions and want to register the drone in the partnership's name. Two partners are U.S. citizens; the third holds a green card as a lawful permanent resident. Under the definition here, the partnership does not meet the "citizen of the United States" test, because that test requires each partner to be an individual who is a citizen of the United States. The third partner is a resident alien under this section, which is a separate defined term, not a sub-case of U.S. citizen. Whether and how a resident alien or a non-qualifying partnership may register is handled in the eligibility sections of Part 48, so see those sections.

Common misreading: People read "resident alien" as a flavor of U.S. citizen because both appear in the same definition list. They are two distinct terms here, and a lawful permanent resident is not a U.S. citizen for Part 48 purposes. The other common slip is treating the corporate test as satisfied by a single element, for example a U.S. place of incorporation alone. All of the listed conditions apply together: U.S. organization, president and at least two-thirds of the board and other managing officers being U.S. citizens, actual control by U.S. citizens, and at least 75 percent of the voting interest owned or controlled by U.S. citizens.
