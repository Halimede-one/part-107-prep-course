This is the opening, scope-setting line for the subpart that covers flying a small unmanned aircraft over people or over moving vehicles. It tells you two things. First, what the subpart contains: the eligibility requirements (what the aircraft and the operator must qualify for) and the operating requirements (how the flight must be conducted) for civil small unmanned aircraft operating over human beings or over moving vehicles in the United States. Second, that these requirements come in addition to the operations already permitted by § 107.39(a) and (b). In other words, this subpart is not the only path to flying over people — § 107.39(a) and (b) permit certain operations on their own, and this subpart adds further ways to qualify. The chunk does not state what those paragraphs of § 107.39 allow, so see that section for their content. Note the word 'civil': this applies to civil small unmanned aircraft, not public aircraft operations.

Illustration: A remote pilot is asked to shoot video of a charity 10K race, flying along a street where runners and a moving camera truck will be below the aircraft. Both conditions in this applicability line are in play — flight over human beings and over a moving vehicle — so the pilot knows the subpart that follows is the body of rules to work through, and must check its eligibility requirements for the aircraft and the operating requirements for the flight. Before doing that, the pilot also checks whether the job already fits an operation permitted by § 107.39(a) or (b), since this line says those are a separate, existing permission rather than something this subpart replaces.

Common misreading: Reading this as a grant of permission. It is not. An applicability section only marks the boundary of what the subpart covers; the actual conditions for flying over people or moving vehicles are in the sections that follow. The other common slip is treating this subpart as the sole route to flying over people and overlooking that § 107.39(a) and (b) permit operations on their own, with this subpart adding to them.
