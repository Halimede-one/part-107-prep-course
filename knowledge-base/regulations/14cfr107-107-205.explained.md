This section is the list of Part 107 rules that the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) can let you deviate from through a certificate of waiver issued under § 107.200. If a rule is not on this list, it cannot be waived. The waivable rules are: § 107.25 operation from a moving vehicle or aircraft; § 107.29(a)(2) and (b), the anti-collision light required for operations at night and during periods of civil twilight; § 107.31 visual line of sight aircraft operation; § 107.33 visual observer; § 107.35 operation of multiple small unmanned aircraft systems; § 107.37(a) yielding the right of way; § 107.39 operation over people; § 107.41 operation in certain airspace; § 107.51 operating limitations for small unmanned aircraft; and § 107.145 operations over moving vehicles. Two of these carry a built-in limit. For § 107.25 and for § 107.31, no waiver will be issued to allow the carriage of property of another by aircraft for compensation or hire. So a waiver cannot be the route to a package delivery operation flown from a moving vehicle or flown beyond visual line of sight. For what each listed section actually requires, see that section.

Illustration: A remote pilot wants to fly a bridge inspection where the aircraft goes out of sight behind the structure, so she applies for a waiver of § 107.31, visual line of sight aircraft operation. That section is on the list in paragraph (c), so a waiver is possible, and because she is inspecting a bridge rather than hauling someone else's property for hire, the carriage-for-compensation bar does not apply to her request. A second pilot at the same firm wants a § 107.31 waiver to fly customer parcels beyond visual line of sight for a fee. That request will not be granted, because paragraph (c) says no waiver of § 107.31 will be issued to allow the carriage of property of another by aircraft for compensation or hire.

Common misreading: People read this list as a promise that the FAA will approve a waiver for anything on it. It is only a list of what is eligible to be considered. Appearing here means a deviation may be authorized, not that it will be. The other common slip is assuming any Part 107 rule can be waived; rules not named in this section are not subject to waiver at all.
