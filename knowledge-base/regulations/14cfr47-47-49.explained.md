This section covers what to do when your aircraft registration certificate goes missing or gets damaged. If the original Certificate of Aircraft Registration is lost, stolen, or mutilated, the registered owner may send the FAA Aircraft Registry a written request. That request has to state the reason a replacement is needed, and it has to include the fee required by § 47.17 (see that section for the amount). The Registry then sends the replacement to the registered owner by email or other electronic or digital means. Only if those means are unavailable does the Registry use a mailing address — either the registered owner's mailing address, or a different mailing address if the owner asks for that in writing. Part (b) covers the gap while you wait. The registered owner may ask for a temporary Certificate of Aircraft Registration pending receipt of the replacement. When the Registry issues that temporary certificate, it must be carried in the aircraft until the replacement certificate arrives.

Illustration: A remote pilot registers a 12-pound drone under Part 47 for commercial survey work. After a job, the printed registration certificate is left in a jacket that goes through the wash and comes out unreadable — mutilated. The owner sends the Registry a written request explaining that the certificate was mutilated, along with the § 47.17 fee, and also asks for a temporary Certificate of Aircraft Registration so flights can continue. The Registry issues the temporary certificate, which the owner carries with the aircraft on every flight until the replacement certificate shows up by email. The reason: the rule lets the owner bridge the waiting period with a temporary certificate, but that temporary certificate must be carried in the aircraft until the replacement is received.

Common misreading: People read the temporary certificate as automatic. It is not — the owner has to request it. And the carriage requirement is not optional: once a temporary certificate is issued, it must be carried in the aircraft until the replacement arrives, not just kept on file at the office.
