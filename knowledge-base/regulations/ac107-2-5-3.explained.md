This part of the advisory circular describes two mental skills, not a numbered rule. Aeronautical Decision-Making (ADM) is a systematic approach to the thinking a pilot uses to consistently settle on the best course of action for whatever circumstances come up. Crew Resource Management (CRM) is described as a component of ADM: the remote pilot in command (PIC) making effective use of all available resources, which the chunk groups as human resources, hardware, and information. The chunk points out that many remote pilots operating under part 107 work with other people, such as a visual observer (VO), someone else manipulating the controls of the small unmanned aircraft system (small UAS), or anyone else the remote PIC interacts with to keep the operation safe. Because of that, the remote PIC has to be able to work in a team and get the most out of the team. The chunk lists what that skill set includes: situational awareness, giving the right tasks to the right people, keeping the workload from overwhelming himself or herself and others, and communicating effectively with crew members such as VOs and people manipulating the controls. For more depth on ADM and CRM, plus sample risk assessment tools for spotting hazards and reducing risk, the chunk directs you to Appendix A, Risk Assessment Tools — see that appendix.

Illustration: You are remote PIC for a roof inspection with one VO and a second person who will take the controls for part of the flight. You find yourself trying to watch the aircraft, read the camera feed, talk to the building owner, and listen for the VO's calls all at once. Applying CRM, you hand the camera feed to the person on the controls, tell the VO to call out any manned aircraft and nothing else, and ask the building owner to wait until the flight is done. The reason: the chunk says the skill set includes proper allocation of tasks and avoiding work overloads for yourself and others, so moving tasks off your own plate and making each person's job explicit is exactly the behavior described.

Common misreading: People read ADM and CRM as a checklist they can be cited for skipping, or assume CRM only applies when a crew is present. This chunk is guidance describing a mental approach and a skill set, and it notes that CRM is a component of ADM about using all available resources — human resources, hardware, and information — so a solo remote PIC still has resources to manage.
