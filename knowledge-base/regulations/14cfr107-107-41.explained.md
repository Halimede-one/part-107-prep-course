This section says you need permission before you fly a small unmanned aircraft in controlled airspace near airports. Specifically, you must have prior authorization from Air Traffic Control (ATC) to operate in Class B, Class C, or Class D airspace, or inside the lateral boundaries of the surface area of Class E airspace that is designated for an airport. "Prior" means the authorization has to be in hand before the flight begins, not requested while you are airborne. "Lateral boundaries of the surface area" means the footprint of that airspace where it touches the ground, as drawn on the chart. The rule applies to any person operating the aircraft, and it does not list exceptions in this chunk.

Illustration: You are hired to photograph a car dealership. You check the chart and find the lot sits inside the Class D surface area of a nearby airport. Even though you only plan to fly at 50 feet above the parking lot and never near the runway, you cannot launch until you have ATC authorization for that location. You obtain the authorization first, then fly. If the request were still pending on the morning of the shoot, the correct decision would be to postpone, because this section requires the authorization before the operation, not during it.

Common misreading: Two things trip people up. First, people assume flying very low keeps them out of controlled airspace. Class B, C, and D airspace, and the designated surface area of Class E, extend down to the ground inside their lateral boundaries, so a 50-foot flight is still inside them. Second, people treat Class E as always requiring authorization. This section only names the surface area of Class E airspace designated for an airport, not every piece of Class E airspace.
