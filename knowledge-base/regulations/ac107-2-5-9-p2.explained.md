This part of the advisory circular deals with keeping the aircraft in visual line of sight (VLOS) when flying at night. Before a night flight, the remote pilot in command (remote PIC) should make sure he or she can keep the small unmanned aircraft inside the intended area of operation and within VLOS for the whole flight. The FAA says that in almost all night cases, the remote PIC may need to shrink the area the aircraft is allowed to operate in. The reason is that less light and less contrast at night make it harder to meet what § 107.31(a) requires — keeping the ability to visually tell the aircraft's location, attitude, altitude, and direction of flight. See § 107.31 for the rule text itself. The key limit in this chunk: a remote pilot cannot rely only on the aircraft's anti-collision lighting, only on Ground Control Station (GCS) telemetry data displays, or on the two together to satisfy § 107.31. Those tools can help, but they are not a substitute for seeing the aircraft.

Illustration: A remote PIC plans an evening roof inspection and normally flies that site out to 1,200 feet from the launch point in daylight. After sunset, standing at the same spot, he finds that past roughly 500 feet he can see the blinking anti-collision light but can no longer tell which way the aircraft is pointed or whether it is climbing or descending. He tightens the flight to a smaller box close in, where he can still make out the airframe's attitude and direction, and finishes the inspection in two passes instead of one. Reason: the strobe alone plus the altitude readout on his controller screen does not meet § 107.31 — he has to be able to visually discern location, attitude, altitude, and direction of flight.

Common misreading: People read the anti-collision lighting requirement for night flight as the thing that makes night VLOS work, and conclude that if the lights are visible, VLOS is satisfied. This chunk says the opposite: visible strobes, telemetry displays, or both combined are not enough on their own for § 107.31 compliance.
