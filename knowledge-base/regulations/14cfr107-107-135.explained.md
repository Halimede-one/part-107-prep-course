Small unmanned aircraft that qualify for Category 2 or Category 3 operations over human beings carry a label saying which category or categories they are qualified for. This section covers what happens when that label goes bad. If the Category 2 or Category 3 label on the aircraft is damaged, destroyed, or missing, the remote pilot in command (the person responsible for the flight) must replace it before conducting operations over human beings. The replacement label must be in English, legible, prominent, and it must stay attached to the aircraft for the whole operation. It must also correctly state the category or categories of operation over human beings that the aircraft is qualified to conduct under this subpart. In short: the duty to re-label falls on the remote pilot in command, not only on the manufacturer, and it has to be done before the flight over people, not after.

Illustration: You are the remote pilot in command for a shoot over a crowd at a street festival. Your aircraft is qualified for Category 2 operations. During pre-flight you notice the Category 2 label on the underside has peeled up and torn, and the text is no longer readable. You cannot fly over the people in that condition. You make a new label in English stating Category 2, write it large enough to read easily, place it where it is prominent, and attach it so it will not peel off during the flight. Now you can conduct the operation over human beings. The reason: the rule requires a legible, prominent label that correctly identifies the qualified category and survives the duration of the operation, and as remote pilot in command the replacement is your job.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, people read this as permission to invent a category. The replacement label must correctly identify what the aircraft is actually qualified for under this subpart, so you cannot write Category 2 on an aircraft that is not qualified for Category 2. Second, people think a missing label only matters for the label's own sake, so a quick sticker after takeoff is fine. The text says the labeling must happen before conducting operations over human beings.
