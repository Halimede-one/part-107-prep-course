This is the tail end of § 47.15, so it starts mid-list. Items (i) through (iii) are the final conditions in a longer subsection that begins earlier in the section (see the first part of § 47.15 for the lead-in language). What they describe are time triggers after which an assignment of a registration number ends because paperwork was never completed:

- (i) Six months after the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) first receives notice of an aircraft sale, or evidence of ownership, from the last registered owner or from successive owners, if no Aircraft Registration Application has come in.
- (ii) Six months after evidence of ownership authorized under § 47.67 has been submitted, if the applicant has not met this part's requirements. See § 47.67 for what that evidence is.
- (iii) Twelve months after a new owner has submitted evidence of ownership and an Aircraft Registration Application under § 47.31, if that applicant or any later applicant has not met this part's requirements. See § 47.31 for those filing requirements.

Paragraph (j) covers what happens to the number itself once an assignment is cancelled. At the time of cancellation, the number may be reserved for one year in the name of the last owner of record, but only if a request was submitted along with the fee required by § 47.17. If the reservation request and the fee are not submitted before cancellation happens, the number cannot be assigned to anyone for five years.

The bracketed material at the end is the amendment history for the section, not a rule.

Note on scope: Part 47 is the aircraft registration part that governs registration numbers generally. Small unmanned aircraft flown under Part 107 are normally registered under Part 48, so this chunk speaks to the Part 47 registration process rather than to day-to-day Part 107 flight operations.

Illustration: An operator buys an aircraft and the seller, who is the last registered owner, sends the FAA notice of the sale. The buyer gets busy and never files an Aircraft Registration Application. Six months after the FAA first received that notice of sale, condition (i) is met and the number assignment can be cancelled. If the buyer wants to hold onto that particular number, paragraph (j) says the request to reserve it for one year, with the § 47.17 fee, has to be in before the cancellation occurs. Filing a week after cancellation is too late, and under (j) the number is then off the table for assignment for five years.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, people read the one-year reservation in (j) as automatic for the last owner of record; it is not, it depends on a request plus the § 47.17 fee being submitted prior to cancellation. Second, people assume the five-year unavailability is a penalty on the former owner only. As written, the number is simply unavailable for assignment for five years, so no one gets it in that window.
