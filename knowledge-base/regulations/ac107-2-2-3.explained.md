This is a reference list, not a rule. Section 2.3 of Advisory Circular (AC) 107-2A simply points small unmanned aircraft system (small UAS) operators toward other FAA material that supports safe operation in the National Airspace System (NAS), and notes that an operator may want to seek out additional publications beyond these lists. Nothing here imposes a requirement. The list is grouped four ways: FAA advisory circulars and orders (weather, English language standards, safety management systems, air traffic and airspace policy orders, airworthiness certification, and FAA Order 8900.1 Volume 16 on UAS); online and mobile sources (the FAA UAS website, the registration site at faadronezone.faa.gov, the B4UFLY mobile app, flight planning and Notice to Airmen (NOTAM) and temporary flight restriction (TFR) sites, FAA Safety, the UAS Data Delivery System, waiver safety explanation guidelines, the Integrated Airman Certification and Rating Application (IACRA), the Airman Certificate Testing Service (ACTS), and Flight Standards District Offices (FSDO)); handbooks and manuals (the Aeronautical Information Manual (AIM), digital aeronautical charts, the Pilot/Controller Glossary, the Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge, the Risk Management Handbook, the Remote Pilot – Small Unmanned Aircraft Systems Airman Certification Standards (ACS), and the Unmanned Aircraft Systems Operating Handbook); and three statutory resources, Public Law (PL) 112-95 of Feb. 14, 2012, PL 114-190 of July 15, 2016, and PL 115-254 of Oct. 5, 2018. Note that several web addresses in this chunk are truncated or missing, and the chunk tells you to use current editions, so look each item up on the FAA site rather than relying on the text here. The chunk ends at the heading for Chapter 3, Background, with no content from that chapter.

Illustration: A remote pilot preparing for the knowledge test wants to know exactly what subject areas the exam can cover. Rather than guessing, she uses this list to pull the Remote Pilot – Small Unmanned Aircraft Systems Airman Certification Standards from the FAA site, and the two weather ACs (AC 00-6, Aviation Weather, and AC 00-45, Aviation Weather Services) to study the weather topics. On the morning of a flight she checks the TFR and NOTAM sources listed here before launching. She treats all of it as study and planning material, not as rules she is being tested on obeying.

Common misreading: People sometimes read a list like this as a set of documents they are legally required to own, read, or carry. It is not. These are supporting references the AC recommends; the binding requirements live in 14 CFR part 107 itself. The reverse mistake also happens: assuming the edition or URL printed here is the one to use. The chunk specifically says current editions, and some addresses in this text are cut off, so always go to the FAA website for the live version.
