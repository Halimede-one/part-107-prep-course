This section covers drones registered in another country that are flown in United States airspace. Before any such flight, the person operating the foreign registered civil unmanned aircraft with remote identification must file a notice of identification with the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), in a form and manner the Administrator accepts. The notice has to include eight items: the operator's name (and the authorized representative's name, if there is one); the physical address of the operator and representative, plus a mailing address if they don't receive mail at the physical address; the telephone number(s) where they can be reached while in the United States; their email address(es); the unmanned aircraft manufacturer and model name; the serial number of the unmanned aircraft or of the remote identification broadcast module; the country of registration; and the registration number. Once the notification is complete, the FAA issues a Confirmation of Identification. That filing and that Confirmation are not United States aircraft registration and do not act as one. To fly, the operator must have obtained the Confirmation, must keep it at the unmanned aircraft's control station, and must produce it when the FAA or a law enforcement officer asks. The holder also has to keep the submitted information accurate and update it before operating a foreign registered drone in the United States. Subpart C, on FAA-Recognized Identification Areas, begins after this section; the source note shown is the amendment citation for the subpart.

Illustration: A film crew from Canada brings a Canadian-registered drone with remote identification to shoot a commercial in Arizona. Before the first flight, the crew's operator submits the notice of identification to the FAA with her name and her United States representative's name, both physical addresses, the cell number she will carry in the States, both email addresses, the drone's manufacturer and model, its serial number, Canada as the country of registration, and the Canadian registration number. The FAA issues a Confirmation of Identification. She saves and prints it and keeps it at the control station. Midway through the shoot a sheriff's deputy stops by and asks for her paperwork; she hands over the Confirmation, which is exactly what paragraph (c) requires her to produce. When the crew switches hotels and her phone number changes, she updates the information before the next flight, because paragraph (d) requires the details to stay current.

Common misreading: People read the Confirmation of Identification as a United States registration for the drone. Paragraph (b)(2) says plainly that neither the notification nor the Confirmation has the effect of United States aircraft registration. It is a notice-and-confirmation process, not a registration, and the aircraft stays registered in its own country.
