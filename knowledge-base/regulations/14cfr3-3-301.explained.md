This section does only one thing: it says who the subpart covers. It does not state any requirement itself — the actual duties appear in the other sections of that subpart, so see those sections.

The subpart applies to individuals who meet all three listed conditions, because the list is joined by "and." Those conditions are: (a) the person does not have a U.S. physical address of record on file with the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA); (b) the person does have a foreign address of record on file with the FAA; and (c) the person holds, or is applying for, a certificate, rating, or authorization under part 47, 61, 63, 65, 67, or 107 of this chapter. Part 107 is the small unmanned aircraft rule, so remote pilot certificate holders and applicants are inside that list of parts — but only if the address conditions in (a) and (b) are also true for them.

If you hold a U.S. physical address of record with the FAA, this subpart does not reach you, no matter which of those parts your certificate comes from.

Illustration: A person living full-time in Lisbon, Portugal applies for a remote pilot certificate under part 107. The only address the FAA has on file for them is their Lisbon address; they have no U.S. physical address of record. Check the three conditions: (a) no U.S. physical address of record — true; (b) a foreign address of record on file — true; (c) applying for an authorization under part 107 — true. All three are met, so this subpart applies to that applicant, and they need to read the rest of the subpart to see what it requires of them. Contrast a remote pilot in Ohio who flies a two-week job in Portugal. Their address of record with the FAA is still their Ohio home, so condition (a) fails and the subpart does not apply to them.

Common misreading: The most common error is treating the three items as alternatives — reading it as "any one of these makes the subpart apply." The list ends with "and," so all three must be true at once. A related error is assuming that merely flying overseas, or merely having a foreign mailing address somewhere, is enough. What matters is the address of record on file with the FAA: no U.S. physical one, and a foreign one present. A second error is reading this section as if it imposed a duty. It does not; it only sets the scope of who the rest of the subpart speaks to.
