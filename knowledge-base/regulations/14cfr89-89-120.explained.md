Part 89 normally requires drones to broadcast remote identification (remote ID) — electronic identifying information about the aircraft and its location. This section creates a narrow path around that requirement. The Administrator of the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) may authorize an operation to fly without remote identification, but only when the operation exists solely for one of two purposes: aeronautical research, or showing compliance with regulations. Two words carry the weight here. 'May authorize' means the FAA decides; you have to ask and receive permission, and the FAA is not obligated to grant it. 'Solely' means that research or compliance demonstration has to be the whole purpose of the flight, not one purpose among several. The chunk does not describe how to apply, what the application must contain, or how long an authorization lasts — it only establishes that the Administrator has this authority.

Illustration: A company is developing a new multirotor airframe and needs to fly a prototype that has no remote ID module installed yet, because the airframe's internal layout is still changing and the radio placement is part of what they're testing. The flights are purely to gather flight-performance data on the prototype. The company requests an authorization from the FAA under this section on the grounds that the operation is solely for aeronautical research. If the FAA grants it, those test flights may proceed without remote identification. If the same company wanted to use those prototype flights to also shoot promotional video for its website, the operation would no longer be solely for research, and this section would not fit.

Common misreading: People read this as a self-declared exemption: 'my flight is research, so remote ID doesn't apply to me.' It doesn't work that way. Nothing in this section lets you decide on your own that your purpose qualifies. The Administrator has to authorize the operation first. Calling a flight 'research' in your own notes changes nothing until you have that authorization in hand.
