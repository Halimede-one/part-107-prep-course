This is a short, single-sentence prohibition. Automatic Dependent Surveillance-Broadcast (ADS-B) Out is equipment that broadcasts an aircraft's identity and position to air traffic control and nearby aircraft. The rule says that equipment cannot be used to satisfy the remote identification requirements of this part. In other words, if you need to meet remote ID, installing or using ADS-B Out does not count toward it. The chunk does not list what does satisfy remote ID — that is covered elsewhere in Part 89, so see those sections.

Illustration: A remote pilot builds a drone and finds an ADS-B Out transmitter module that fits the airframe. He reasons that since ADS-B Out broadcasts his aircraft's identity and position continuously, it should more than cover the remote identification rule. Under this section, it does not. ADS-B Out equipment cannot be used to comply with the remote identification requirements of this part, so he still has to meet remote ID by one of the means Part 89 actually allows, and he should look to the rest of Part 89 to find which one applies to him.

Common misreading: Some read this as a ban on carrying or using ADS-B Out on a drone at all. The sentence is narrower than that: it says ADS-B Out cannot be used as a way of complying with remote identification. Whether ADS-B Out may be installed or operated on an unmanned aircraft is a separate question that this chunk does not address.
