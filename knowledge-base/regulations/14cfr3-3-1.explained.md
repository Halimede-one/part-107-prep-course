This is the applicability section for a subpart of 14 CFR Part 3 — the part that deals with records and statements about aviation products. Paragraph (a) sets the reach: the subpart covers any person who makes a record about a type-certificated product, or about a product, part, appliance, or material that may be used on a type-certificated product. The trigger is not your job title or certificate; it is the act of making a record, plus the record's subject matter being tied to a type-certificated product. Paragraph (b) carves out one narrow exception: § 3.5(b) does not apply to records made under part 43 of this chapter. That is a carve-out from one paragraph only — the rest of the subpart still reaches those records. This chunk does not tell you what § 3.5(b) requires or what part 43 covers, so see those sections for that. The bracketed line at the end is amendment history, not a rule.

Illustration: A remote pilot also helps out in a maintenance shop and writes a log entry describing a bracket the shop fabricated, noting what aircraft it can be installed on. Because the entry is a record about a part that may be used on a type-certificated product, this subpart applies to the pilot as the person who made that record. The pilot writes the entry carefully and accurately, because the subpart's requirements on records now attach to it. If the same pilot instead jots a note about an item with no connection to any type-certificated product, paragraph (a) does not pull that note into this subpart.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, people read paragraph (b) as meaning part 43 records are exempt from the whole subpart. It says only that § 3.5(b) does not apply to them. Second, people assume this subpart covers every record they ever write about an aircraft. Paragraph (a) limits it to records regarding a type-certificated product, or a product, part, appliance, or material that may be used on one.
