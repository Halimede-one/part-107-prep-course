This section covers foreign civil aircraft. When a person is required to comply with Part 48 because of an authorization to operate issued under part 375 of this title (see that part), the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) issues them a "recognition of ownership" rather than a U.S. aircraft registration. The one exception is corporations eligible to register under § 48.20(c) — see that section for who those are. The chunk is explicit about the limit of the document: a recognition of ownership does not have the effect of U.S. aircraft registration. It records who owns the aircraft; it does not make the aircraft a U.S.-registered one. The chunk ends with the heading "Subpart C—Aircraft Marking" and no text under it, so the marking rules themselves are not in this chunk.

Illustration: A survey firm based outside the United States holds an authorization to operate issued under part 375 and brings its small unmanned aircraft to the U.S. for a job. Because that authorization triggers the Part 48 compliance requirement, the firm goes through the Part 48 process and the FAA issues it a recognition of ownership. The firm should not then describe the aircraft as U.S.-registered on paperwork or to a client, because this section says the recognition of ownership does not have that effect. If the firm were instead a corporation eligible to register under § 48.20(c), this section would not apply to it — that is the stated exception.

Common misreading: People read "recognition of ownership" as just another name for a registration certificate. It is not. The chunk says plainly that it does not have the effect of U.S. aircraft registration, so anything that depends on the aircraft being U.S.-registered is not satisfied by holding one.
