This rule bars operating a civil aircraft inside the United States when you know that controlled substances are on board. The substances it names are narcotic drugs, marihuana, and depressant or stimulant drugs or substances, as those terms are defined in Federal or State statutes. The trigger is knowledge: the rule applies to a person who operates the aircraft knowing the substances are being carried in it. Part 107 incorporates this section, so a small unmanned aircraft is covered the same way a crewed aircraft is. Paragraph (b) carves out an exception: the prohibition does not apply to carriage that is authorized by or under any Federal or State statute, or by any Federal or State agency.

Illustration: A remote pilot is hired to fly a package delivery demonstration across a business park. While loading, the client mentions the test parcel contains a small amount of marijuana. The remote pilot asks whether the shipment is authorized under a state program or by a state agency. The client says no, it is just a personal item. The pilot declines the flight, because operating the aircraft knowing that marihuana is carried in it is prohibited, and no paragraph (b) authorization exists here. On a different job, the same pilot flies a sample transport between two clinics under a state agency authorization. That flight is permissible, because paragraph (b) excludes carriage authorized by a state agency.

Common misreading: People read this as a rule about the pilot being impaired. It is not. Impairment and alcohol or drug use by the crew are addressed elsewhere, including § 91.17. This section is about cargo: what is carried in the aircraft, and whether you know it is there. A sober pilot who knowingly hauls an unauthorized controlled substance violates this section.
