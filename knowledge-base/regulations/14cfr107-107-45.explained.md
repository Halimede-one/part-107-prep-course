Prohibited areas and restricted areas are types of special use airspace. This section says you may not fly a small unmanned aircraft in either one unless you have permission from the using agency or the controlling agency, whichever applies. The rule is a flat bar with one exit: permission from the right agency in advance. Note that the permission comes from the agency that uses or controls the area, not from the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) generally and not from air traffic control as a matter of course. Which agency to ask depends on the area, and the chunk does not list the agencies or tell you how to find them.

Illustration: A remote pilot is hired to photograph a construction site. Checking the chart, she sees the site sits inside a restricted area. She does not launch. Instead she identifies the using agency for that restricted area and requests permission before the flight date. Permission from that agency is the only thing that makes the flight lawful under this section, so without it the job does not happen, no matter how low or how brief the flight would be.

Common misreading: People assume that getting an airspace authorization for controlled airspace, or flying very low, or flying when the restricted area is "cold" and not in use, satisfies this section. The chunk offers only one exception: permission from the using or controlling agency, as appropriate.
