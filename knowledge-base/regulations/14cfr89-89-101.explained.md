This section says who the remote identification subpart covers. It applies to two groups. First, people flying unmanned aircraft that are registered, or that are required to be registered, under part 47 or part 48 of this chapter. Second, people flying foreign civil unmanned aircraft in the United States. There is one carve-out in paragraph (b): the subpart does not apply to unmanned aircraft operations under part 91 of this chapter that are transmitting ADS-B Out (Automatic Dependent Surveillance-Broadcast Out, a system that broadcasts an aircraft's position to air traffic control and nearby aircraft) in accordance with § 91.225. For the details of registration under parts 47 and 48, or the ADS-B Out requirements of § 91.225, see those sections.

Illustration: You bought a quadcopter for aerial photography work and registered it under part 48. Because your aircraft is registered under part 48, the remote identification rules in this subpart apply to your flights. A friend asks whether the rules skip small drones. They don't — what matters here is whether the aircraft is registered or required to be registered, not how small it is. The part 91 exception doesn't help you either, since your flights aren't part 91 operations with ADS-B Out installed.

Common misreading: People read paragraph (b) as a general escape hatch — "just put ADS-B Out on my drone and remote ID doesn't apply." It is narrower than that. The exception only covers unmanned aircraft operations conducted under part 91 that are transmitting ADS-B Out pursuant to § 91.225. A typical small drone flight is not a part 91 operation, so bolting on a transmitter does not move you into paragraph (b).
