This chunk defines a term and states one prerequisite. Anyone who exercises the authority of pilot in command (PIC) under part 107 is called the "remote PIC." Before a person can act in that role, that person must hold a Remote Pilot Certificate with a small unmanned aircraft system (small UAS) rating. The chunk cites § 107.12 as the rule that imposes this requirement — see that section for its details. Note the ordering the chunk uses: the certificate must be obtained prior to acting as remote PIC, not afterward.

Illustration: A real estate agent buys a drone and plans to shoot listing photos for her brokerage. She has studied the material and scheduled her knowledge test for next week, but a client wants photos this weekend. Because the chunk says the certificate must be obtained prior to acting as remote PIC, she cannot be the remote PIC on Saturday. Her options are to postpone the shoot until she holds the Remote Pilot Certificate with a small UAS rating, or to have someone who already holds that certificate act as remote PIC for the flight.

Common misreading: People read "remote PIC" as a label for whoever happens to be holding the controller. The chunk ties the term to exercising the authority of pilot in command under part 107 — which is a role, not a grip on the control sticks. This chunk does not say anything about who may manipulate the controls or about anyone other than the remote PIC; it only states the certificate requirement for the person acting in that role.
