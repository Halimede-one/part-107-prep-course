Holding a remote pilot certificate is not enough on its own. To actually act as remote pilot in command (the person responsible for the flight), you must have done one of three things within the previous 24 calendar months, in a manner acceptable to the Administrator:

(a) passed an initial aeronautical knowledge test covering the areas of knowledge in § 107.73;
(b) completed recurrent training covering the areas of knowledge in § 107.73; or
(c) if you hold a pilot certificate other than a student pilot certificate issued under part 61, and you meet the flight review requirements of § 61.56, completed training covering the areas of knowledge in § 107.74.

The chunk points to §§ 107.73, 107.74, and 61.56 for what those areas of knowledge and flight review requirements contain — see those sections.

Paragraph (d) is a grandfather clause. If, before April 6, 2021, you passed a recurrent aeronautical knowledge test in a manner acceptable to the Administrator, or satisfied the training requirement in paragraph (c), and that happened within the previous 24 calendar months, you are treated as complying with paragraph (b) or (c), whichever applies to you.

Note the phrase "24 calendar months." That counts whole months, not a date-to-date anniversary.

Illustration: A remote pilot passed the initial knowledge test and has flown commercially since. She is asked to fly a roof inspection and checks her records: her last completed recurrent training covering the § 107.73 areas of knowledge was more than 24 calendar months ago, and she holds no part 61 pilot certificate. She declines to act as remote pilot in command until she completes recurrent training, because § 107.65 bars her from exercising those privileges without one of the three listed items inside the previous 24 calendar months. She can still fly as a crew member under someone else's remote PIC, or complete the recurrent training first and then fly the job herself.

Common misreading: Two trip people up. First, people assume the certificate itself expires. The certificate is not what lapses here — what lapses is your ability to exercise the privileges, and you restore it by completing one of the items in (a), (b), or (c). Second, people read paragraph (c) as a shortcut available to any part 61 certificate holder. It is not: the section requires both that the certificate is not a student pilot certificate and that you meet the § 61.56 flight review requirements, and you still have to complete training covering the § 107.74 areas of knowledge.
