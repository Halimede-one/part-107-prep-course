This section lists the subject areas that the small unmanned aircraft system (sUAS) training must cover for one specific group of people: pilots who already hold a pilot certificate issued under part 61 of this chapter, other than a student pilot certificate, and who meet the flight review requirements of § 61.56 (see that section for what those requirements are). For that group, the training covers eight areas of knowledge: (a) the regulations that apply to small unmanned aircraft system rating privileges, limitations, and flight operation; (b) how weather affects small unmanned aircraft performance; (c) small unmanned aircraft loading; (d) emergency procedures; (e) crew resource management; (f) determining the performance of the small unmanned aircraft; (g) maintenance and preflight inspection procedures; and (h) operation at night. The chunk is a content outline. It tells you what the training must address, not how long it takes, where you take it, or how often. The bracketed line at the end is the amendment history for the section.

Illustration: A private pilot holds a part 61 certificate and is current under § 61.56. She wants to start flying a drone for a roofing inspection business, so she looks up what the drone training will ask her to learn. This section tells her the answer: she should expect material on the small unmanned aircraft system rules and their limits, weather effects on small unmanned aircraft performance, loading, emergency procedures, crew resource management, performance determination, maintenance and preflight inspection, and operation at night. When she sees a module on crew resource management, she knows it belongs there even though her manned flying already covered the topic, because paragraph (e) names it as part of this training.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, this list is the training content for part 61 certificate holders who meet the § 61.56 flight review requirements, not a general syllabus for everyone seeking remote pilot privileges. Someone without a part 61 certificate should not assume these eight items are their path. Second, the fact that 'operation at night' appears in paragraph (h) is a statement about what the training covers, not a grant of night operating privileges or a description of the night rules themselves. Those live elsewhere in the part.
