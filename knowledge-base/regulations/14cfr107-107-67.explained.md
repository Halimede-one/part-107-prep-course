This section covers the mechanics of taking the aeronautical knowledge test for a remote pilot certificate. Three points. First, the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) Administrator decides who gives the tests and how they are given — you cannot arrange your own test on your own terms. Second, when you show up to apply for the test, you must bring proper identification, and that identification has to contain four things: your photograph, your signature, your date of birth, and your permanent mailing address. The date of birth matters because it has to show you meet, or will meet, the age requirement in this part for the certificate and rating you are after before your airman knowledge test report expires (see the sections of this part that set the age requirement and the report's validity period). If your permanent mailing address is a post office box, that alone is not enough — you must also give a current residential address. Third, the Administrator sets the minimum passing grade; the number is not written into this section.

Illustration: You book a knowledge test at an FAA-approved testing center and bring your state driver's license. It has your photo, your signature, your date of birth, and your address. At check-in the proctor notices the address on the license is a P.O. box. Under paragraph (b)(4), that is not sufficient on its own, so you are asked for a current residential address as well. Because you also brought a utility bill showing your street address, you satisfy the identification requirement and sit the test. Had you brought only the license, the testing center could have turned you away — not to be difficult, but because the rule requires a residential address whenever the permanent mailing address is a P.O. box.

Common misreading: People read "minimum passing grade" and assume this section states a percentage. It does not — paragraph (c) only says the Administrator specifies it. Another trip-up is assuming the identification requirements are a suggestion from the testing center; they are in the regulation itself, and all four elements in paragraph (b) must be present on the identification you bring to the application.
