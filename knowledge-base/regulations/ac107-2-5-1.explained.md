This is the tail of the applicability statement for a chapter of Advisory Circular (AC) 107-2A. It tells you what the chapter covers: the operating limitations that apply to small unmanned aircraft system (small UAS) operations, and the responsibilities of four kinds of people — the remote pilot in command (PIC), the person manipulating the controls, the visual observer (VO), and anyone else who directly participates in the operation. The chunk then gives the test for who counts as a direct participant: a person is a direct participant if his or her involvement is necessary for the safe operation of the small UAS. That is a function test, not a job-title test. Ask whether the operation's safety depends on what the person is doing. If it does, that person is a direct participant and the chapter's guidance on participant responsibilities reaches them.

Illustration: You are the remote PIC flying a roof inspection. With you are a VO watching the aircraft, a crew member holding a radio relaying your calls to the building manager inside, and the building manager's assistant who wandered over to watch from the parking lot. The VO and the radio relay crew member are doing work the safe conduct of the flight depends on, so by the chunk's test they are direct participants and the chapter's participant responsibilities apply to them. The assistant watching from the parking lot contributes nothing the operation's safety depends on, so that person is not a direct participant under this test.

Common misreading: People read "anyone else who may directly participate" as covering every person who is present, on the client's payroll, or who signed in at the job site. The chunk sets a narrower test: involvement must be necessary for the safe operation of the small UAS. Being nearby, being interested, or being employed by the customer does not by itself make someone a direct participant.
