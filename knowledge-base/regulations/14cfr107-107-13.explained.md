This section is short because it does the work by cross-reference. If you operate a civil small unmanned aircraft system (sUAS) — that is, a drone and its associated parts that is not a public aircraft — for purposes of flight, you must comply with § 91.203(a)(2) of this chapter. That is the registration provision in Part 91, and this rule pulls it into Part 107. The chunk itself does not spell out what § 91.203(a)(2) requires, how registration is obtained, or what documentation must be carried; for those details, see that section. The practical takeaway from this chunk alone is that registration compliance is not optional background paperwork — it is a Part 107 obligation that attaches to the act of operating the aircraft for flight.

Illustration: You buy a new drone to photograph a client's roof. Before the first flight, you work through your Part 107 checklist: airspace, weather, airworthiness condition. You reach § 107.13 and see it points to § 91.203(a)(2). Because your drone is a civil aircraft and you are about to operate it for purposes of flight, you stop and confirm your registration obligations under that cross-referenced section before you launch, rather than treating registration as something separate from your Part 107 duties.

Common misreading: People read § 107.13 as a one-line rule with no content and move past it. It is a pointer, not a dead end — the actual requirement lives in § 91.203(a)(2), and § 107.13 makes a failure to meet it a Part 107 violation. A second misreading is thinking it applies only when you are flying commercially; the chunk ties the obligation to operating a civil sUAS for purposes of flight, not to the purpose of the mission.
