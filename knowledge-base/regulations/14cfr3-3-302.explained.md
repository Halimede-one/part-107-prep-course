This is a definitions-only chunk. It defines three address-related terms used elsewhere in the rules, and it does not itself impose a duty to do anything. (1) "U.S. agent address" means an address in the States of the United States, the District of Columbia, or any U.S. territory or possession. Which kind of address depends on who the agent is: if the agent is an entity, it must be the entity's office address; if the agent is an individual, it must be that person's usual place of residence, or, where applicable, the person's U.S. military office address. If someone is serving as a U.S. agent in their official capacity with the military, a military office address may be used. A U.S. agent address may never be a post office box, a military post office box, or a mail drop box. (2) "U.S. agent for service" (shortened to "U.S. agent") means an entity, or an adult — defined here as an individual who is 18 or older — with a U.S. address, whom a holder or applicant for a certificate, rating, or authorization designates to receive Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) service on their behalf. In other words, it is the person or organization you name to accept FAA paperwork for you. (3) "U.S. physical address" also means an address in the States, the District of Columbia, or any U.S. territory or possession, but it excludes post office boxes, military post office boxes, mail drop boxes, and commercial addresses that are not also residential addresses. Note the difference between the two address definitions: the agent-address definition rules out the three box types, while the physical-address definition rules out those three plus commercial addresses that are not also residences. Whether and when you must supply either one is set by other sections, not by this one — see those sections.

Illustration: A remote pilot living abroad needs to name someone in the United States to receive FAA correspondence for her. She first thinks of her 19-year-old cousin in Ohio and asks for his mailing address; he gives her a UPS Store mail drop box he uses for packages. That will not work: the definition says a U.S. agent address may not be a mail drop box. Her cousin does qualify as a U.S. agent on the other points — he is an individual 18 or older with a U.S. address — so she instead uses his usual place of residence, his apartment street address in Ohio, which fits the definition of a U.S. agent address for an individual.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, people treat "U.S. agent address" and "U.S. physical address" as the same thing. They are not: a commercial address that is not also a residence is still excluded from a "U.S. physical address," but that exclusion does not appear in the U.S. agent address definition, which instead points to an entity's office address or an individual's residence. Second, people assume a military post office box is acceptable because a military office address can be. The chunk allows a military office address in the two situations it describes, but it separately and flatly bars military post office boxes.
