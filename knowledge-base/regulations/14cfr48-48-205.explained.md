This section covers how the unique identifier — the registration number you get when you register your small unmanned aircraft — has to be shown on the aircraft itself. Three requirements: keep it legible, meaning readable and not worn away or smudged; attach it by whatever means it takes so it stays attached for the whole operation; and display it legibly on an external surface of the aircraft. "External surface" means the outside of the aircraft, not inside a battery compartment or under a removable hatch. The chunk does not name a minimum character size or a specific attachment method, so any method that actually holds through the flight satisfies paragraph (b).

Illustration: You wrote your registration number on a paper label and stuck it to the underside of your drone's shell with a piece of tape. On the last flight the tape lifted at one corner and the label is now half peeled off, with the ink faded from sun and rain so only the first few characters can be read. Before the next flight you replace it with a weatherproof adhesive label, pressed flat on the outside of the airframe where it can be read without opening anything. Reason: the old label failed all three requirements — it was no longer legible, it did not stay affixed through the operation, and a label flapping loose is not a display.

Common misreading: People assume that because Remote Identification broadcasts the number electronically, the physical marking is no longer needed. This section still requires the identifier to be legibly displayed on an external surface, and says nothing about an electronic broadcast substituting for it.
