This part of the chapter covers what happens when a small unmanned aircraft is loaded beyond what it can handle. An overloaded aircraft may not get off the ground at all. If it does lift off, it may fly in unexpected and unusually poor ways. The first sign of trouble usually shows up during takeoff. The chunk lists the main performance penalties of excess weight: higher takeoff speed, longer takeoff run, reduced rate and angle of climb, lower maximum altitude, shorter range, reduced cruising speed, reduced maneuverability, higher stalling speed, higher approach and landing speed, and longer landing roll. The practical point is that the remote pilot has to know how weight affects the specific aircraft being flown. Extra weight eats into safety margins by itself, and gets more dangerous when combined with other things that already reduce performance. The pilot also has to think ahead about how an overweight aircraft would behave if an emergency came up.

Illustration: A remote pilot normally flies a fixed-wing small unmanned aircraft with a light camera. For a mapping job the client wants a heavier sensor added. On the first takeoff attempt the aircraft uses noticeably more of the field before it lifts off, then climbs shallowly and slowly toward the tree line at the end of the strip. That is the chunk's warning showing up exactly where it says it will, during takeoff: longer takeoff run, reduced rate and angle of climb. The pilot lands, removes the added weight, and reconsiders the payload rather than pressing on, because the aircraft has already demonstrated it has less climb performance than the departure path needs.

Common misreading: People read the list as ten separate problems and assume a heavy aircraft only has whichever one they notice. They are all consequences of the same cause, and they stack. An overloaded aircraft that struggled on takeoff will also have a shorter range, less maneuverability, and a longer landing roll on the same flight. The other trap is treating a successful liftoff as proof the load was fine. The chunk says an overloaded aircraft may become airborne and still fly with unusually poor characteristics.
