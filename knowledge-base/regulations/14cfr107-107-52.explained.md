This rule says that when you fly a small unmanned aircraft system (small UAS, meaning the drone plus its control equipment) under Part 107, you may not have a transponder turned on. A transponder is the radio device that replies to Air Traffic Control (ATC) radar with a code and, in some versions, altitude. The default under Part 107 is transponder off. The only way to fly with one on is if the Administrator authorizes it — the chunk does not describe how to request that authorization, so see other sections or FAA guidance for the process. Note the wording: the prohibition is on operating with a transponder on, not on carrying one. The citation line at the end is just the amendment history showing when this text was added.

Illustration: You buy a larger hobby airframe that came from the factory with a Mode S transponder installed, and you plan to use it for a Part 107 bridge inspection near a Class D airport. You get your airspace authorization through LAANC and head out. Before launch you confirm the transponder is switched off, because Part 107 does not let you operate with it on unless the Administrator has authorized it, and your airspace authorization is not that authorization. The drone can keep the hardware aboard; it just stays powered down for the flight.

Common misreading: People read this as "my drone must not have a transponder" or confuse it with Remote ID. The rule is about the transponder being on during operation, not about owning or installing one, and it says nothing about Remote ID broadcast modules, which are a separate requirement in their own sections. Others assume an airspace authorization or a controller's verbal okay covers it; the chunk requires authorization by the Administrator for the transponder specifically.
