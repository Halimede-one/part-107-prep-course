If you register an aircraft, the mailing address on that registration has to stay current. This section says that within 30 days after any change in a registered owner's mailing address, the registered owner must notify the Registry in writing about the change. There is a second piece for people who use a post office box or mailing drop as their mailing address: in that case the owner must also give the Registry the owner's physical address or location, not just the box. And if that physical address or location later changes, the owner must notify the Registry in writing of the new address or location within 30 days as well. So a box user has two things that can trigger the 30-day notice clock, the mailing address and the physical location. Once the Registry accepts the notice, it will issue a revised Certificate of Aircraft Registration showing the new mailing address, and the chunk says this is done without charge. The duty sits on the registered owner, and it is a written notice, not a phone call.

Illustration: A remote pilot owns a drone registered in her name and she moves from an apartment to a house across town on June 1. She receives mail at a post office box that she keeps after the move, so her mailing address has not changed at all, but her physical address has. Because she uses a post office box for mailing purposes and her physical location changed, she must notify the Registry in writing of her new physical address within 30 days, so by July 1. Her mailing address stayed the same, so the certificate itself does not need a new mailing address on it, but the written notice of the new physical location is still required.

Common misreading: People read this as being only about mailing addresses and assume that if their mail still arrives, nothing needs reporting. For post office box and mailing drop users the physical address or location is separately reportable, and a change to it starts its own 30-day clock even when the mailing address is untouched.
