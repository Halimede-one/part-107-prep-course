A person who holds a certificate issued under this subpart can give it up on purpose and have it cancelled. This is voluntary — nobody is taking it away. But the request has to carry a specific signed statement, or something equivalent to it. The statement says the holder is surrendering the remote pilot certificate with a small UAS (unmanned aircraft system) rating for cancellation, that the request is made for the holder's own reasons, and that the holder understands the certificate will not be reissued unless the holder again completes the requirements in §§ 107.61 and 107.63 — see those sections for what those requirements are. The point of the signed statement is to make clear the holder knew what they were giving up. The chunk also marks the start of Subpart D, Operations Over Human Beings, with its source note; no rule text from that subpart appears here.

Illustration: A remote pilot decides she is done flying commercially and wants nothing more to do with the certificate. She writes to the FAA asking that her remote pilot certificate be cancelled, but her letter just says "please cancel my certificate." That is not enough under paragraph (b) — the request has to include the signed statement quoted in the rule, or an equivalent, acknowledging that the surrender is for her own reasons and that getting the certificate back means completing the requirements in §§ 107.61 and 107.63 all over again. She rewrites the letter with that statement and signs it.

Common misreading: People read "voluntary surrender" as a pause button — hand it in now, ask for it back later. The signed statement says the opposite: the certificate will not be reissued unless the holder again completes the requirements in §§ 107.61 and 107.63. Surrender is a cancellation, not a suspension.
