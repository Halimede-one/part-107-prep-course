This section bans flying a small unmanned aircraft over a person unless one of three things is true. First, the person is directly participating in the operation — that means someone who is part of the flight crew or otherwise involved in flying the aircraft. Second, the person is under a covered structure or inside a stationary vehicle that can give reasonable protection if the aircraft falls on them. Note the vehicle must be stationary, not moving. Third, the flight meets the requirements of at least one of the operational categories in subpart D of this part — see that subpart for what those categories require. If none of the three applies, you may not fly over that person. The rule is written as a prohibition on the operator, so the responsibility sits with the person flying.

Illustration: You are hired to shoot photos of a farmers market. Your visual observer stands beside you, several vendors are under canvas-roofed booths, and shoppers are walking in the open aisles. You may fly over your visual observer, because that person is directly participating in the operation. You may fly over the booths if the covering gives reasonable protection from a falling aircraft. You may not fly over the shoppers in the open aisles unless your flight meets one of the subpart D operational categories. So you plan your flight lines along the booth rows and the empty edge of the lot, and you hold position rather than crossing the open aisle when shoppers are in it.

Common misreading: Two traps. One is reading "directly participating" loosely to cover the client who hired you or spectators who agreed to be filmed — the chunk ties it to participating in the operation of the aircraft, not to consent or to being nearby. The other is forgetting the word "stationary" in paragraph (b): a person inside a car that is driving down the road is not covered by that exception.
