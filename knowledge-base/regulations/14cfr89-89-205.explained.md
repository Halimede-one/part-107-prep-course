This section limits who may ask the FAA to create an FAA-recognized identification area (FRIA) under this subpart. Only two kinds of applicants are eligible. The first is a community-based organization that the Administrator recognizes. The second is an educational institution, and the chunk spells out that this includes primary and secondary schools, trade schools, colleges, and universities. Nobody else is on the list. An individual drone pilot, a club that has not been recognized by the Administrator, a hobby shop, or a private company cannot submit the application themselves. The chunk covers eligibility to apply only; it says nothing about what the application must contain, how long an area lasts, or what flying inside one allows, so see the other sections of this subpart for those points.

Illustration: A group of five neighbors who fly model aircraft in a shared field want their field designated as a FRIA so they can keep flying their older aircraft that lack remote identification. Under this section they are not eligible to apply as an informal group of individuals. Their options are to work through a community-based organization that the Administrator recognizes, or to partner with an eligible educational institution such as a nearby high school or community college that would be the applicant. The reason is that the section lists only those two categories of eligible persons.

Common misreading: People read "community-based organization" as any local flying club. The chunk says it must be a community-based organization recognized by the Administrator, so recognition is part of the eligibility, not just being a community group.
