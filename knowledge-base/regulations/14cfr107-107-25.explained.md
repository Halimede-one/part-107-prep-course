This section bans two things. First, you may never operate a small unmanned aircraft system (sUAS, meaning the drone plus its control equipment) from a moving aircraft. There is no exception to that one. Second, you may not operate from a moving land vehicle or a moving water-borne vehicle (a car, truck, boat, and so on) unless two conditions are both met: the small unmanned aircraft is flown over a sparsely populated area, and it is not transporting another person's property for compensation or hire. Both conditions have to hold at once. If the area is not sparsely populated, the moving-vehicle operation is out. If you are carrying someone else's property for pay, the moving-vehicle operation is out, even over a sparsely populated area. Note that the restriction is on where the aircraft is flown, and on what it is carrying, not just on where the vehicle is driving.

Illustration: You are hired to photograph a rancher's fence line that runs for eleven miles across open grazing land. Driving the line and launching from a stop each time would take all day, so you ride in the passenger seat while a colleague drives the ranch road slowly, and you fly the drone alongside the truck. The land is open range with one house in sight and no other structures or people. That is a sparsely populated area, and you are carrying no one else's property on the aircraft, only your own camera, so flying from the moving truck is allowed. Later the same client asks you to fly a parcel of documents from the ranch office to a crew at the far gate while continuing to drive the road. You decline the moving-vehicle version of that task: the drone would be transporting another person's property for compensation, which removes the exception, so you stop the truck and fly from a stationary position instead.

Common misreading: People read the two conditions in paragraph (b) as alternatives, as if a sparsely populated area excuses a paid delivery, or as if an unpaid flight excuses operating over a populated area. The word 'and' joins them: sparsely populated and not transporting another person's property for compensation or hire. People also try to apply paragraph (b)'s exception to paragraph (a); there is no sparsely-populated exception for operating from a moving aircraft.
