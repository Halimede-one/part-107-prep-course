This section does not spell out its own alcohol and drug rules. Instead it points to two other sections of the regulations, §§ 91.17 and 91.19 of this chapter, and says that three groups of people must comply with them: anyone manipulating the flight controls of a small unmanned aircraft system (small UAS, meaning the drone plus its control equipment), anyone acting as remote pilot in command, and anyone acting as a visual observer. So the duty is not limited to the certificate holder running the flight. A helper on the sticks and a spotter watching the aircraft are covered too. For what §§ 91.17 and 91.19 actually require, see those sections.

Illustration: You are the remote pilot in command for a roof inspection and you bring a friend along as your visual observer. At lunch the friend has a beer. Even though the friend never touches the controller, § 107.27 applies to visual observers by name, so you cannot simply treat the friend as a bystander. You either check the friend's condition against the requirements in §§ 91.17 and 91.19 or you find a different observer, because the rule reaches the observer role, not just the pilot role.

Common misreading: People read this as a rule that only binds the remote pilot in command, or they assume that because Part 107 flying is low-risk the alcohol and drug provisions are somehow lighter. The chunk does neither. It names three roles, and it hands the actual substance of the requirement off to §§ 91.17 and 91.19 without softening it.
