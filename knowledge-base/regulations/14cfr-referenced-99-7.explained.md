This is a short security rule that part 107 pulls in, so it applies to remote pilots flying small unmanned aircraft. If you are operating an aircraft inside an ADIZ (Air Defense Identification Zone) or a Defense Area, you must follow two things at once: the normal rules of part 99, and any special security instructions the Administrator issues in the interest of national security. Those special instructions come out of an agreement between the FAA (Federal Aviation Administration) and the Department of Defense, or between the FAA and a U.S. Federal security or intelligence agency. The chunk does not list what the instructions say or where to find them — the point is simply that when they are issued and you are in one of those areas, they are binding on you on top of the regular rules.

Illustration: You plan a mapping flight for a client whose property sits inside an Air Defense Identification Zone. Before the flight you check the FAA's notices and find that a special security instruction has been issued for that airspace. Even though your flight would otherwise be routine and legal, § 99.7 requires you to comply with that instruction, so you follow its terms — and if you cannot, you do not fly. The reason: inside an ADIZ or Defense Area, the special security instruction applies in addition to, not instead of, everything else you normally follow.

Common misreading: People read "in addition to the applicable rules of this part" as if the special instructions replace the ordinary rules, or as if following part 99's routine requirements is enough. It is the opposite — the instructions stack on top. The other common slip is assuming this only concerns manned aircraft; the section says "each person operating an aircraft," and part 107 incorporates it, so it reaches remote pilots too.
