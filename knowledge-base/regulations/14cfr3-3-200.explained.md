This section links Transportation Security Administration (TSA) security findings to FAA certificates. If TSA tells the FAA that a person holding a certificate — or part of a certificate — issued by the FAA poses, or is suspected of posing, a risk of air piracy or terrorism, or a threat to airline or passenger safety, the FAA will issue an order amending, modifying, suspending, or revoking that certificate or that part of it. The trigger is the TSA notification itself. The rule speaks of certificates issued by the FAA generally, so it reaches individual certificate holders without naming any one kind of certificate. Note the chunk does not describe any procedure, notice, or review process that follows the order; for anything beyond the sentence above, see other sections of this part.

Illustration: A person holds an FAA-issued certificate and flies small drones for a roofing inspection company. TSA notifies the FAA that it suspects this individual of posing a threat to airline or passenger safety. Under this section, the FAA will issue an order acting against the certificate — amending, modifying, suspending, or revoking it, or a part of it. The reason is simply that TSA made the notification; the FAA's action follows from that notice rather than from anything the pilot did or failed to do in the course of a flight.

Common misreading: Two common misreadings. First, people assume the FAA independently investigates and must prove the security concern before acting; the text says the FAA will issue the order when TSA notifies it, and it covers individuals TSA merely suspects of posing a risk. Second, people read 'certificate' here as limited to airline transport or airman medical certificates; the section says any certificate or part of a certificate issued by the FAA and held by an individual.
