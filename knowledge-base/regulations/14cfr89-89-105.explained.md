This is the core remote identification (remote ID) requirement. Remote ID is the capability for an unmanned aircraft to broadcast identifying information about itself while it flies. The rule says that after September 16, 2023, no person may operate an unmanned aircraft anywhere in the airspace of the United States unless the operation meets the requirements of either § 89.110 or § 89.115 — see those sections for what each one requires. There are two ways out of the requirement: the Administrator can authorize something different, or the operation can fall under § 89.120, which this chunk does not describe — see that section. Note how broad the wording is: it applies to any person operating any unmanned aircraft in U.S. airspace, not just to Part 107 operations.

Illustration: A remote pilot buys a used aircraft at a swap meet and plans to fly it for a roof inspection next weekend. Before flying, she has to confirm the operation will satisfy § 89.110 or § 89.115, or that it is covered by § 89.120 or a separate authorization from the Administrator. Her flight date is well after September 16, 2023, so the requirement is in force. If the aircraft cannot meet either section and no exception applies, she does not fly — the rule bars the operation outright, not just the identification part of it.

Common misreading: People read this as a rule about equipment on the aircraft, so they assume a drone without broadcast hardware is simply non-compliant and nothing more. The section is written about the operation, not the aircraft: if the operation does not meet § 89.110 or § 89.115 and no exception applies, the flight itself is prohibited. The other common slip is treating the September 16, 2023 date as still pending — it has passed, so the requirement already applies.
