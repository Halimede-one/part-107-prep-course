This section covers registration for small unmanned aircraft (drones) flown only under 49 U.S.C. 44809, the statute for limited recreational operations. Four points:

(a) One certificate, many aircraft. A Certificate of Aircraft Registration issued under § 48.110 (see that section) for aircraft operated exclusively in compliance with 49 U.S.C. 44809 counts as registration for all of that person's aircraft used exclusively for those operations. The registration attaches to the individual named on the application, not to a single airframe.

(b) When it starts. The aircraft is registered when the applicant receives the certificate. The effective date is the date of issue printed on the certificate.

(c) Expiration and renewal. The certificate expires 3 years after the date of issue unless renewed. To renew, the holder verifies — in the form and manner the Administrator prescribes — that the § 48.110 information is still accurate, and supplies updated information if it is not. The verification may be done at any time in the six months preceding the month of expiration. A renewed certificate expires three years from the expiration date of the previous certificate, not three years from the day you renewed.

(d) Other things that end it. Apart from being revoked, canceled, or expired, a certificate stops being effective on the date the holder loses U.S. citizenship; thirty days after the holder's death; or when an owner who is a non-citizen resident alien loses resident alien status, unless that person becomes a U.S. citizen at the same moment.

Illustration: A recreational flyer registered under this section on June 10, 2024, so the certificate expires June 10, 2027. In February 2027 she buys a second drone and flies it only recreationally — she does not need a separate registration, because paragraph (a) makes her one certificate cover all aircraft she owns and uses exclusively for 49 U.S.C. 44809 operations. In March 2027 she renews early: March falls within the six months before the month of expiration, so the timing is allowed. Her new certificate runs three years from the previous expiration date, so it is good until June 10, 2030 — renewing three months early does not cost her three months of coverage.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, the blanket "all my drones on one registration" treatment in paragraph (a) applies only to aircraft used exclusively in compliance with 49 U.S.C. 44809. The moment a flyer wants to operate an aircraft outside that statute, this section's coverage no longer fits it. Second, people read the renewal clock as restarting on the day they renew; paragraph (c)(2) says the new certificate expires three years from the previous certificate's expiration date.
