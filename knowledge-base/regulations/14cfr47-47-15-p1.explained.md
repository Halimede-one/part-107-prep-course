This section covers how a U.S. registration number (also called a registration mark, the "N-number") is obtained and used. Anyone applying to register an aircraft must write the number on the Aircraft Registration Application, AC Form 8050-1, and on any evidence sent with it. There is no charge for the number assignments described in paragraph (a). Paragraph (a) does not apply to three groups: aircraft manufacturers requesting a block of numbers under paragraph (c), people requesting a special registration number under paragraphs (d) through (f), and holders of a Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate requesting a temporary number under § 47.16 (see that section).

How you get the number depends on the aircraft's history. If the aircraft was never registered anywhere, you ask the Registry in writing, describing the aircraft by make, type, model, and serial number (or, for amateur-built aircraft, as provided in § 47.33(b)), and state that it has never been registered anywhere; if it came into the United States from a foreign country, you must also submit evidence it was never registered abroad. If it was last registered in the United States, you simply use the number already assigned to it, unless you are applying for a different number under paragraphs (d) through (f); if no number is assigned, you request one in writing with make, model, and serial number. If it was last registered in a foreign country, you must request a U.S. number in writing with make, model, and serial number, whether or not the foreign registration has ended, along with either evidence that foreign registration terminated under § 47.37(b) or your affidavit showing it has ended, or, if it has not ended, your affidavit stating the number will not be placed on the aircraft until foreign registration ends.

Authority to use a number obtained under (a)(1), (2), or (3) expires 90 days after the date it is issued unless you submit an Aircraft Registration Application and comply with § 47.33 or § 47.37, as applicable, within that period. The Registry can extend the 90 days if you show the delay was due to circumstances beyond your control.

The number format: no more than five symbols after the prefix letter "N." It can be all numbers (N10000), one to four numbers plus one suffix letter (N1000A), or one to three numbers plus two suffix letters (N100AB). The letters "I" and "O" may not be used. The first zero in a number must always be preceded by at least one of the numbers 1 through 9.

Manufacturers may request enough numbers to cover estimated production for the next 18 months, at no charge. Any available, unassigned number can be requested as a special registration number, either for a new aircraft or to change an existing aircraft's number, by applying to the Registry with the fee required by § 47.17. The Registry authorizes the change on the Assignment of Special Registration Numbers, AC Form 8050-64, and that authorization expires one year from issuance unless the number is permanently placed on the aircraft. Within five days after placing the number on the aircraft, the owner must complete and sign that form, state the date the number was placed on the aircraft, and return the original to the Registry. The duplicate form plus the current Certificate of Aircraft Registration must be carried in the aircraft as temporary authority to operate. That temporary authority ends when the owner receives the revised Certificate of Aircraft Registration showing the new number, and in no case is it valid more than 120 days from the date the number was placed on the aircraft. A special registration number may be reserved for no more than 1 year, renewable year to year by applying to the Registry with the § 47.17 fee.

When aircraft registration has ended as described in § 47.41(a), the number assignment is no longer authorized for use except as provided in § 47.31(c), and it will be cancelled: after the expiration date on the Certificate of Aircraft Registration if registration was not renewed under § 47.40(c); after the expiration date on the Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate for aircraft registered under Subpart C when the certificate was not renewed and the owner did not apply for registration under § 47.31; or when ownership has transferred.

Illustration: An operator buys a large unmanned aircraft that must be registered under Part 47 rather than the simpler online process. The aircraft has never been registered anywhere, so the operator writes to the Registry describing it by make, type, model, and serial number and stating it has never been registered. The Registry issues N4621G at no charge. The operator then gets busy and lets two and a half months pass without filing the Aircraft Registration Application. On day 88 the operator realizes the authority to use N4621G expires 90 days after the date it was issued, files AC Form 8050-1, and complies with § 47.33 within the window. Decision: file inside the 90 days, because the number assignment lapses otherwise and the operator would have to start the request over. Had a documented shipping or court delay outside the operator's control caused the holdup, the operator could have asked the Registry for an extension of the 90-day period.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, getting a number is not the same as being registered — the number assignment is only a 90-day placeholder, and the aircraft is not registered until the application is filed and § 47.33 or § 47.37 is satisfied. Second, people read the special-number temporary authority as lasting "until my new certificate shows up," with no outer limit. It does end when the revised Certificate of Aircraft Registration arrives, but it is never valid for more than 120 days from the date the number was placed on the aircraft. Also note the no-charge language applies to the assignments in paragraph (a) and the manufacturer blocks in paragraph (c); special registration numbers require the fee under § 47.17.
