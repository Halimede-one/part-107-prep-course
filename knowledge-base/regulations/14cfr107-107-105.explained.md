This section is a signpost. It tells you that flying a small unmanned aircraft over human beings is not a free-for-all: if you want to do it, your operation has to fit into one of four defined categories, and each category has its own rules in its own sections. The chunk assigns the sections: § 107.110 covers Category 1; §§ 107.115 and 107.120 cover Category 2; §§ 107.125 and 107.130 cover Category 3; and § 107.140 covers Category 4. The duty falls on the remote pilot in command (the person responsible for the flight). The chunk also names three exceptions where this routing does not control the operation: §§ 107.39(a) and (b) and § 107.145. This chunk does not state what any of those sections require, so for the actual conditions in each category, and for what the exceptions allow, see those sections.

Illustration: You are hired to shoot overhead footage of a charity 5K with runners passing beneath your flight path. Before accepting, you check this section and see that operating over human beings is only permitted in accordance with one of the four categories. Your next step is to determine which category your aircraft and operation could qualify under, and then read that category's own sections (for example, § 107.110 if you are looking at Category 1) to learn the actual conditions. You cannot simply decide the flight is safe and launch, because this section channels every over-people operation into a category or into one of the listed exceptions.

Common misreading: People read the list of four categories as if it were the rule itself and assume that merely picking a category authorizes the flight. It does not. This section only tells you which sections apply; the requirements you must actually satisfy live in those other sections.
