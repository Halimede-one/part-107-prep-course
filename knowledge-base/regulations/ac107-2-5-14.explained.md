This chunk covers flying a small unmanned aircraft from a vehicle that is in motion. Part 107 allows you to operate from a moving land vehicle (a car, truck, or similar) or a water-borne vehicle (a boat), but only over a sparsely-populated area. Operating from a moving aircraft is not allowed at all. There is also a separate limit tied to carrying cargo: if the small unmanned aircraft is transporting another person's property for compensation or hire, it may not be operated from any moving vehicle. The advisory circular cites § 107.25 for these rules — see that section for the regulatory text.

The sparsely-populated area part can be waived under §§ 107.200 and 107.205 (see those sections for how waivers work). A waiver of that provision does not loosen anything else. Every other Part 107 restriction still applies. The remote pilot in command (remote PIC) must still keep visual line of sight (VLOS) with the aircraft. The remote PIC is still responsible for making sure no person is exposed to undue risk if the aircraft suffers a loss of control (LOC) for any reason. And if a visual observer (VO) is riding in a different vehicle than the remote PIC, the two must still maintain effective communication.

Finally, the FAA points out that Federal rules are not the only rules in play. State and local traffic laws may apply to someone who is driving a vehicle while operating an unmanned aircraft from it. Everyone involved in the operation is responsible for complying with those State and local laws in addition to FAA regulations.

Illustration: A remote PIC is hired to shoot video of a pickup truck driving a ranch road through open rangeland with scattered buildings and almost no people. She rides in the truck bed with a spotter while a second person drives. Because the area is sparsely populated and the truck is a land vehicle, flying from the moving truck is permitted under Part 107. She keeps the aircraft in her own unaided sight the whole time, because the moving-vehicle allowance does not relax the visual line of sight requirement. The next week the same client asks her to launch from the truck to deliver a package of samples to a buyer across the property, paid per delivery. She declines to launch from the moving truck: the aircraft would be transporting another person's property for compensation, and that may not be done from any moving vehicle. She lands the truck-based idea and flies the delivery from a fixed ground position instead. She also checks with the ranch and the county, since State and local traffic law could restrict what the driver and crew may do in a moving vehicle.

Common misreading: Two things get confused here. First, people read a waiver of the sparsely-populated provision as a waiver of the whole moving-vehicle rule — it is not. Waiving that provision only removes the sparsely-populated area limit; visual line of sight, the duty to protect people from the consequences of a loss of control, and effective communication with a visual observer in another vehicle all still apply. Second, people assume "moving vehicle" includes aircraft, or that the compensation limit is about being paid for the flight. Operating from a moving aircraft is flatly prohibited, waiver aside, and the compensation restriction is specifically about the small unmanned aircraft carrying another person's property for compensation or hire — not about whether the pilot is being paid to fly.
