This section sets the terms for Category 1 operations — the simplest of the categories that allow flight over people. Two things must be true about the aircraft. First, it must weigh 0.55 pounds or less at takeoff and stay at or under that weight for the whole operation, counting everything on board or attached to it — battery, camera, payload, propeller guards, anything. Second, it must have no exposed rotating parts that would lacerate human skin on impact with a person. Part (b) adds a separate limit: no remote pilot in command may fly a small unmanned aircraft in sustained flight over open-air assemblies of human beings unless the operation meets the requirements of either § 89.110 or § 89.115(a) — see those sections for what they require. That assembly restriction in (b) applies regardless of which category you are flying under.

Illustration: You own a sub-250-gram drone that qualifies as Category 1 and you want to shoot footage of a neighborhood block party. You check the weight with the battery and the small ND filter you clipped on, and the total is still under 0.55 pounds. The props are shrouded, so there are no exposed rotating parts. For flying over the handful of people in the yard, you meet 107.110(a). But the main street is closed and a few hundred people are packed in watching a band — that is an open-air assembly. You decide not to fly sustained passes over the crowd, because paragraph (b) requires the operation to meet § 89.110 or § 89.115(a) first, and you have not confirmed you meet either.

Common misreading: People read the 0.55-pound limit as a takeoff-only check. The chunk says the aircraft must stay at 0.55 pounds or less throughout the duration of each Category 1 operation, including everything on board or attached. The other common error is assuming Category 1 eligibility by itself permits flight over crowds — paragraph (b) places a separate condition on sustained flight over open-air assemblies that Category 1 status alone does not satisfy.
