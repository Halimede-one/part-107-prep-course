This rule says you may not fly an aircraft over or near any area the President, the Vice President, or other public figures will visit or travel through, if doing so goes against the restrictions the Administrator has set and published in a Notice to Airmen (NOTAM). The rule applies to any person operating an aircraft, and part 107 incorporates it, so it applies to you as a remote pilot flying a small unmanned aircraft. Two things make this rule work: the restriction has to be established by the Administrator, and it has to be published in a NOTAM. That means the NOTAM is where you find out what is actually restricted — the area, the times, and what is prohibited. This section does not spell out distances or altitudes; it points you to the published NOTAM for those details.

Illustration: You have a commercial shoot scheduled Thursday afternoon in a downtown park. On Wednesday night a NOTAM is published establishing flight restrictions around that part of the city because the Vice President is speaking there Thursday. Your park sits inside the restricted area during the hours you planned to fly. You do not launch. Flying there during those hours would be operating in the vicinity of an area to be visited by the Vice President contrary to a published restriction, which is exactly what this section prohibits. You reschedule for Friday, after the NOTAM's effective period ends.

Common misreading: People read "the President, the Vice President, or other public figures" and assume the rule only bites when a named official is physically present, so a drone flight a few blocks away or an hour early must be fine. The rule is not written around the person's location — it is written around the restrictions published in the NOTAM. The NOTAM defines the area and the times, and those boundaries are what you have to respect. A second misreading is assuming the restriction is permanent or always the same size; it is whatever that particular NOTAM establishes, so you have to read the current one rather than rely on memory of a past event.
