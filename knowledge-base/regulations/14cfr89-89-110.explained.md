This section is one of the ways to satisfy the remote identification requirement in § 89.105 (see that section): flying a drone that is itself built as a standard remote identification unmanned aircraft. Unless the Administrator authorizes something different, you meet the requirement by operating such an aircraft under the conditions listed here.

There are two sets of conditions. The first set is about how you operate. From takeoff to shutdown, the aircraft must broadcast the message elements described in § 89.305 (see that section for what those elements are). If the broadcast stops, the person manipulating the flight controls must land the aircraft as soon as practicable.

The second set is about the aircraft itself. Its serial number must be listed on a declaration of compliance that the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) has accepted, or the aircraft must be covered by a design approval or production approval issued under part 21 and meet the requirements of subpart F of this part. The remote identification equipment must be functional and compliant from takeoff to shutdown, and it must not have been disabled. Finally, the aircraft's serial number must appear on its Certificate of Aircraft Registration under the applicable requirements of parts 47 and 48, or the serial number must have been given to the FAA in a notice of identification under § 89.130 before the operation.

Illustration: You are flying a roof inspection with a drone that came from the factory with built-in remote identification, and its serial number is on an FAA-accepted declaration of compliance and on your Certificate of Aircraft Registration. Partway through the flight your controller shows a warning that remote ID broadcast has stopped. You do not finish the last two roof faces. You bring the aircraft back and land it as soon as practicable, because paragraph (a)(2) requires landing as soon as practicable once the aircraft is no longer broadcasting the § 89.305 message elements. You troubleshoot on the ground before flying again.

Common misreading: Two things get missed. First, people treat 'has remote ID built in' as the whole requirement and forget the paperwork half: the serial number has to be on an FAA-accepted declaration of compliance (or the part 21 approval route) and also on the registration certificate, or else submitted in a § 89.130 notice of identification before the flight. Second, people read the broadcast window as 'during flight' and assume it ends at touchdown. The chunk says takeoff to shutdown, so the broadcast obligation runs until the aircraft is shut down, not just until it is on the ground.
