This paragraph sets out two linked duties. First, a small unmanned aircraft must be kept in a condition for safe operation. Second, before each flight, the remote pilot in command (remote PIC) must actually check the aircraft to verify that it is in that condition. The advisory circular cites § 107.15 as the rule behind this. The paragraph does not itself list what to inspect — it points to Chapter 7, Small Unmanned Aircraft Maintenance and Inspection, for guidance on how to decide whether an aircraft is in a condition for safe operation. For the details of that guidance, see that chapter.

Illustration: A remote PIC arrives at a site to fly a roof inspection. The aircraft flew fine the previous week and has been in its case since. She still walks through a preflight check of the aircraft before launching, because the duty under this paragraph is to verify the condition for safe operation prior to flight, not to rely on how it behaved last time. During the check she notices a hairline crack in one propeller, swaps it, and then flies.

Common misreading: People read "maintained in a condition for safe operation" as satisfied by a general sense that the aircraft is fine, or by a maintenance log entry. The paragraph makes the preflight check a separate, affirmative act: the remote PIC must conduct a check to verify the condition, each time, prior to flight.
