This chunk covers paragraph 6.8 of Advisory Circular (AC) 107-2A, on aeronautical knowledge training. It applies to a narrow group: people who hold a part 61 airman certificate other than a student pilot certificate, and who meet the flight review requirements of § 61.56. (The sentence opening paragraph 6.8 is cut off at the start of the chunk, and the heading for Chapter 7 appears at the end with no content yet.) For that group, paragraph 6.8.1 says the person may complete training instead of taking the initial knowledge test. This is the same path described in paragraph 6.7 — see that paragraph for its details. The training can be taken online at https://www.faasafety.gov. The chunk also says the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) offers completion options beyond what is on that website, including special pilot proficiency programs such as an FAA-provided WINGS course specific to small unmanned aircraft systems (small UAS). The chunk describes the purpose of those programs as offering tools and resources that strengthen decision-making skills and help the remote pilot keep operating safely within the bounds of part 107. Note what this paragraph does not do: it substitutes for the initial knowledge test, not for anything else in the certification process. The chunk does not list any other requirement being waived.

Illustration: A private pilot holds a current part 61 private pilot certificate and completed a flight review eight months ago, so she meets § 61.56. She wants to start flying a small drone commercially. Instead of scheduling and paying for the initial aeronautical knowledge test at a testing center, she logs into https://www.faasafety.gov and completes the online small UAS training course. Her reason: paragraph 6.8.1 lets a part 61 certificate holder other than a student pilot, who meets the § 61.56 flight review requirements, complete training in place of the initial knowledge test. Her flight instructor also mentions an FAA-provided WINGS course specific to small UAS operations as another route the FAA offers.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, people read "holds a part 61 certificate" and stop there — but the chunk twice excludes the student pilot certificate and twice conditions the option on meeting the § 61.56 flight review requirements. Both conditions must hold. Second, people treat the online course as the whole process. The chunk says the training replaces the initial knowledge test only; it says nothing about skipping any other step. And https://www.faasafety.gov is not the only place to complete it — the chunk says the FAA offers additional options, including WINGS courses specific to small UAS.
