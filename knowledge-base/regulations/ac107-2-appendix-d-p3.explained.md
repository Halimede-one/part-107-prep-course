This part of Appendix D is written for the person who checks an applicant's identity during the remote pilot certification process, and it tells that person two things.

First, paragraph D.9.1.8 covers what to do when something looks wrong with the identity document. If the document appears fraudulent, shows signs of tampering, or if the identifying information on it does not satisfactorily match the information on the application as explained in this guidance, the instruction is: do not challenge the individual. Instead, finish the application process, copy the documents that were presented, and record the person's name, address, and contact information including phone numbers, emails, and so on. That information then goes to supervisory, security, investigative, or law enforcement personnel as soon as possible. The point is to avoid a confrontation and let the proper authorities handle it.

Second, D.9.2 lists the documents, or combinations of documents, that are acceptable for proving identity. Most entries require that the document be unexpired: U.S. passport (book or card), Enhanced Tribal Card (ETC), Free and Secure Trade (FAST) Card, Global Entry Card, U.S. Enhanced Driver's License (EDL) or Enhanced Identification Card (EID), foreign passport, Reentry Permit (Form I-327), a driver's license issued by a State or outlying possession of the United States, a photo ID card issued by a State or outlying possession, a photo ID card issued by a regional, county, or city/municipal governmental authority (including law enforcement, governmental, education, transportation, or utility authorities), a U.S. military ID card, a U.S. retired military ID card, a U.S. military dependent's card, a Department of Homeland Security (DHS)/Transportation Security Administration (TSA) Transportation Worker Identification Credential (TWIC), and a Merchant Mariner Credential (MMC). The Permanent Resident Card (Form I-551), commonly called a Green Card, and a Native American tribal document with photo are also listed.

Some entries are combinations. An unexpired temporary driver's license works when paired with the expired driver's license. A U.S. Birth Certificate works with an unexpired government-issued ID. A U.S. Naturalization Certificate with a raised seal, or a Certificate of Naturalization issued by U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) or the Immigration and Naturalization Service (INS) (Form N-550 or N-570), works with a government-issued ID. A Certificate of Birth Abroad with raised seal, Department of State Form FS-545, or DS-1350 works with a government-issued ID. A Certificate of U.S. Citizenship with raised seal, or a Certificate of Citizenship issued by USCIS or INS, or a Certificate of Repatriation issued by USCIS or INS, works together with a government-issued ID. Finally, a Federal agency's written certification of its employee's U.S. citizenship is acceptable if the training is being conducted on behalf of that agency.

For a State-issued photo ID card, the list adds a condition: it must include a State or State agency seal or logo, such as a State university ID. The list also says permits are not considered valid identity documents, giving gun permits as the example.

Illustration: An applicant arrives to take the knowledge test and hands over a State photo ID card. The person checking identity notices the laminate has been lifted at one corner and the date of birth looks like it has been altered, and the name on the card does not match the name on the application. Rather than accusing the applicant of anything, the checker says nothing about it, completes the application process as normal, photocopies the card, and writes down the applicant's name, address, phone number, and email. Immediately afterward, the checker passes all of it to their supervisor and the appropriate security or law enforcement contact. The reason for handling it this way is exactly what D.9.1.8 directs: do not challenge the individual, document everything, and hand it up the chain promptly. Confronting a person over a suspected fraudulent document is not the checker's job and could create a safety problem on the spot.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, people read D.9.1.8 as permission to refuse the applicant or call them out on the spot. It says the opposite: complete the application process and report afterward. Second, people assume any official-looking card counts. The list is specific. Most items must be unexpired, a State photo ID card must carry a State or State agency seal or logo, permits such as gun permits do not count, and several items on the list only work when paired with a second document, such as a U.S. Birth Certificate with an unexpired government-issued ID. Showing up with just one half of a required pair is not enough.
