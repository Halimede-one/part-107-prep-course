This rule is a recordkeeping duty, and it falls on a narrow group: a person who submits a means of compliance that the Administrator accepts under this subpart. A "means of compliance" is the documented method a party puts forward to show that remote identification equipment meets the requirements of this part. If the FAA accepts that submission, the submitter has to keep certain records for as long as the means of compliance stays accepted, plus an additional 24 calendar months after that, and must make those records available for the Administrator to inspect. Three categories must be kept: (a) all documentation and substantiating data submitted to the FAA to get the means of compliance accepted; (b) records of all test procedures, methodology, and other procedures, as applicable; and (c) any other information needed to justify and substantiate how the means of compliance enables compliance with the remote identification requirements of this part. The line at the end of the chunk is a heading announcing Subpart F, Remote Identification Design and Production — see that subpart for its content.

Illustration: A standards organization submits a means of compliance describing how a broadcast module meets remote identification requirements, and the FAA accepts it. The organization keeps the full submission package, its lab test methodology, and its supporting analysis on file. Four years later the FAA notifies the organization that the means of compliance is no longer accepted. The records cannot be discarded at that moment — the rule requires retention for as long as the means of compliance is accepted plus an additional 24 calendar months, so the files stay available for inspection for two more years past the end of acceptance.

Common misreading: A remote pilot reading this may think they personally have to keep remote identification paperwork for 24 calendar months. They do not. This section applies only to the person who submits an accepted means of compliance, which is typically an industry body or a manufacturer-side submitter, not an operator flying under Part 107.
