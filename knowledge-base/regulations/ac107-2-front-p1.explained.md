This is front matter: the advisory circular's cover page and the first part of its table of contents. There is no rule text here. What it does tell you: Advisory Circular (AC) 107-2A, dated 2/1/21, was issued by the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) and initiated by AFS-800, signed by Robert C. Carty, Deputy Executive Director, Flight Standards Service. Its stated job is to provide guidance for conducting small unmanned aircraft systems (UAS) operations in the National Airspace System (NAS) in accordance with Title 14 of the Code of Federal Regulations (14 CFR) part 107. The contents listing shows how the document is laid out: Chapter 1 General (purpose, where to find it, what it cancels, requests for information, feedback form), Chapter 2 References, Chapter 3 Background, Chapter 4 covering part 107 Subpart A (General), and Chapter 5 covering part 107 Subpart B (Operating Rules). The chapter structure mirrors the structure of part 107 itself, so the AC can be used as a companion read alongside the regulation.

Illustration: A remote pilot wants to understand what the FAA expects regarding aeronautical decision-making before a job. Scanning this table of contents, the pilot sees paragraph 5.3, Aeronautical Decision-Making (ADM) and Crew Resource Management (CRM), on page 5-2, and goes straight there rather than reading the whole document. The table of contents is a navigation tool, and the page numbers are chapter-relative (5-2 means Chapter 5, page 2).

Common misreading: Do not treat an advisory circular as the regulation. This document says it provides guidance for operating in accordance with part 107; the binding requirements live in part 107 itself. Also, the topics named in the contents are only labels here, with no requirements stated yet, so nothing in this chunk tells you what any of those paragraphs actually require.
