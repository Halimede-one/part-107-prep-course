This section sets the price of registering a small unmanned aircraft (a drone) with the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), and the price of renewing that registration. There are two registration paths in Part 48, described in § 48.100 and § 48.105 — see those sections for what each one covers. The fee is $5.00 either way, but the unit of measure differs. Under § 48.100 the fee is $5.00 per aircraft. Under § 48.105 the fee is $5.00 per certificate. The same $5.00 applies whether you are registering for the first time or renewing. Paragraph (c) says the money has to travel with the paperwork: every application and every renewal must be accompanied by the applicable fee, paid to the FAA through the web-based aircraft registration system. The chunk also leaves room for another payment method if the Administrator prescribes one, but the web-based system is the stated default. The line following the citation is a heading for the next subpart, not a rule.

Illustration: A remote pilot buys a second drone for a roof-inspection side business and goes to register it. His first drone is already registered and he pays nothing extra for it this year. For the new airframe he submits an application through the FAA's web-based registration system and pays the $5.00 fee at the time of the application, because paragraph (c) requires the fee to accompany the application. Two years later, when that registration comes up for renewal, he pays $5.00 again — renewal carries the same fee as the original issuance.

Common misreading: People read "$5.00" and assume one payment covers their whole fleet forever. It does not. Which $5.00 you owe depends on which section you register under — per aircraft under § 48.100, per certificate under § 48.105 — and the fee comes due again at renewal, not just at first issuance.
