This is a scope statement, not an operating rule. It tells you what the subpart it opens covers. Three things: (a) the requirements a "means of compliance" must meet for subpart D of this part; (b) the procedures for submitting a means of compliance and getting it accepted by the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), where that means of compliance is used in designing and producing standard remote identification unmanned aircraft or remote identification broadcast modules, so those products meet the minimum performance requirements of this part; and (c) the rules that apply to the persons who submit a means of compliance for FAA acceptance. A "means of compliance" is the method a designer or producer uses to show a product meets the performance requirements. So this subpart speaks to the people who design, produce, or submit methods for FAA acceptance — not to the person flying the aircraft. The chunk points to subpart D and to "the minimum performance requirements of this part" without stating them; see those sections for the substance.

Illustration: A remote pilot is studying Part 89 and reaches this subpart. She reads 89.401 and sees it covers requirements for means of compliance, the submission and acceptance process, and the rules for persons submitting. None of that describes anything she does as an operator — she neither designs nor produces aircraft nor submits methods to the FAA. She notes the subpart as background on how her drone's remote identification capability came to be approved, then moves on to the subparts that govern operations. Correct call: this subpart's three stated purposes all concern design, production, and submission, so it does not impose tasks on her as a pilot.

Common misreading: People assume that because the subpart mentions "standard remote identification unmanned aircraft" and "broadcast modules," it must contain the rules for flying with them. It does not. This section describes a subpart about methods of showing compliance and the FAA's acceptance process. The chunk itself points to subpart D for the compliance requirements, and the operating rules live elsewhere in the part.
