This section lists two kinds of drug- and alcohol-related conduct that can cost you a remote pilot certificate with a small unmanned aircraft system (UAS) rating, or keep you from getting one.

The first is a conviction. If you are convicted of violating any Federal or State law about growing, processing, manufacturing, selling, disposing of, possessing, transporting, or importing narcotic drugs, marijuana, or depressant or stimulant drugs or substances, the FAA may deny your certificate application for a period of up to 1 year after the date of final conviction. It may also suspend or revoke a certificate you already hold.

The second is committing an act prohibited by § 91.17(a) or § 91.19(a) of this chapter. The chunk does not spell out what those two sections prohibit, so see those sections for their content. Committing such an act carries the same two consequences: denial of an application for up to 1 year after the date of that act, or suspension or revocation of an existing certificate.

Note the difference in the clock. For paragraph (a), the 1-year denial period runs from the date of final conviction. For paragraph (b), it runs from the date of the act itself, with no conviction required.

Illustration: A commercial photographer plans to apply for a remote pilot certificate so she can fly a drone for real estate listings. Eight months ago she was finally convicted under a State statute for possession of a controlled substance. Under paragraph (a)(1), the FAA may deny her application for a period of up to 1 year after the date of final conviction, so an application filed now can be turned down on that basis. She waits until more than a year has passed from the conviction date before applying. The reason is that the denial window in the rule is measured from the final conviction, not from when she decided to start flying.

Common misreading: People read this as applying only to flying under the influence, and assume a drug conviction unrelated to any aircraft is harmless. Paragraph (a) does not mention aircraft at all. A conviction for the listed drug offenses is grounds for denial, suspension, or revocation on its own. The other trap is reading paragraph (b) as requiring a conviction. It does not. It is triggered by committing an act prohibited by § 91.17(a) or § 91.19(a), and the up-to-1-year denial period runs from the date of that act.
