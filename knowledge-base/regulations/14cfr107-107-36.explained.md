This is a flat prohibition. A small unmanned aircraft may not carry hazardous material at all. There is no permission, no weight allowance, and no exception written into this section. The chunk does not define hazardous material itself; it points to the definition in 49 CFR 171.8, which is a Department of Transportation regulation outside this section. If you need to know whether a specific substance counts, see that section for the definition.

Illustration: A remote pilot is hired by a pest control company to spray a small amount of chemical concentrate over a field using a drone. Before accepting, the pilot checks whether the concentrate meets the hazardous material definition in 49 CFR 171.8. If it does, the pilot cannot carry it under this section, and the answer is no regardless of how small the quantity is or how short the flight. The reason is that this section bars carriage outright rather than limiting it.

Common misreading: People assume a tiny quantity must be fine, or that securing the container properly makes it acceptable. The chunk sets no threshold and no packaging condition. They also assume the determination is theirs to make by common sense. It is not; the term is defined in 49 CFR 171.8, so whether a substance qualifies is settled by that definition, not by how dangerous it feels.
