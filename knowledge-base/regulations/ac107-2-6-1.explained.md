This is the opening scope line of a chapter in the advisory circular, not a rule itself. It says the chapter covers airman certification requirements and procedures for anyone acting as remote pilot in command (PIC) of a small unmanned aircraft system (small UAS) operated in the National Airspace System (NAS). It also clears up vocabulary: what people in everyday speech call a "license," the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) calls a "certificate," and the process of getting one is "certification."

Illustration: You search the FAA website for a "drone license" and find nothing by that name, but you do find material on remote pilot certification. Those are the same thing in FAA language, so this chapter is where you look for how to become the remote PIC of a small UAS. Because the chunk only states the chapter's scope, it tells you where to read next, not what the requirements are — see that chapter for those.

Common misreading: Because the FAA avoids the word "licensing," some people conclude a remote pilot certificate is something lesser, like a registration or permit. The chunk says the opposite: "certification" is simply the FAA's term for what others call licensing.
