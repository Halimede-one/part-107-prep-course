This passage explains how a small unmanned aircraft gets registered before you fly it under part 107. There are two paths. Part 48 is the streamlined online registration option, and it applies to small unmanned aircraft that will be operated only within the territorial limits of the United States. You do that at https://faadronezone.faa.gov/. Alternatively, an owner or operator can choose to register under part 47 instead, in the same manner as manned aircraft. Either way, registration under part 47 or part 48 must happen before you operate under part 107.

Section 5.4.1 adds a second layer for foreign civil aircraft. If small Unmanned Aircraft System (small UAS) operations involve foreign civil aircraft, the operator needs a Foreign Aircraft Permit under 14 CFR part 375, as described in section 375.41, before conducting any commercial air operations under that authority. The chunk defines foreign civil aircraft two ways: (a) an aircraft of foreign registry that is not part of the armed forces of a foreign nation, or (b) a U.S.-registered aircraft owned, controlled, or operated by persons who are not citizens or permanent residents of the United States. Application instructions are in section 375.43, and applications should go by electronic mail to the Department of Transportation (DOT) Office of International Aviation, Foreign Air Carrier Licensing Division. Foreign-owned and operated small UAS still must be registered under part 47 or part 48, including submission of an Affidavit of Ownership for Unmanned Aircraft if necessary. The chunk points to 49 U.S.C. section 44101(b)(1) for exceptions; see that provision for what those exceptions are.

Illustration: A remote pilot who is a lawful permanent resident of the United States buys a drone for roof inspection work and registers it online through FAADroneZone under part 48, since all of her flights will be inside the United States. That is enough for her. Her business partner, who is neither a U.S. citizen nor a permanent resident, wants to own and operate a second drone under the same business doing paid inspections. That aircraft fits definition (b) of foreign civil aircraft, a U.S.-registered aircraft owned, controlled, or operated by a person who is not a citizen or permanent resident. So before any commercial air operations with it, the partner needs a Foreign Aircraft Permit under part 375 in addition to registering the aircraft under part 47 or part 48, with an Affidavit of Ownership for Unmanned Aircraft if required. The reason is that the permit requirement attaches to who owns, controls, or operates the aircraft, not to where it is registered.

Common misreading: Two traps here. First, people assume part 48 online registration is the only option; the chunk says an owner may elect part 47 instead, the same route manned aircraft take. Second, people read the Foreign Aircraft Permit paragraph as a substitute for registration. It is not. A foreign-owned and operated small UAS must still be registered under part 47 or part 48, and the permit is an additional requirement before commercial air operations. Also note that part 48's streamlined option is described as being for aircraft operated only within the territorial limits of the United States.
