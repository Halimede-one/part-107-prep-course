This section says who may apply to register an aircraft in the United States and what registration does and does not prove. A person who wants to register an aircraft must submit an Aircraft Registration Application, AC Form 8050-1, under this part. The aircraft can only be registered by its owner and in the owner's legal name — not a nickname, not a club name, not an employer's name unless the employer is the actual owner. The chunk also quotes 49 U.S.C. 44103(c), which says registration is not evidence of ownership in any proceeding where someone's ownership is being disputed. The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) does not issue a certificate of ownership and does not endorse ownership information on a Certificate of Aircraft Registration. The FAA issues that certificate to whoever appears to be the owner based on the evidence of ownership submitted with the application under § 47.11, or already recorded at the Registry — see that section for what counts as evidence. Finally, the section defines "owner" broadly for purposes of this part: it includes a buyer in possession, a bailee, a lessee of an aircraft under a contract of conditional sale, and the assignee of any of those people.

Illustration: A remote pilot is leasing an aircraft under a conditional sale contract and has not finished paying for it. She wonders whether she can register it in her own name or must wait for the seller to do it. Paragraph (d) answers this: for purposes of this part, "owner" includes a lessee of an aircraft under a contract of conditional sale, so she falls within the definition and may apply. She fills out AC Form 8050-1 in her own legal name and includes the evidence of ownership required under § 47.11.

Common misreading: People treat the Certificate of Aircraft Registration as proof that they own the aircraft — handing it over in a sale dispute or an insurance claim as if it settled the question. The chunk says the opposite: registration is not evidence of ownership in any proceeding where ownership is at issue, and the FAA neither issues a certificate of ownership nor endorses ownership information on the registration certificate. The FAA issues it to whoever *appears* to be the owner from the paperwork submitted.
