This section tells manufacturers what paperwork they owe the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) before they can sell remote identification equipment. It applies to the "person responsible for the production" of either a standard remote identification unmanned aircraft under § 89.515 or a remote identification broadcast module under § 89.520 — not to the remote pilot who buys and flies the product. That person must submit a declaration of compliance for FAA acceptance, in a form and manner acceptable to the Administrator.

Paragraph (b) lists what the declaration must contain for a standard remote identification unmanned aircraft, at a minimum: (1) the producer's name, physical address, telephone number, and email address; (2) the aircraft's make and model; (3) its serial number, or the range of serial numbers being covered; (4) the FCC Identifier of the 47 CFR part 15-compliant radio frequency equipment built into the aircraft; (5) the means of compliance used in design and production; (6) whether this is an initial or an amended declaration, and if amended, why; (7) a declaration that the producer can show the aircraft was designed and produced to meet the minimum performance requirements of § 89.310 using an FAA-accepted means of compliance, and that the producer complies with § 89.515(b); and (8) a statement that the 47 CFR part 15-compliant radio frequency equipment is integrated without modifying its authorized radio frequency parameters.

Paragraph (c) mirrors that list for a remote identification broadcast module, with two differences. The performance requirements referenced are those of § 89.320 rather than § 89.310, and the compliance declaration points to § 89.520(b). Item (8) also adds a second statement: that instructions have been provided for installing the broadcast module without modifying its authorized radio frequency parameters. That extra item makes sense because a module is installed by someone else after purchase, so the installation instructions matter.

For the contents of § 89.310, § 89.320, § 89.515, and § 89.520, see those sections — this chunk only cites them.

Illustration: A small company builds a bolt-on broadcast module for older drones. Before offering it for sale, the engineer preparing the FAA submission works down paragraph (c): company contact details, the module's make and model, the block of serial numbers the first production run will use, the FCC Identifier of the radio board inside, the means of compliance used, and a note that this is an initial declaration. She then adds the declaration that the module was designed and produced to meet the minimum performance requirements of § 89.320 using an FAA-accepted means of compliance and that the company complies with § 89.520(b). Last, she writes the two statements required by (c)(8): the radio equipment is integrated without changing its authorized radio frequency parameters, and installation instructions are supplied so the buyer does not change them either. She includes the installation-instructions statement because her product is a module, not a built-in system — paragraph (b)(8), which covers standard remote identification unmanned aircraft, does not ask for it.

Common misreading: Pilots sometimes read this section as something they have to do. It is not. The declaration of compliance is submitted by whoever is responsible for producing the aircraft or module, and this section says nothing about a remote pilot filing anything. A second misreading is treating the lists as complete: both paragraph (b) and paragraph (c) say the declaration must include "at a minimum" these items, so the FAA can require more in the form and manner it accepts.
