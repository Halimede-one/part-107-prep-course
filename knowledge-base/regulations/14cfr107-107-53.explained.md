Automatic Dependent Surveillance-Broadcast (ADS-B) Out is equipment that broadcasts an aircraft's identity and position to air traffic control and to other aircraft. This section says that when you fly a small unmanned aircraft system (small UAS) under Part 107, you may not have ADS-B Out equipment running in transmit mode. The only exception in the chunk is if the Administrator authorizes it otherwise. Note what the rule does and does not reach: it bars operating with the equipment transmitting, not owning or carrying it. If your aircraft has ADS-B Out hardware, the rule points to keeping it from transmitting during a Part 107 flight, absent an authorization.

Illustration: A remote pilot buys a larger multirotor for survey work, and the airframe came with an ADS-B module that can both receive traffic and broadcast the aircraft's own position. Before the first job, the pilot goes into the settings and confirms the broadcast side is switched off, leaving only the receive function active. Reason: operating under Part 107 with ADS-B Out in transmit mode is prohibited unless the Administrator has authorized it, so the pilot either disables transmit or seeks that authorization first.

Common misreading: Some read this as a ban on all ADS-B gear, including receivers used to see nearby manned traffic. The chunk prohibits only ADS-B Out equipment in transmit mode. Others assume a waiver or authorization is impossible; the chunk opens with 'Unless otherwise authorized by the Administrator,' so an authorization path exists, though the chunk does not describe how to get one.
