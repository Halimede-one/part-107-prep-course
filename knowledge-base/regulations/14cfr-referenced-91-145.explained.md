This section covers temporary flight restrictions (TFRs) around air shows and big sporting events. Part 107 incorporates it, so it binds remote pilots flying small unmanned aircraft just as it binds manned pilots.

The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) issues a Notice to Airmen (NOTAM) marking off an area of airspace when it decides a restriction is needed to protect people or property on the surface or in the air, to keep air traffic safe and efficient, or to stop unsafe crowding of aircraft near an aerial demonstration or major sporting event. The chunk lists examples of such events: the Blue Angels, the Thunderbirds, the Golden Knights, the Summer and Winter Olympic Games, the Annual Tournament of Roses Football Game, World Cup Soccer, the Major League Baseball All-Star Game, the World Series, the Kodak Albuquerque International Balloon Fiesta, the Sandia Classic Hang Gliding Competition, the Indianapolis 500 Mile Race, and any other demonstration or sporting event the FAA decides needs a restriction under paragraph (b).

For events not on that list, paragraph (b) gives the twelve factors the FAA weighs: where the event is held, the effect on known aircraft operations, existing air traffic control (ATC) airspace traffic management restrictions, estimated duration, degree of public interest, number of spectators, provisions for spectator safety, number and types of participating aircraft, use of mixed high and low performance aircraft, impact on non-participating aircraft, weather minimums, and the emergency procedures that will be in effect.

The NOTAM itself tells you what you need: the event name, the effective dates and times, the geographic features or coordinates, and any other restrictions or procedures for flight in that airspace (paragraph (c)).

Paragraph (d) is the operative prohibition. Once such a NOTAM is issued, no person may operate an aircraft or device, or engage in any activity inside the designated airspace, except in accordance with the authorizations, terms, and conditions published in the NOTAM. The only ways around that are authorization from air traffic control, or a Flight Standards Certificate of Waiver or Authorization issued for that demonstration or event. Note the words "aircraft or device" and "any activity" — the reach is broad.

Paragraph (e) gives the normal sizes. For an aerial demonstration, the restricted area is normally limited to a 5 nautical mile radius from the center of the demonstration and an altitude of 17000 mean sea level (for high performance aircraft) or 13000 feet above the surface (for certain parachute operations). For a major sporting event, it is normally limited to a 3 nautical mile radius from the center of the event and 2500 feet above the surface. In both cases the area will be no greater than the minimum airspace necessary to manage aircraft operations near the specified area, and the actual size varies with the event and the paragraph (b) factors.

Paragraph (f): the NOTAM will be issued at least 30 days in advance, unless the FAA finds good cause for a shorter period and explains that in the NOTAM. Paragraph (g): when warranted, the FAA Administrator may exclude essential military, medical and rescue, Presidential and Vice Presidential, visiting heads of state, law enforcement and security, and public health and welfare flights from this section.

Illustration: You have a paid job to shoot promotional video of a downtown plaza on a Saturday afternoon. The plaza sits about 2 nautical miles from a stadium hosting the World Series that same day. You check NOTAMs the morning of the flight and find a temporary flight restriction for the event: a 3 nautical mile radius around the stadium, surface to 2500 feet, effective from two hours before first pitch until two hours after the final out. Your plaza is inside that circle, and your planned 200-foot altitude is inside it too, because the restriction starts at the surface. Decision: do not launch during those hours. Paragraph (d) says no person may operate an aircraft or device inside the designated airspace except per the NOTAM's terms, and the NOTAM gives you no opening. Your options are to fly outside the effective times, to relocate outside the circle, or to seek authorization from air traffic control or a Flight Standards Certificate of Waiver or Authorization issued for the event. Having a Part 107 certificate is not itself permission.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, people treat the 5 nautical mile / 3 nautical mile and the 17000 / 13000 / 2500 foot figures as the rule. They are not — they are what the restricted area will "normally" be limited to. The actual boundaries are whatever the NOTAM states, so read the NOTAM rather than assuming the standard shape. Second, people read the event list in paragraph (a) as the complete set of events that get restrictions. Item (12) covers any other demonstration or sporting event the FAA decides needs one, so a local air show or a college game can carry a TFR even though it is not named here. Also, the 30-day advance notice in paragraph (f) is not guaranteed; the FAA can issue on shorter notice for good cause, which is why you check NOTAMs on the day of the flight and not a month before.
