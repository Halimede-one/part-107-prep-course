This section lists four conditions that make an aircraft registration invalid from the start. Registration is invalid if, at the time it is made: the aircraft is already registered in a foreign country; the person applying is not the owner; the applicant is not qualified to apply under this part (Part 47 sets out who may apply — see that part); or the applicant's interest in the aircraft came from a transaction that was not made in good faith, and was instead done to dodge compliance with 49 U.S.C. 44101-44104 (the federal statutes on aircraft registration), whether or not the owner knew about it. Note the timing: the test is the state of things when the registration was made. If one of those conditions applied then, the registration was never valid — it does not have to be revoked to be void. Paragraph (b) adds a duty: if a registration is invalid under paragraph (a), and the Certificate of Aircraft Registration was issued in paper form, the holder must return that paper certificate to the Registry as soon as possible.

Illustration: A remote pilot's employer buys a drone that weighs more than the small-aircraft threshold for Part 47 registration, and the pilot's name is put on the registration application because the pilot handles all the paperwork. The pilot does not own the aircraft — the company does. Under paragraph (a)(2), the applicant is not the owner, so the registration is invalid. The correct move is to register in the owner's name instead, and if a paper Certificate of Aircraft Registration was already issued on the bad application, the holder must send it back to the Registry as soon as possible.

Common misreading: People read invalidity as something the FAA has to declare before it takes effect. It is not. The registration is invalid because of what was true when it was made; the duty in paragraph (b) to return a paper certificate is a consequence of the invalidity, not the thing that creates it. Also, (b) only speaks to certificates issued in paper form — that does not mean a non-paper registration escapes paragraph (a).
