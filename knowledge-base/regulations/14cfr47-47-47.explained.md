This section covers what you must do to cancel a U.S. Certificate of Aircraft Registration when the aircraft is being exported to another country. It applies to the holder of that certificate, and also to the holder of an irrevocable deregistration and export request authorization recognized under the Cape Town Treaty that has been filed with the FAA (Federal Aviation Administration). Either of those parties may ask for cancellation, and the request goes to the Registry.

The request package has three parts. First, a written request for cancellation that describes the aircraft by make, model, and serial number, and states the U.S. registration number and the country the aircraft is going to. Second, evidence about recorded rights. If the aircraft is not subject to the Cape Town Treaty, you need evidence satisfactory to the FAA that each holder of a recorded right has been satisfied or has consented to the transfer. If the aircraft is subject to the Treaty, the evidence covers each holder of a recorded right established before the date the Treaty entered into force with respect to the United States. Third, a written certification that all registered interests ranking in priority to the requestor's have been discharged, or that the holders of those interests have consented to the cancellation for export.

Two more rules close it out. If the aircraft is subject to the Cape Town Treaty and an irrevocable deregistration and export request authorization is on file with the Registry, the Registry will honor a cancellation request only if an authorized party makes it. And once cancellation happens, the Registry notifies the country the aircraft is being exported to.

Illustration: An agricultural spraying company owns a 68-pound unmanned aircraft registered under this part, and sells it to a buyer in Canada. The company still has a bank lien recorded against the aircraft. To cancel the U.S. registration for export, the company sends the Registry a written request listing the aircraft's make, model, and serial number, its U.S. registration number, and Canada as the destination country. Because the lienholder is the holder of a recorded right, the company must also provide evidence satisfactory to the FAA that the bank has been satisfied or has consented to the transfer, plus a written certification that interests ranking ahead of the company's have been discharged or their holders consented. The company pays off the loan, gets a release from the bank, and includes it. The Registry then cancels the certificate and notifies Canada. Reason: the rule will not let an aircraft leave the U.S. registry with unresolved recorded rights attached.

Common misreading: People assume that selling the aircraft abroad cancels the U.S. registration by itself, or that the foreign buyer handles the paperwork. This section puts the burden on the certificate holder (or the holder of a filed irrevocable deregistration and export request authorization) to request cancellation and to prove the recorded-rights and priority-interest conditions are met. A second misreading: where a Cape Town Treaty irrevocable deregistration and export request authorization is on file, having a Certificate of Aircraft Registration is not enough on its own, because paragraph (b) says the Registry will honor the request only if an authorized party makes it.
