This section lets the FAA Administrator close off airspace temporarily around an accident, disaster, or other newsworthy event. The closure is announced by a Notice to Airmen (NOTAM), and the NOTAM has to say what hazard or condition caused it. There are three reasons the FAA can issue one: (1) to protect people and property on the ground or in the air from a hazard tied to an incident on the surface; (2) to give disaster relief aircraft a safe place to work; or (3) to stop sightseeing and other aircraft from piling up over something that draws a lot of public interest.

Which reason the NOTAM was issued under determines who may still fly there. If it was issued under (a)(1) — the hazard case — the rule is tightest: nobody may operate an aircraft inside the area unless that aircraft is taking part in the hazard relief activities and is flying under the direction of the official in charge of on-scene emergency response. There is no news exception and no transit exception.

If it was issued under (a)(2) — the disaster relief case — you may fly inside only if at least one of five conditions is met: you are in the relief activity under the direction of the on-scene official; you are carrying law enforcement officials; you are operating on an air traffic control (ATC) approved instrument flight rules (IFR) flight plan; you are going directly to or from an airport inside the area, or visual flight rules (VFR) flight above or around the area is impractical because of weather or terrain, and you notify the Flight Service Station (FSS) or ATC facility named in the NOTAM for disaster relief advisories, and your flight does not hamper or endanger relief work and is not for the purpose of observing the disaster; or you are carrying properly accredited news representatives and, before entering, you file a flight plan with the FAA or ATC facility named in the NOTAM and you stay above the altitude used by the relief aircraft, unless the on-scene official authorizes otherwise.

If it was issued under (a)(3) — the congestion-of-sightseers case — you may fly inside only if at least one of four conditions is met: the operation is directly to or from an airport inside the area, or VFR above or around the area is impractical because of weather or terrain, and it is not for the purpose of observing the incident or event; you are on an ATC approved IFR flight plan; you are carrying incident or event personnel or law enforcement officials; or you are carrying properly accredited news representatives and filed a flight plan beforehand with the FSS or ATC facility named in the NOTAM.

Whenever this section requires a flight plan or a notification to an FSS or ATC facility, it must include the aircraft identification, type and color; the radio communication frequencies to be used; the proposed times of entry into and exit from the designated area; the name of the news media or organization and the purpose of the flight; and anything else ATC asks for.

Illustration: A warehouse fire breaks out two miles from your office. You check NOTAMs and find a temporary flight restriction over the scene, issued to protect persons and property on the surface from the hazard — paragraph (a)(1). A local news station calls and offers to pay you to put your drone up and stream the smoke column. Under this section the answer is no. The (a)(1) case allows only aircraft that are participating in the hazard relief activities and operating under the direction of the official in charge of on-scene emergency response. Carrying or working for news representatives is not one of the ways into an (a)(1) area. If instead the NOTAM had been issued to provide a safe environment for disaster relief aircraft under (a)(2), the news path would exist, but it would require properly accredited news representatives, a flight plan filed with the facility named in the NOTAM before entry, and operating above the altitude the relief aircraft are using unless the on-scene official says otherwise.

Common misreading: People treat the exceptions as one list that applies to every temporary flight restriction. They do not. The exceptions are tied to the reason the NOTAM was issued, and the NOTAM states that reason. The news-representative route and the airport-transit route exist under (a)(2) and (a)(3) but not under (a)(1). A second misreading: the transit allowances in (c)(4) and (d)(1) are not a general permission to fly through for a look — both say the operation must not be for the purpose of observing the disaster, incident, or event, and (c)(4) also requires that it not hamper or endanger relief activities.
