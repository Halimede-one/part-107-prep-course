This section covers how the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) handles an application for an FAA-recognized identification area (FRIA) — a defined area where unmanned aircraft may be flown without broadcasting remote identification. The Administrator reviews the application, and may ask the applicant for more information or documents to fill out the application. The Administrator then either approves or denies it. The chunk lists factors the Administrator may weigh, and says plainly that the list is not exhaustive: (a) whether any existing FAA flight or airspace restriction that limits unmanned aircraft system operations overlaps the proposed area — examples given are special use airspace designated under part 73, temporary flight restrictions issued under part 91, or any other special flight rule, restriction, or regulation in this chapter limiting unmanned aircraft system operations in the interest of safety, efficiency, national security and/or homeland security; (b) safe and efficient use of the airspace by other aircraft; (c) safety and security of people and property on the ground; and (d) whether a FRIA is actually needed where it is proposed, and how close other FRIAs already are. Note what this section does not do: it does not say who may apply, what an application must contain, or how long an approval lasts. Those belong to other sections of part 89 — see those sections.

Illustration: A model aircraft club near a military operations area wants a FRIA at its field. The club submits an application, and the FAA writes back asking for a site map with the exact boundary coordinates — permitted here, since the Administrator may require additional information to supplement an application. On review, the FAA notes the proposed boundary overlaps special use airspace under part 73 that limits unmanned aircraft operations, and that another approved FRIA sits a few miles away. Both are factors the Administrator may consider — the overlap under paragraph (a), the proximity and need under paragraph (d) — and the application is denied. The club's options are to redraw the boundary clear of the restricted airspace and reapply, or to use the nearby existing FRIA.

Common misreading: People read the four listed items as a checklist and assume that an application clearing all four must be approved. The chunk says the Administrator 'may take into consideration matters such as, but not limited to' these — they are examples of concerns, not a complete or binding test. A clean record on (a) through (d) does not guarantee approval.
