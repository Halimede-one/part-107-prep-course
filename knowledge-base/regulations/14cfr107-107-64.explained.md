This section covers the short-lived certificate the FAA (Federal Aviation Administration) can issue while your permanent remote pilot certificate is being processed. A temporary remote pilot certificate with a small UAS (unmanned aircraft system) rating is issued for up to 120 calendar days. During that window, the Administrator reviews whether you are qualified under this part, and if so, a permanent certificate is then issued. The temporary certificate stops being valid at whichever of three things happens: the expiration date printed on the certificate arrives, you receive your permanent certificate, or you receive notice that the certificate you applied for is denied or revoked. So the temporary document is a placeholder, not a second, independent credential.

Illustration: You pass your knowledge test, complete your application, and are handed a temporary remote pilot certificate. Six weeks later the permanent certificate arrives in the mail. You keep flying commercial inspection jobs, but you now carry the permanent certificate, because the temporary one expired the moment you received the permanent one. Tucking the temporary card in your flight bag as a backup is pointless at that point. It no longer has any effect.

Common misreading: People assume the temporary certificate is always good for the full 120 calendar days, as if the date on it were a guarantee. It is not. It is an outer limit of up to 120 calendar days, and the certificate also ends early on receipt of the permanent certificate or on receipt of a notice of denial or revocation, whichever comes first.
