This section is a rule for producers (manufacturers), not for pilots. If a company produces a standard remote identification unmanned aircraft under § 89.510 or § 89.515, or produces a remote identification broadcast module under § 89.520, it must assign that aircraft or module a serial number that complies with ANSI/CTA-2063-A. ANSI stands for American National Standards Institute and CTA stands for Consumer Technology Association. The standard is titled 'Small Unmanned Aerial Systems Serial Numbers (September 2019),' and it is incorporated by reference into this section, which means the standard's text carries the force of the rule even though it is not reprinted in the regulation. The rest of the section tells you where to go read it: the FAA's Office of Rulemaking (ARM-1), 800 Independence Avenue SW, Washington, DC 20590, telephone 202-267-9677; the Consumer Technology Association at 1919 South Eads Street, Arlington, VA 22202, CTA@CTA.tech, 703-907-7600, or https://www.cta.tech; and the National Archives and Records Administration. For the sections cited here (§ 89.510, § 89.515, § 89.520), see those sections. This chunk does not say what the serial number format looks like, where it must be displayed, or what a remote pilot must do with it.

Illustration: A remote pilot buys a new aircraft advertised as a standard remote identification unmanned aircraft and finds a long alphanumeric serial number on the label and in the manufacturer's declaration paperwork. The pilot wonders whether that odd-looking string is a typo. It is not. Under this section the producer was required to assign a serial number complying with ANSI/CTA-2063-A, so the format is set by that incorporated standard rather than by the manufacturer's own numbering preference. The pilot uses the serial number as given when registering the aircraft, because that is the number the producer assigned under this rule.

Common misreading: People read this as a duty on the pilot or owner to generate or verify a compliant serial number. The rule is written against the producer: 'No person may produce... unless the producer assigns.' A remote pilot cannot cure a missing or non-compliant serial number by making one up. Also, the long list of addresses and phone numbers is not a reporting requirement; it is only telling you where copies of the incorporated standard can be inspected or purchased.
