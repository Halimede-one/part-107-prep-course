This part of Advisory Circular (AC) 107-2A explains how you demonstrate aeronautical knowledge for a Remote Pilot Certificate with a small unmanned aircraft system (small UAS) rating. The FAA publishes an Airman Certification Standards (ACS) document that sets the standards for the knowledge test, and the AC points to FAA web pages and to Chapter 2, References, for study material. A note flags that the knowledge test information applies to people who do not already hold a current part 61 airman certificate.

To take the initial test, you schedule an appointment at an FAA-approved knowledge testing center (KTC) and bring proper government-issued photo identification on test day; the AC links a site for finding the nearest KTC. The test covers 13 aeronautical knowledge areas: applicable regulations on small UAS rating privileges, limitations, and flight operation; airspace classification, operating requirements, and flight restrictions; aviation weather sources and weather effects on performance; small unmanned aircraft loading; emergency procedures; crew resource management (CRM); radio communication procedures; determining the performance of the small unmanned aircraft; physiological effects of drugs and alcohol; aeronautical decision-making (ADM) and judgment; airport operations; maintenance and preflight inspection procedures; and operation at night.

A part 61 certificate holder who meets the flight review requirements specified in § 61.56 may complete training instead of taking the knowledge test. That training covers 8 topics: applicable regulations on privileges, limitations, and flight operation; effects of weather on performance; small unmanned aircraft loading; emergency procedures; CRM; determining performance; maintenance and preflight inspection procedures; and operation at night.

After certification, to keep exercising the privileges of the certificate, the holder must successfully complete recurrent training within 24 calendar-months of passing either the initial knowledge test or the initial knowledge training. Figure 6-1 gives three examples of how the cycle works. In the first, an individual passes an initial knowledge test on September 13, 2020, and recurrent training must be completed no later than September 30, 2022, which does not exceed 24 calendar-months. In the second, the individual does not pass recurrent training until October 5, 2020; that person may not exercise the privileges of the Remote Pilot Certificate between October 1, 2020, and October 5, 2020, when the training is completed, and the next recurrent knowledge training must be completed no later than October 31, 2022. In the third, the individual elects to take recurrent training early and completes it on July 15, 2020 (on or prior to September 30, 2020); the next recurrent training must then be completed no later than July 31, 2022.

Illustration: A roof-inspection pilot holds a Remote Pilot Certificate and finished recurrent training early, on July 15, 2020. Looking at Figure 6-1, she marks her next deadline as July 31, 2022, not two years from her original test date. In July 2022 a client asks her to fly a job on August 3. She checks her calendar, sees the July 31, 2022 deadline, and completes recurrent training before the end of July so she can legally fly the August job. Her reason: completing training early moved the next deadline earlier too, and privileges only continue if recurrent training is completed within the 24 calendar-months.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, people read the 24 calendar-months as an exact date-to-date anniversary. Figure 6-1 shows the deadline falling at the end of a month: a test on September 13, 2020 gives a deadline of September 30, 2022. Second, people assume a late completion restores the old cycle and that the lapse is harmless. The figure's second example shows the opposite: the pilot could not exercise the privileges of the certificate between October 1, 2020 and October 5, 2020, and the next deadline shifted to October 31, 2022. Also note that a part 61 holder who meets the § 61.56 flight review requirements does not skip the knowledge requirement entirely — that person completes the listed training instead of the test.
