This section covers one narrow step for holders of a Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate. A dealer certificate lets its holder operate aircraft it holds for sale without registering each one individually. Before the holder actually uses that certificate to operate an aircraft, the holder must send evidence of ownership to the Registry. The rule names the standard of that evidence as the one in § 47.11 — see that section for what qualifies. The chunk gives one acceptable document: an Aircraft Bill of Sale, AC Form 8050-2, or its equivalent. Manufacturers are excepted from this sending requirement. Filing this evidence carries no recording fee.

Illustration: A company that sells aircraft, but does not manufacture them, holds a Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate. It takes a used aircraft into inventory and wants to fly it to a buyer demonstration under the dealer certificate rather than registering the aircraft in the company's name. Before that flight, the company sends the Registry the Aircraft Bill of Sale (AC Form 8050-2) it received from the seller, as evidence of ownership. It submits no payment, because the chunk states there is no recording fee for this filing. If the company were the manufacturer of the aircraft, the parenthetical exception would apply and this sending step would not be required of it.

Common misreading: "There is no recording fee" is sometimes read as meaning the dealer certificate itself, or aircraft registration generally, is free. The chunk says only that this particular submission of ownership evidence carries no recording fee; it says nothing about other fees.
