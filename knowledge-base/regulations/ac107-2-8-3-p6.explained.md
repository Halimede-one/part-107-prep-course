These are two summary tables from the advisory circular showing, for each of the four operations-over-people categories, who the aircraft may fly over. The rows split people into two groups: those directly participating in the operation, and those not directly participating. The columns are Category 1, Category 2, Category 3, and Category 4.

Table for a closed or restricted access site: all four categories are allowed over directly participating people. Over people not directly participating, Category 1 and Category 2 are allowed, Category 3 requires that the people be on notice, and Category 4 depends on operating limitations.

Table for operations not over or within a closed or restricted access site: again all four categories are allowed over directly participating people. Over people not directly participating, Category 1 and Category 2 are allowed, Category 3 is transit only with no sustained flight, and Category 4 depends on operating limitations.

The footnotes apply to both tables and add the same restriction for Categories 1, 2, and 4: the remote pilot may not operate in sustained flight over an open-air assembly unless the operation meets the requirements of section 89.110 or section 89.115(a), and that prohibition can be waived. For Category 3 the footnote is different and harder: a Category 3 eligible small unmanned aircraft must not operate over open-air assemblies of human beings at all, citing section 107.125(b). For Category 4, the footnote says the aircraft may operate over human beings if that is not prohibited by the operating limitations in the FAA-approved Flight Manual or as otherwise specified by the Administrator. Part 89 is the remote identification rule; see those sections for what section 89.110 and section 89.115(a) actually require.

The practical message is that the category of the aircraft, whether the site is closed or restricted, whether the people are participating, and whether the gathering is an open-air assembly all have to be checked together before you fly over anyone.

Illustration: A remote pilot has a Category 3 eligible aircraft and two jobs on the same day. Job one is inside a fenced construction site that the general contractor has closed to the public, and the workers have been told in a morning briefing that a drone will be flying overhead. Under the closed or restricted access site table, Category 3 over people not directly participating is allowed because those workers are on notice, so the pilot can fly the planned grid. Job two is a photo shoot along a downtown sidewalk with pedestrians walking through, not a closed site. Under the second table, Category 3 over people not directly participating is transit only with no sustained flight, so the pilot plans a route that crosses the sidewalk and keeps going rather than hovering above it. If the downtown job had been an outdoor concert crowd, the pilot would decline the Category 3 flight entirely, because a Category 3 eligible aircraft must not operate over open-air assemblies of human beings.

Common misreading: The word "Allowed" in the Category 1 and Category 2 cells gets read as unconditional. It is not. The asterisked footnotes still bar sustained flight over an open-air assembly unless the operation meets section 89.110 or section 89.115(a), and that bar is only removable by waiver. A second misreading is treating Category 3's two tables as interchangeable: a closed or restricted access site lets you fly over non-participants who are on notice, while outside such a site you get transit only with no sustained flight. The site status changes the answer.
