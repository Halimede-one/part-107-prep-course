This section lists the extra paperwork a person must file along with an Aircraft Registration Application, AC Form 8050-1, when their claim to the aircraft comes from something other than a plain purchase from the prior owner. The documents are called "evidence of ownership," and they must be recordable under §§ 49.13 and 49.17 of this chapter — see those sections for what recordable means. The requirement does not apply in the situations covered by §§ 47.33 and 47.35; see those sections. Which document you submit depends on how you came to hold the aircraft. A buyer in possession, bailee, or lessee under a contract of conditional sale submits the contract; an assignee under such a contract submits the contract (unless already recorded at the Registry) plus the assignment from the original buyer, bailee, lessee, or prior assignee. A repossessor submits a Certificate of Repossession of Encumbered Aircraft, FAA Form 8050-4, or its equivalent, signed by the applicant and stating the aircraft was repossessed or seized under the security agreement and applicable local law; plus the security agreement (unless already recorded at the Registry) or a copy certified as true under § 49.21 of this chapter; plus, if repossession went through foreclosure proceedings resulting in a sale, a bill of sale signed by the sheriff, auctioneer, or other authorized person who conducted the sale and stating the sale was made under applicable local law. A buyer at a judicial sale, or at a sale to satisfy a lien or charge, submits that same kind of signed bill of sale. An owner whose title was in controversy and settled by a court submits a certified copy of the court's decision. An executor or administrator of a deceased owner's estate submits a certified copy of the letters testamentary or letters of administration appointing them, and the Certificate of Aircraft Registration is issued to them in that capacity. A buyer from a deceased owner's estate submits both a bill of sale signed for the estate by the executor or administrator and a certified copy of those letters; if no executor or administrator has been or will be appointed, the buyer instead submits a bill of sale signed by the heir-at-law plus an affidavit from the heir-at-law stating that no application for appointment has been made, that so far as he can determine none will be made, and that he is the person entitled to or having the right to dispose of the aircraft under applicable local law. A guardian of another person's property that includes an aircraft submits a certified copy of the court order appointing them, and the certificate is issued to them as guardian. A trustee of property that includes an aircraft, as described in § 47.7(c), submits either a certified copy of the court order appointing the trustee or a complete and true copy of the instrument creating the trust; if there is more than one trustee, each trustee must sign the Aircraft Registration Application, and the certificate is issued to a single applicant as trustee or to several trustees jointly as co-trustees.

Illustration: A survey company's owner dies, and his widow wants to keep operating the company's large unmanned aircraft, which is registered under Part 47. She has been appointed administrator of his estate. To put the aircraft's registration in order, she files an Aircraft Registration Application, AC Form 8050-1, and attaches a certified copy of the letters of administration naming her administrator. Under paragraph (e), that is the evidence of ownership her situation calls for, and the Certificate of Aircraft Registration comes back issued to her as administrator rather than as a private individual. If instead she decided to sell the aircraft to a neighboring survey firm, that buyer would file under paragraph (f) with both a bill of sale she signed for the estate and a certified copy of the same letters.

Common misreading: People assume a bill of sale is always enough. In several of these situations it is not, or not by itself: the estate buyer needs the bill of sale plus the certified letters, the repossessor needs the Form 8050-4 and the security agreement as well, and the conditional-sale assignee needs the contract plus the assignment. Also note these documents are submitted in addition to AC Form 8050-1, not as a substitute for it.
