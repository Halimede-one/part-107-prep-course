This section has two parts. First, nobody may operate a civil small unmanned aircraft system (sUAS) unless it is in a condition for safe operation, and the remote pilot in command (the person directly responsible for and the final authority over the flight) must check the system before each flight to decide whether it is in that condition. The check is required prior to each flight, not once a day or once a week. Second, if a problem shows up after takeoff, the rule bars continuing the flight when the person flying knows, or has reason to know, that the system is no longer in a condition for safe operation. "Has reason to know" covers signs a reasonable pilot would notice even without certainty about the cause.

Illustration: You are flying three roof inspections on the same block. After the first flight you land, swap batteries, and the aircraft lifts off for the second job. Partway through, one motor starts making a grinding sound and the aircraft begins drifting against your inputs. Under paragraph (b) you land as soon as you safely can rather than finishing the roof, because you now have reason to know the system is no longer in a condition for safe operation. Before the third job, paragraph (a) requires a fresh check of the aircraft and system — the pre-flight check applies to that flight too, not just to the first one of the day.

Common misreading: People read this as a one-time or daily airworthiness check. The text says prior to each flight, so every flight gets its own check. The other trap is treating paragraph (a) as the whole duty: even a flight that was safe at takeoff has to stop once you know or have reason to know the system is no longer in a safe condition.
