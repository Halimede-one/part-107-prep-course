This chunk is the body of Advisory Circular (AC) 107-2A paragraph 8.13; the opening words of the sentence were cut off by the page break, so it begins mid-sentence with "remote pilot has additional responsibilities." The point is that flying a small unmanned aircraft over people adds duties on top of everything else part 107 already requires. Three duties are named here. First, the remote pilot must determine that the aircraft is in the appropriate category for the kind of operation being flown. Second, the remote pilot must verify the aircraft is properly labeled and that it is listed on a Declaration of Compliance (DOC) the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) has accepted. Third, the AC says the remote pilot should perform all of the recommended preflight actions for all flights that earlier chapters and appendices of this AC describe. The chunk does not spell out what the categories are or what the labeling must say, so see the relevant sections of part 107 for those details.

Illustration: A remote pilot is asked to film a crowd gathering at a street festival, flying directly above the spectators. Before accepting, the pilot works through 8.13: which category does this operation over people fall into, and is this particular aircraft eligible for that category? The pilot checks the airframe for the manufacturer's label and then confirms the exact model appears on an FAA-accepted DOC. The label is there, but the model is not on an accepted DOC, so the pilot declines to fly over the spectators and instead repositions to shoot from an area clear of people. The reason: verifying the DOC listing is the pilot's own responsibility under 8.13, and without it the over-people operation cannot be justified.

Common misreading: Pilots sometimes assume that because the manufacturer built and marketed the aircraft for flight over people, the category and compliance questions are settled. This paragraph puts both determinations on the remote pilot: the pilot determines the appropriate category and the pilot verifies the labeling and the DOC listing. A second misreading is treating 8.13 as a replacement for ordinary preflight duties; it is in addition to the other operational requirements in part 107, and the AC still expects all the recommended preflight actions from earlier chapters and appendices.
