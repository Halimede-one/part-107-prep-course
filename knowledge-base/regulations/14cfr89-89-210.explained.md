An FAA-recognized identification area (FRIA) is a defined flying site where unmanned aircraft may be flown without broadcasting remote identification. This section covers how a request to establish one is submitted and what paperwork must be in it.

Subsection (a) says an eligible person who wants a FRIA established may submit an application in a form and manner acceptable to the Administrator. The chunk does not spell out who counts as an eligible person; that is set by § 89.205, so see that section.

Subsection (b) lists the nine items a request must contain. All of them are required: the name of the eligible person under § 89.205; the name of the individual making the request on that person's behalf; a declaration that this individual has authority to act for the community-based organization or educational institution; the name and contact information of the primary point of contact for communications with the FAA; the physical address of the proposed area; the location of the proposed area in a form and manner prescribed by the Administrator; a copy of any existing letter of agreement about the flying site, if there is one; a description of the intended purpose of the area and why the proposed area is necessary for that purpose; and any other information the Administrator requires.

Note that items (5) and (6) are separate requirements. A street address is not the same thing as the location data the Administrator prescribes, and both are asked for. Item (7) applies only if a letter of agreement already exists. Item (9) leaves room for the FAA to ask for more.

Illustration: A university's engineering department wants its athletic field designated as a FRIA so student-built aircraft without remote identification can be flown there for coursework. The faculty advisor assembles the request: the institution's legal name, her own name, a signed declaration that she is authorized to act for the university, the department's contact details for FAA correspondence, the field's street address, the location in whatever format the Administrator prescribes, and a written explanation of why the designation is needed for the hands-on build-and-fly curriculum. Because the university already holds a letter of agreement with the nearby airport covering the field, she includes a copy of it under item (7). She does not skip the purpose statement in item (8), since the FAA needs the justification for why the area is necessary for that purpose, not just a description of what the site is.

Common misreading: People read (a) as a right to a FRIA and treat the application as a formality. It is not. The chunk only says an eligible person may submit an application in a form and manner acceptable to the Administrator and lists what must be in it. Approval is a separate matter not covered in this chunk. A second misreading is treating the purpose statement in (8) as optional narrative; it is a required item, and it must address necessity, not just purpose.
