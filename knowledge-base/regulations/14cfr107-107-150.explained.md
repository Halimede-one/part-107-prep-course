This chunk is a short provision about small unmanned aircraft systems (small UAS, meaning the drone plus its control equipment) that can operate in more than one mode or physical configuration. It says such a system may be eligible for one or more categories of operation over human beings under this subpart. The condition attached is a design condition: a remote pilot in command must not be able to inadvertently switch between the modes or configurations. In other words, having two or more qualifying setups is allowed, but the change from one to the other cannot happen by accident. The chunk does not list the categories or state what each one requires, so for those details see the other sections of this subpart. Note that the chunk is one provision and may not be the whole section.

Illustration: A remote pilot owns a drone that the manufacturer offers in two setups: with a propeller guard assembly bolted on, and without it. The manufacturer's documentation shows each setup qualifies for a different category of operation over human beings. Because swapping the guards requires removing hardware with tools on the ground, the pilot cannot flip between the two setups by accident in flight. The pilot installs the guards, confirms which category that configuration corresponds to, and plans the operation to the rules for that category. If instead the aircraft had a bare toggle on the controller that silently changed the configuration mid-flight, that would defeat the condition in this provision, and the pilot should not rely on dual eligibility.

Common misreading: People read this as permission for the pilot to switch categories freely during a flight, as long as the pilot is careful. The provision is about the equipment, not the pilot's attention: the system has to be built so inadvertent switching cannot happen. It also does not say that having two modes automatically makes a drone eligible for two categories; eligibility still comes from meeting the requirements of each category as set out elsewhere in this subpart.
