This chunk continues a sentence that began in the section heading: beginning January 13, 2020, all applicants must establish an FAA Tracking Number (FTN) within IACRA (the Integrated Airman Certification and Rating Application system) before taking any FAA airman knowledge test. The FTN is an identification number that gets printed on your Airman Knowledge Test Report (AKTR) in place of the older Applicant ID number. To get one, go to the IACRA website at https://iacra.faa.gov/iacra/ and follow the instructions there. Once you have the FTN, you register and schedule your FAA Airman Knowledge Test through the website operated by PSI Services LLC at https://faa.psiexams.com/FAA/login. The order matters: IACRA first for the FTN, then PSI to book the test. Two bullets at the end point to an FTN Frequently Asked Questions page and a video about the IACRA registration process, but the web addresses for both were lost in this excerpt.

Illustration: You decide on a Monday to get your remote pilot certificate and call a testing center to book the knowledge test for Saturday. The scheduling site asks for your FTN and you don't have one. Before you can finish booking, you stop, go to https://iacra.faa.gov/iacra/, register, and get your FTN. Then you return to https://faa.psiexams.com/FAA/login and schedule the test. You do it in that order because the chunk says the FTN must be established within IACRA before taking any FAA airman knowledge test, and the scheduling site needs the number to register you.

Common misreading: People assume the FTN is the remote pilot certificate number, or that registering in IACRA is itself an application for the certificate. This chunk only says the FTN is an identification number used for knowledge testing and printed on the Airman Knowledge Test Report in place of the Applicant ID. Another slip is thinking you can book the test first and sort out the FTN later; the chunk puts the FTN before any FAA airman knowledge test.
