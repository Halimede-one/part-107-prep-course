This section sets how long an FAA-recognized identification area (FRIA) lasts and how to keep it alive. A FRIA is a defined location where unmanned aircraft may be flown without broadcasting remote identification. Unless something else in this subpart says otherwise, a FRIA stays in effect for 48 calendar months from the date the FAA approves the request to establish it. It does not renew itself. Anyone who wants to continue the FRIA past that point has to send in a renewal request no later than 120 days before the FRIA expires, using a form and manner the Administrator accepts. If the request comes in after that 120-day deadline, or after the FRIA has already expired, the Administrator may deny it.

Illustration: A model aircraft club's flying field was approved as a FRIA on June 1, 2021, so it is in effect for 48 calendar months. The club secretary marks a calendar reminder for early 2025, because the renewal request has to be submitted no later than 120 days before expiration. She files the renewal in January 2025, inside the window. Had she waited until the month of expiration, the Administrator could have denied the late request, and club members flying aircraft without remote identification broadcast would have lost the location that made those flights permissible.

Common misreading: People read the 120 days as a grace period after expiration. It is the opposite: it is a deadline before expiration. Filing at 90 days out, or after the FRIA has lapsed, gives the Administrator grounds to deny the renewal. Also note the text says the Administrator 'may' deny a late request, not 'will' — but nothing in this chunk entitles you to acceptance of a late filing.
