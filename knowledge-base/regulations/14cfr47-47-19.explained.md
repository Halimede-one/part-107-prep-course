This short section says where your paperwork goes. Anything you send to the Administrator (the Federal Aviation Administration, or FAA) under Part 47 — an application, a submission, a request, a notification, or any other communication — has to be delivered to the Registry. It also has to be delivered by a means and in a form the Administrator finds acceptable, and the section says that includes electronic or digital forms and means, as long as they are compatible with the Registry's systems. In plain terms: send it to the Registry, and send it the way the FAA has set up to receive it. The line 'Subpart B—Certificates of Aircraft Registration' at the end is just a heading for the next group of sections and carries no rule of its own.

Illustration: You register a drone that you will fly for pay and later need to tell the FAA about a change the rules require you to report. You type up a letter and mail it to the local Flight Standards District Office you have dealt with before, because that is the FAA office you know. That is the wrong destination under this section. Communications under this part must be delivered to the Registry, by a means and in a form acceptable to the Administrator. So you resend it through the Registry, using the electronic method the Registry's system accepts, and your filing is properly made.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, people read 'to include electronic or digital forms and means' as a promise that any electronic method works — but the chunk qualifies it: the means must be acceptable to the Administrator and compatible with the Registry's systems. Your own preferred format or channel does not qualify just because it is digital. Second, this section does not tell you what to file, when, or for which aircraft; it only tells you where and in what form. The filing requirements themselves live in other sections of this part.
