This section covers how you actually apply for a remote pilot certificate with a small unmanned aircraft system (small UAS) rating. The application must be made in a form and manner acceptable to the Administrator, and it must include one of two things.

The first path is for people who are not already pilots: evidence that you passed an initial aeronautical knowledge test. If you file a paper application, that evidence has to be an airman knowledge test report showing you passed.

The second path is for people who already hold a pilot certificate issued under part 61 — any pilot certificate other than a student pilot certificate — and who also meet the flight review requirements in § 61.56 (see that section). Those applicants submit a certificate of completion of an initial training course under this part covering the areas of knowledge listed in § 107.74 (see that section) instead of a knowledge test report.

If you use that second path, three extra conditions apply. The application has to go to one of the listed recipients: the responsible Flight Standards office, a designated pilot examiner, an airman certification representative for a pilot school, a certificated flight instructor, or another person authorized by the Administrator. Whoever accepts the submission must verify your identity in a manner acceptable to the Administrator. And the person making the application must show, by logbook endorsement or another acceptable manner, that the applicant meets the § 61.56 flight review requirements.

Illustration: A real estate photographer with no manned-flight background wants to fly drones commercially. She has no part 61 pilot certificate, so paragraph (a)(2) is closed to her. She takes the initial aeronautical knowledge test, and her application includes her passing airman knowledge test report as the evidence required by (a)(1). Her neighbor, a private pilot who is current on his flight review under § 61.56, takes a different route: he completes the initial training course covering the § 107.74 knowledge areas, then brings the completion certificate to a certificated flight instructor, who verifies his identity and endorses his logbook to show he meets the § 61.56 flight review requirement. Both end up with the same certificate; the difference is which paragraph of (a) they satisfied.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, people assume holding any pilot certificate alone lets them skip the knowledge test — but paragraph (a)(2) requires both a part 61 certificate (not a student pilot certificate) and current compliance with the § 61.56 flight review requirements, plus the initial training course completion certificate. Second, people think the training-course route can be mailed in like a test report. It cannot: paragraph (b) requires submission to one of the listed people or offices, in-person identity verification, and a showing of flight review currency.
