This section covers a Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate — the special registration an aircraft dealer uses rather than the standard certificate for a single owned aircraft. Two rules are stated. First, duration: the certificate expires 1 year after the date it is issued. If a dealer holds more than one, each additional certificate expires on the same date the original certificate expires, so they all run out together rather than each getting its own fresh year. Second, reporting: the holder must immediately notify the Registry of a change of name, a change of address, a change that affects status as a citizen of the United States, or the discontinuance of business. "Immediately" is the chunk's own word — no grace period is given. These duties fall on the holder of the dealer's certificate, not on an ordinary aircraft owner.

Illustration: A business that sells small unmanned aircraft holds a Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate issued on March 1 and applies for two additional certificates in September. Those additional certificates do not run until the following September — they expire on the same date as the original, the next March 1. In November the business moves to a new storefront across town. The owner notifies the Registry of the address change right away rather than waiting for renewal, because paragraph (b)(2) requires immediate notice of a change of address.

Common misreading: People assume each additional certificate carries its own full year from its own issue date. It does not — the chunk ties every additional certificate to the expiration date of the original. A second misreading is treating the four notice items as things to mention at renewal time; the chunk says the holder must notify the Registry immediately.
