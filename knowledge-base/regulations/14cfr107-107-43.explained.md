This rule is short and absolute: you may not fly a small unmanned aircraft in a way that gets in the way of operations or traffic patterns at any airport, heliport, or seaplane base. Three things are worth noticing. First, it applies to "any person" operating a small unmanned aircraft, not just certificated remote pilots. Second, it covers three kinds of facilities, not just runway airports: heliports and seaplane bases count too. Third, the chunk sets no distance, altitude, or airspace condition. The test is interference with operations and traffic patterns, so the question is always about the effect of your flight on manned traffic, not about whether you crossed some line on a chart.

Illustration: You are hired to photograph a waterfront restaurant. The restaurant sits beside a seaplane base, and while you set up you watch a floatplane taxi out and lift off along the channel. Your planned orbit at 150 feet would put the aircraft right over that channel on the departure path. Even though nothing in this chunk names a distance, you reposition to shoot from the landward side and keep your flight off the channel, because flying through a floatplane's departure path is exactly the interference with operations the rule forbids. If the shot genuinely required the channel side, you would wait for a gap in floatplane activity and keep a spotter watching the water.

Common misreading: People read "vicinity of airports" in the title and assume this section is about airspace authorization, so if the airport sits in Class G and no authorization is needed, the section does not apply. It still applies. Airspace permission and this interference prohibition are separate requirements, and this chunk names no airspace class, no distance, and no exception. A small uncontrolled airport, a hospital heliport, or a seaplane base with no tower and no controlled airspace is still a place where you may not interfere with traffic patterns.
