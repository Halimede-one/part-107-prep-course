This section defines the role of the remote pilot in command (RPIC) for a small unmanned aircraft system (small UAS). Someone must be designated as RPIC before or during the flight — every flight needs a person holding that role. That person is directly responsible for the operation and is the final authority over it. The RPIC has to make sure the aircraft will pose no undue hazard to other people, other aircraft, or other property if control of the aircraft is lost, for any reason at all. The RPIC must also make sure the operation complies with all applicable regulations of this chapter, and must have the ability to direct the aircraft so that compliance with the applicable provisions of this chapter is actually achievable. Note the wording in (c): the hazard test is about what happens in the event of a loss of control, not only about normal flight.

Illustration: A two-person crew plans to fly a small unmanned aircraft to photograph a building. The certificated remote pilot is designated as RPIC and a second person will handle the camera. The client asks the RPIC to fly a path directly over a crowded sidewalk, arguing that the aircraft has never failed. The RPIC declines that path and repositions to approach the building from over a fenced, empty parking area instead. The reason is paragraph (c): the RPIC must ensure the aircraft poses no undue hazard if control is lost for any reason, and a fly-away or motor failure over the sidewalk would drop the aircraft onto people. The RPIC is also the final authority under (b), so the client's preference does not override that judgment.

Common misreading: People read (a)'s "before or during the flight" as permission to leave the RPIC role unfilled and sort it out later, or to argue after an incident about who was in charge. The point is that the role must be designated and held by someone for the flight, not that designation is optional. A related misreading is treating (b) as if the RPIC's authority were only a formality — it is also where the responsibility lands, including the duties in (c) through (e).
