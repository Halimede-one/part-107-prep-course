This section bans cheating and related misconduct on an FAA (Federal Aviation Administration) knowledge test, and lists what happens if you do it. Paragraph (a) lists seven things an applicant may not do: copy or intentionally remove any knowledge test; give any part or copy of a test to another applicant or receive one from another applicant; give or receive assistance during the period the test is being given; take any part of a test on behalf of another person; be represented by, or represent, another person for a test; use any material or aid during the period the test is being given unless the Administrator specifically authorizes it; and intentionally cause, assist, or participate in any of those acts. Paragraph (b) sets the consequence: if the Administrator finds you committed one of those acts, then for 1 year after the date you committed it you may not apply for any certificate, rating, or authorization issued under this chapter, and you may not apply for or take any test under this chapter. Paragraph (c) adds that any certificate or rating you already hold may be suspended or revoked on the same finding.

Illustration: A test applicant sits down at a testing center and keeps a folded sheet of airspace notes in her pocket, pulling it out partway through the exam. That is using a material or aid during the period the test is being given, which paragraph (a)(6) prohibits unless the Administrator specifically authorized it. If the Administrator finds she did this, she cannot apply for any certificate, rating, or authorization under this chapter, and cannot apply for or take any test under this chapter, for 1 year after the date of the act. If she already held a certificate or rating, it could also be suspended or revoked under paragraph (c).

Common misreading: People read the 1-year period as a waiting time before retaking that one knowledge test. It is broader than that: for 1 year it blocks applying for any certificate, rating, or authorization issued under this chapter, and applying for or taking any test under this chapter. People also assume the 1-year ban is the whole penalty, but paragraph (c) separately allows suspension or revocation of a certificate or rating already held.
