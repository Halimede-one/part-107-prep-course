This chunk summarizes remote identification (remote ID) as it affects Part 107 pilots. The actual rules live in part 89; two other advisory circulars, AC 89-1 (means of compliance process) and AC 89-2 (declaration of compliance process), carry more detail.

After September 16, 2023, most small unmanned aircraft that are registered or required to be registered must comply with remote ID. The serial number of a standard remote identification unmanned aircraft, or of the remote identification broadcast module if one is installed, must be listed on the Certificate of Aircraft Registration. A given serial number may only be listed on one Certificate of Aircraft Registration at a time. You may move a broadcast module from one aircraft operated under part 107 to another, but you must also move the serial number from the first aircraft's certificate to the second aircraft's certificate before you fly. Aircraft that are not required to be registered under part 48, such as one weighing 0.55 pounds or less, still have to comply with remote ID when operated under any operating part for which registration is required. The purpose is to supply location and identity data on small unmanned aircraft in the National Airspace System (NAS), giving the FAA, national security agencies, and law enforcement a way to tell compliant users from those potentially posing a safety or security risk. A list of compliant makes and models will appear at https://www.faa.gov/uas when developed.

A standard remote identification unmanned aircraft broadcasts, over radio frequency (RF) spectrum: the Unmanned Aircraft Identification (either the serial number or a session ID); latitude, longitude, and geometric altitude of both the control station (CS) and the aircraft; the aircraft's velocity (horizontal and vertical speed and direction); a time mark; and an emergency status code (§ 89.110).

If your aircraft is not a standard remote identification unmanned aircraft, you have two choices (§ 89.115). First, equip it with a remote identification broadcast module, either integrated by the manufacturer as an upgrade or retrofit, or a standalone module you secure to the aircraft before takeoff. That module broadcasts the Unmanned Aircraft Identification, which is the aircraft's serial number; latitude, longitude, and geometric altitude of the aircraft; latitude, longitude, and geometric altitude of the takeoff location; velocity; and a time mark. An aircraft using a broadcast module must be operated within visual line of sight (VLOS) (§ 89.115(a)). Second, fly within visual line of sight inside an FAA-recognized identification area (FRIA), which works regardless of the operating part — part 91, part 107, or other. A list of FRIAs will be available at https://www.faa.gov/uas when ready.

Illustration: You own two registered aircraft and one standalone broadcast module. Monday you fly aircraft A for a roof inspection with the module strapped on, and the module's serial number is listed on aircraft A's Certificate of Aircraft Registration. Tuesday the job calls for aircraft B, so you unclip the module and mount it on B. Before you launch, you go back into the registration records and move the module's serial number off aircraft A's certificate and onto aircraft B's certificate, because that serial number can only sit on one certificate at a time and the move has to happen prior to operation. You also plan the Tuesday flight within visual line of sight, since an aircraft relying on a broadcast module must be flown within VLOS.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, people assume the broadcast module's message set matches a standard remote ID aircraft's. It does not: the module broadcasts the takeoff location rather than the control station location, uses the serial number rather than a session ID option, and the chunk does not list an emergency status code for it. Second, people read "0.55 pounds or less means no registration" as "no remote ID." The chunk says the opposite — such an aircraft must still comply with remote ID whenever it is operated under an operating part for which registration is required.
