This section lists what a person must have or do to qualify for a remote pilot certificate with a small unmanned aircraft system (small UAS) rating. There are four requirements. First, be at least 16 years of age. Second, be able to read, speak, write, and understand English — with one exception: if the applicant cannot meet one of those four language abilities for medical reasons, the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) may put operating limitations on the certificate as needed for safe operation. Third, the applicant must not know, or have reason to know, that he or she has a physical or mental condition that would interfere with the safe operation of a small UAS. Fourth, the applicant must demonstrate aeronautical knowledge in a manner acceptable to the Administrator, by one of two routes: pass an initial aeronautical knowledge test covering the areas of knowledge in § 107.73, or — if the person holds a pilot certificate issued under part 61 that is not a student pilot certificate, and meets the flight review requirements of § 61.56 — complete training covering the areas of knowledge in § 107.74. The whole section is also subject to §§ 107.57 and 107.59; see those sections for what they add.

Illustration: A 15-year-old who has studied hard and feels ready wants to take the initial knowledge test and apply for the certificate. Knowledge alone does not satisfy paragraph (a): the age floor is 16, so she is not yet eligible for the certificate. Separately, her uncle holds a current private pilot certificate under part 61 and is current on his flight review under § 61.56. He does not need to sit the initial knowledge test — under paragraph (d)(2) he can instead complete the training covering the areas of knowledge in § 107.74.

Common misreading: Two trip-ups. First, paragraph (c) is not a medical exam requirement — nothing here calls for a medical certificate or a doctor's sign-off. It is a self-assessment standard: you must not know, or have reason to know, of a disqualifying condition. Second, the part 61 training route in (d)(2) is only open to a pilot certificate holder who also meets the § 61.56 flight review requirements, and a student pilot certificate does not count; a lapsed or student-only pilot still goes the initial knowledge test route.
