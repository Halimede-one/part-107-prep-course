This section covers what happens if you say no to an alcohol test or refuse to let the results be shared. Two kinds of refusal are named. The first is refusing to submit to a test that measures the percentage by weight of alcohol in the blood, when a law enforcement officer requests it under § 91.17(c) of this chapter — see that section for what it says about those requests. The second is refusing to furnish, or refusing to authorize the release of, test results that the Administrator requests under § 91.17(c) or (d) — again, see that section. Either refusal is grounds for two possible outcomes. If you have applied for a remote pilot certificate with a small unmanned aircraft system (UAS) rating, your application can be denied for a period of up to 1 year after the date of the refusal. If you already hold a remote pilot certificate with a small UAS rating, it can be suspended or revoked. Note that the refusal itself is the grounds here — this section is about declining the test or the release of results, not about the alcohol level itself.

Illustration: A remote pilot is flying a mapping mission over a construction site. A police officer who has been called to the scene approaches and, citing § 91.17(c), asks the pilot to take a blood alcohol test. The pilot has had nothing to drink and is annoyed at the interruption, so he declines on principle and walks away. Even though he was sober, the refusal is itself grounds under this section for suspension or revocation of his remote pilot certificate with a small UAS rating. Had he been a first-time applicant instead of a certificate holder, that same refusal could support denial of his application for up to 1 year from the date he refused.

Common misreading: People read this as a drinking rule and assume that if they are sober they have nothing to lose by refusing. The chunk makes the refusal itself the grounds for denial, suspension, or revocation, independent of what a test would have shown. The second trap is thinking only the roadside test counts — refusing to furnish or authorize release of results the Administrator requests is listed as a separate refusal with the same consequences.
