This is a one-line applicability statement. It tells you what the subpart it opens is for: it lays out the procedural requirements — the steps and process — for establishing an FAA-recognized identification area. An FAA-recognized identification area is a defined location tied to the remote identification rules in Part 89. This chunk does not say who may apply, what the steps are, or what flying inside such an area allows; those details live in the other sections of the subpart. Read this line only as a signpost: if your question is about how an identification area gets established, this subpart is where to look.

Illustration: A model aircraft club wants to keep flying aircraft that have no remote identification equipment from its field. A member reads this section hoping to find the application steps and finds only a scope statement. The correct move is to keep reading the rest of this subpart, because this line tells her she is in the right place for the procedure but gives her none of it.

Common misreading: People read "prescribes procedural requirements to establish" as if this single sentence created the area or granted permission to fly there. It does neither. It only announces the subject of the subpart that follows.
