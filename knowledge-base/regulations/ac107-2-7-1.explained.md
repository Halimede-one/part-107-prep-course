This is the opening paragraph of the advisory circular's chapter on inspection and maintenance. It tells you two things. First, the requirement it is built on: under section 107.15, the remote pilot in command (remote PIC) has to check the small unmanned aircraft before each flight to decide whether the small unmanned aircraft system (small UAS) is in a condition for safe operation. Second, where to look for help: this chapter gives guidance on how to inspect and maintain a small UAS, and Appendix C, titled Small UAS Maintenance and Inspection Best Practices, holds expanded information and best practices. Note the word 'guidance' — an advisory circular explains how to comply; the requirement itself lives in section 107.15, which you should read directly for its exact wording.

Illustration: You are the remote PIC for a roof survey and you fly the same aircraft three times that morning from the same launch point. Before each of those three flights you run your preflight check, because the rule is tied to each flight, not to each day or each job site. On the third check you notice a propeller with a nick in the leading edge. You replace it before launching, because the check exists to answer one question: is this small UAS in a condition for safe operation? If the answer is no, you do not fly it until it is.

Common misreading: People read Appendix C as a checklist they are required to follow item by item. The chunk calls it expanded information and best practices, and calls this chapter guidance. The binding duty is the one stated from section 107.15 — check the aircraft before each flight and determine it is in a condition for safe operation. Appendix C helps you do that well; it is not itself the requirement.
