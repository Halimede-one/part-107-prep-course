This section bans two things, and it applies to any person, not just the remote pilot in command. Part (a) says no one may operate a small unmanned aircraft system (sUAS, the drone plus its control equipment) in a careless or reckless manner that endangers another person's life or property. Part (b) says no one may allow an object to be dropped from a small unmanned aircraft in a way that creates an undue hazard to persons or property. Note what (b) does not say: it does not forbid dropping objects. It forbids dropping them in a manner that creates an undue hazard. The standard in both parts is about how you fly or how you release, not about a fixed number you can measure.

Illustration: You are hired to drop small sandbags onto a field to mark survey points. The field is fenced and empty, and you fly a tight pattern at low altitude over bare ground with no one inside the fence. That release is planned so nothing falls near a person or anything breakable, so it does not create an undue hazard. Mid-job, a groundskeeper walks in under your flight path and starts mowing. If you keep dropping bags along that same line while he is beneath it, you are now creating an undue hazard under (b) and arguably operating carelessly under (a). The decision is to stop releasing, hold the aircraft clear, and wait until he is out of the drop area or shift your pattern to ground he is not on.

Common misreading: Many people read this section as an outright ban on dropping anything from a drone. It is not. Part (b) prohibits dropping an object in a manner that creates an undue hazard to persons or property, which leaves room for planned, controlled releases over areas where nothing can be hurt. The other common error is assuming only the remote pilot in command can violate this section; the text begins 'No person may,' so it reaches anyone involved, including a crew member who releases the object.
