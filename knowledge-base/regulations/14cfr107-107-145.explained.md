This section governs flying a small unmanned aircraft over a person who is inside a moving vehicle. The default is that you may not do it. You can only do it if you meet the conditions listed here.

First, the operation must qualify under one of the four operations-over-people categories: Category 1 under § 107.110, Category 2 under § 107.115, Category 3 under § 107.125, or Category 4 under § 107.140. See those sections for what each category requires.

Second, if you are flying under Category 1, 2, or 3, you must also satisfy one of two added conditions for the entire operation. Either (1) the aircraft stays within or over a closed- or restricted-access site, and every person inside a moving vehicle at that site has been put on notice that a small unmanned aircraft may fly over them; or (2) the aircraft does not maintain sustained flight over moving vehicles. It is an either/or choice, not both.

Third, if you are flying under Category 4, the aircraft must have an airworthiness certificate issued under part 21, and you must operate it according to the operating limitations in the approved Flight Manual or as otherwise specified by the Administrator. Those operating limitations must not prohibit operations over human beings inside moving vehicles. If the Flight Manual forbids it, you cannot do it.

Illustration: A remote pilot is hired to shoot promotional video of cars on a private test track. The track is fenced, access is controlled, and every driver signs a briefing sheet stating a drone may fly overhead. The pilot's aircraft is Category 2 declared. Because the site is closed-access and all the drivers inside the moving cars are on notice, the pilot may hold position over a car as it passes beneath. Two weeks later the same pilot is asked to film traffic on a public highway overpass with the same aircraft. The highway is not a closed- or restricted-access site, and the drivers have no notice, so the first option in paragraph (b)(1) is unavailable. The pilot may still fly, but only if the aircraft does not maintain sustained flight over the moving vehicles under (b)(2) — so the pilot plans a crossing path where the drone transits over the roadway rather than hovering above or tracking alongside a moving car.

Common misreading: Two traps. One: pilots read this as a separate permission and skip the category requirement. It is not. Paragraph (a) means you still need to qualify under Category 1, 2, 3, or 4 first; this section adds conditions on top of that, it does not replace it. Two: pilots treat the closed-site condition and the no-sustained-flight condition as a combined requirement, or assume a closed site alone is enough. For Categories 1 through 3 you need one or the other, and the closed-site route also requires that the people inside the moving vehicles be on notice — a fence by itself does not satisfy (b)(1).
