This section covers how long an aircraft registration lasts and how to renew it. A Certificate of Aircraft Registration issued under § 47.31 (see that section) expires seven years after the last day of the month it was issued in. So the clock does not start on the day you got the certificate — it starts at the end of that whole month. If your certificate shows an expiration date, you may renew it by sending in an Aircraft Registration Renewal Application, AC Form 8050-1B, along with the fee set by § 47.17 (see that section for the amount). There is a window for doing this: the six months before the certificate's expiration date. Separately, the Administrator (the FAA) can make the owner of a registered aircraft file a complete Aircraft Registration Application, AC Form 8050-1, and pay the fee before the expiration date, if the FAA finds the certificate contains inaccurate information. That is a full application, not the shorter renewal form.

Illustration: You register a drone and the FAA issues the Certificate of Aircraft Registration on March 12, 2026. Under paragraph (a), expiration is counted from the last day of that month — March 31, 2026 — so the certificate expires seven years later, in March 2033. You mark your calendar for the six months before that expiration date, because that is when paragraph (b) lets you file AC Form 8050-1B with the § 47.17 fee. If you had moved and the FAA determined your certificate showed an address or owner name that was no longer accurate, paragraph (c) lets the FAA require you to submit the full AC Form 8050-1 with the fee before the expiration date, instead of simply renewing.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, people count the seven years from the issue date itself; the chunk counts from the last day of the month of issuance. Second, people assume they can renew any time before expiration — the chunk limits renewal applications to the six months preceding the expiration date, and it uses a different form (8050-1B) than the original application (8050-1).
