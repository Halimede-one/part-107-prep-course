This section covers what the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) does after someone submits a declaration of compliance. A declaration of compliance is the paperwork a producer files to state that its product meets the remote identification requirements of this subpart. Two things happen. First, the Administrator reviews the submitted declaration, and may come back and ask for more information or documentation to supplement what was filed. Second, if the Administrator decides the submitter has shown compliance with this subpart's requirements, the FAA notifies the submitter that the declaration has been accepted. So acceptance is an affirmative step the FAA takes and communicates — not something that happens automatically on filing. Note who this section speaks to: it governs the submitter of a declaration (a producer), not the remote pilot flying the aircraft.

Illustration: A small company finishes a remote identification broadcast module and files its declaration of compliance with the FAA. Two weeks later the FAA emails asking for the test records behind one of the claims in the filing. Under paragraph (a), that request is a normal part of the evaluation, and the company sends the records. A month after that, the FAA notifies the company that the Administrator has accepted the declaration. Only at that notification point does the company have an accepted declaration — before it, the filing was under evaluation.

Common misreading: People read submitting a declaration of compliance as the same thing as having one accepted. This chunk separates the two: the FAA evaluates first, may ask for more, and then notifies the submitter of acceptance. A filed-but-not-yet-notified declaration is not an accepted one.
