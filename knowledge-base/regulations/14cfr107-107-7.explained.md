This section covers who has to show what, and to whom, when someone asks. Paragraph (a) applies to a remote pilot in command, an owner, or a person manipulating the flight controls. That person must carry the remote pilot certificate with a small unmanned aircraft system (small UAS) rating plus identification, in physical possession and readily accessible, whenever using the privileges of that certificate. They must present that certificate and identification — identification containing the information listed at § 107.67(b)(1) through (3), see that section — for inspection when asked by the Administrator (the Federal Aviation Administration, or FAA), an authorized representative of the National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB), any Federal, State, or local law enforcement officer, or an authorized representative of the Transportation Security Administration (TSA). They must also make available to the Administrator, on request, any document, record, or report that the regulations of this chapter require be kept. Paragraph (b) widens the list of people: the remote pilot in command, visual observer, owner, operator, or person manipulating the flight controls must, on request, let the Administrator test or inspect the small UAS itself, the remote pilot in command, the person manipulating the controls, and the visual observer if there is one, to determine compliance with this part. Note the request authority in (b) belongs to the Administrator only, not to the broader list in (a)(2). Paragraphs (c) and (d) apply to a different group: anyone holding an FAA-accepted declaration of compliance under subpart D of this part. On request, that person must make the declaration of compliance available to the Administrator along with any other required document, record, or report, and must allow the Administrator to inspect its facilities, technical data, and any manufactured small UAS, and to witness any tests needed to determine compliance with that subpart.

Illustration: You are flying a roof inspection from a parking lot when a city police officer walks over and asks to see your credentials. Your remote pilot certificate is in your wallet in your pocket, along with your driver's license. A local law enforcement officer is on the list in (a)(2)(iii), so you hand both over for inspection. The right decision is to present them, because the rule requires physical possession, ready accessibility, and presentation on request from that officer. If instead the certificate were at home in a drawer and you only had a photo of it on your phone, you would not have it in your physical possession as (a)(1) requires.

Common misreading: Two things get mixed up. First, people assume the four requesters in (a)(2) can also demand a test or inspection of the aircraft. They cannot under this section — paragraph (b) names only the Administrator for tests and inspections, while the police officer, NTSB, and TSA representatives in (a)(2) are listed for certificate and identification inspection. Second, people read paragraphs (c) and (d) as applying to every remote pilot. They apply only to a person holding an FAA-accepted declaration of compliance under subpart D.
