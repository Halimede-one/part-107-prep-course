This section sets out who may register an aircraft in the United States and the rule that an eligible aircraft generally cannot be operated unless it has been registered.

Paragraph (a) lists the eligibility conditions. An aircraft can be registered under 49 U.S.C. 44103 only if it is not already registered under the laws of a foreign country, and it falls into one of four ownership categories: owned by a citizen of the United States; owned by an individual citizen of a foreign country who has been lawfully admitted for permanent residence in the United States; owned by a corporation that is not a U.S. citizen but is organized and doing business under the laws of the United States or of a State within the United States, where the aircraft is based and primarily used in the United States; or an aircraft of the United States Government, or of a State, the District of Columbia, a territory or possession of the United States, or a political subdivision of a State, territory, or possession.

Paragraph (b) is the operating prohibition. If an aircraft is eligible for registration under 49 U.S.C. 44101-44104, no person may operate it unless one of three things is true: the owner has registered it; it is carrying aboard the temporary authorization required by § 47.31(c) (see that section for what that authorization is); or it is an aircraft of the Armed Forces of the United States.

Paragraph (c) clarifies that "governmental units" means the government entities named in paragraph (a) plus Puerto Rico.

Note the structure: paragraph (a) is about who is allowed to register, and paragraph (b) is about not flying until registration (or a listed alternative) is in place.

Illustration: A drone owner holds a green card and lives in Ohio as a lawful permanent resident, but is a citizen of another country. She wants to register her aircraft. Under paragraph (a)(2), an individual citizen of a foreign country who has been lawfully admitted for permanent residence in the United States is an eligible owner, so she qualifies on that basis — provided the aircraft is not registered under the laws of a foreign country. Before she flies it, paragraph (b) requires that the aircraft have been registered by its owner, or be carrying the temporary authorization required by § 47.31(c). She waits for registration to be completed rather than flying on the assumption that having applied is enough.

Common misreading: Two common misreadings. First, people assume only U.S. citizens can register an aircraft; paragraph (a) also covers lawful permanent residents, certain non-citizen corporations organized and doing business under U.S. or State law whose aircraft is based and primarily used in the United States, and government aircraft. Second, people read paragraph (b) as a rule for owners only. It says "no person may operate," so the prohibition reaches whoever is flying the aircraft, not just the person whose name is on it.
