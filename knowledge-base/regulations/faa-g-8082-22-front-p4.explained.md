This is front matter — the end of the table of contents, the Introduction, and contact/reference information. It contains no operating rule, but the Introduction does carry one piece of useful content: the list of knowledge areas covered on the airman knowledge test for a Remote Pilot Certificate with a Small Unmanned Aircraft Systems Rating, which the study guide says are required by Title 14 of the Code of Federal Regulations (14 CFR) part 107, section 107.73(a). The twelve areas are: applicable regulations relating to small unmanned aircraft system rating privileges, limitations, and flight operation; airspace classification, operating requirements, and flight restrictions affecting small unmanned aircraft operation; aviation weather sources and effects of weather on small unmanned aircraft performance; small unmanned aircraft loading; emergency procedures; crew resource management; radio communication procedures; determining the performance of small unmanned aircraft; physiological effects of drugs and alcohol; aeronautical decision-making and judgment; airport operations; and maintenance and preflight inspection procedures. The chunk also lists how to reach the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA): 1-866-TELL-FAA (1-866-835-5322) toll-free 24 hours a day, the website www.faa.gov, e-mail to a representative at a local Flight Standards District Office (FSDO) through the "Contact FAA" link at the bottom of the FAA home page, or mail to Federal Aviation Administration, 800 Independence Ave, SW, Washington, DC 20591. Finally, it names reference material available to pilots, almost all of it downloadable at www.faa.gov: the Aeronautical Information Manual (AIM), handbooks, Advisory Circulars (ACs), the Airman Certification Standards, and 14 CFR part 107. It notes that commercial aviation publishers also offer reference material.

Illustration: A student building a study plan reads this list and turns it into a checklist of twelve topics, then notices that weather, loading, and performance are three separate areas rather than one. She allots study time to each instead of assuming the test is mostly regulations, and she downloads the AIM and part 107 from www.faa.gov as her primary references.

Common misreading: The twelve knowledge areas are the subjects the test draws from, not a promise that this study guide is the only material needed — the chunk itself points to the AIM, handbooks, Advisory Circulars, the Airman Certification Standards, and 14 CFR part 107 as additional references. Also, this list describes test content; it is not a list of operating requirements for a flight.
