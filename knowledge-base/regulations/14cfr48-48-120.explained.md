This section lists four situations that make a small unmanned aircraft registration invalid from the start. The test is applied as of the moment the registration was made, not later. A registration is invalid if: (a) the aircraft is already registered in a foreign country; (b) the person applying is not the owner — with one exception, where an applicant may register on behalf of an owner who is under 13 years of age; (c) the applicant is not eligible to apply under this part (see the eligibility provisions of this part); or (d) the applicant's interest in the aircraft came from a transaction that was not made in good faith, but was instead set up to avoid compliance with 49 U.S.C. 44101-44103, whether or not the owner knew about it. 'Invalid' means the registration never had legal effect — so a certificate in hand does not mean the aircraft is validly registered if one of these conditions applied when the application was filed.

Illustration: A club member buys a drone and asks a friend to register it in the friend's name because the friend already has an account set up. The friend is not the owner, and the owner is an adult, so the under-13 exception in paragraph (b) does not apply. If the friend registers it anyway, that registration is invalid under paragraph (b) — the aircraft is effectively unregistered even though a certificate was issued. The correct move is for the actual owner to register the aircraft in their own name.

Common misreading: People read the under-13 exception as a general permission for someone else to handle registration on an owner's behalf. It is narrow: the chunk allows an applicant who is not the owner only when the owner is under 13 years of age. For any other owner, an application by a non-owner makes the registration invalid.
