This chunk tells you where Part 107 stops applying. Part 107 covers civil small unmanned aircraft system (UAS) operations flown inside the National Airspace System (NAS). The chunk then lists eight categories that Part 107 does not cover: limited recreational operations of UAS flown in accordance with Title 49 of the United States Code (49 U.S.C.) section 44809; operations conducted outside the United States; amateur rockets; moored balloons; unmanned free balloons; kites; public aircraft operations; and air carrier operations. Note what being outside Part 107 means and does not mean. The chunk only says Part 107 does not apply. It does not say these activities are unregulated, and it does not describe what rules do apply instead. For recreational flying, the chunk points to 49 U.S.C. section 44809 — see that statute for its conditions. For public aircraft operations and air carrier operations, the chunk says nothing about what governs them, so do not assume.

Illustration: You hold a remote pilot certificate and fly a 2 lb quadcopter. On Saturday you fly it at a park purely for fun. On Monday your employer pays you to photograph a roof with the same aircraft. The Monday flight is a civil small UAS operation in the NAS, so Part 107 applies and you operate under your certificate and its rules. For the Saturday flight you have a choice: you can still operate under Part 107, or you can fly as a limited recreational operation under 49 U.S.C. section 44809, in which case this chunk says Part 107 does not apply. You decide per flight, before you launch, because the two paths carry different conditions — and the chunk does not tell you what the section 44809 conditions are, so you would read that statute.

Common misreading: The biggest trap is reading 'part 107 does not apply' as 'no rules apply.' It means only that this particular rule set is not the governing one; something else may govern instead, and the chunk does not say what. A second trap is thinking you can drift between recreational and Part 107 status mid-flight or decide afterward which one you were under — the exclusion is tied to whether the operation is conducted in accordance with section 44809, which is a choice you make for the operation. A third trap is assuming 'public aircraft operations' means any government-related flight; the chunk gives no definition of that term, so do not supply one.
