This paragraph lists who may apply for a Remote Pilot Certificate with a small unmanned aircraft system (small UAS) rating, drawing on the requirements of section 107.61. An applicant must be at least 16 years of age; be able to read, speak, write, and understand the English language; and be in a physical and mental condition that would not interfere with the safe operation of a small UAS. The applicant must also pass the initial aeronautical knowledge test at an FAA-approved Knowledge Testing Center (KTC). There is one alternate path: a person who already holds a pilot certificate issued under 14 CFR part 61 (other than a student pilot certificate) and who meets the flight review requirements of section 61.56 only has to successfully complete a part 107 online training course, available at https://www.faasafety.gov. The chunk points to section 107.17 for small UAS operating prohibitions that apply to an individual with known medical conditions, and to paragraphs 6.7 and 6.8 for more on knowledge tests and training — see those sections for their content.

Illustration: A private pilot holds a certificate issued under part 61 and completed a flight review eight months ago, so she meets the flight review requirements of section 61.56. She wants to start flying a small drone commercially. Rather than schedule a test at a Knowledge Testing Center, she logs into faasafety.gov and completes the part 107 online training, because the chunk lets a current part 61 certificate holder substitute that training for the initial aeronautical knowledge test. Her co-worker, who has never held a pilot certificate, does not qualify for the online path and books a seat at a KTC for the initial knowledge test instead.

Common misreading: Two traps here. First, the online-training shortcut is not open to anyone who has ever taken a flight lesson — it requires an actual part 61 pilot certificate other than a student pilot certificate, plus meeting the section 61.56 flight review requirements. Second, "physical and mental condition that would not interfere with safe operation" is not satisfied by holding a medical certificate, and holding no medical certificate does not excuse it; it is a self-assessment standard, and the note sends you to section 107.17 for how known medical conditions affect operating.
