This is the opening "applicability" section of Part 47 — it tells you what the rest of the part covers rather than imposing a duty on you directly. Part 47 sets out the requirements for registering aircraft under the statute at 49 U.S.C. 44101-44104. Within the part, Subpart B covers anyone applying for or holding a Certificate of Aircraft Registration, and Subpart C covers anyone applying for or holding a Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate. The chunk also makes clear that Part 47 governs how the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) issues those certificates no matter the channel used — by mail, electronically, or by other means. For the specific requirements in Subparts B and C, see those subparts; this section does not state them.

Illustration: You register a drone through the FAA's online system and get back a Certificate of Aircraft Registration. You wonder whether the Part 47 rules really apply to you, since you never mailed in a paper form. They do: this section says Part 47 applies to the FAA's issuance of such certificates "whether by mail, electronic, or other means." Because you now hold a Certificate of Aircraft Registration, you are a holder under Subpart B, and Subpart B's requirements apply to you — you would look to that subpart to find out what they are.

Common misreading: People read a section like this as if it contained registration rules, then go looking for weight thresholds or renewal periods in it. It contains none. It only maps out which subpart applies to which kind of applicant or certificate holder, and confirms that electronic issuance counts the same as paper.
