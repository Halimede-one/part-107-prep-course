If you hold a Certificate of Aircraft Registration for a small unmanned aircraft, you are responsible for keeping the registration information current. The information in question is whatever you supplied under § 48.110 — see that section for the list. Two things trigger an update, and both must be done through the web-based small unmanned aircraft registration system within 14 calendar days: (1) any change in that information, and (2) any reason the registration has to be cancelled, including sale or transfer of the aircraft, its destruction, or its export. Note the clock is 14 calendar days, not business days, so weekends and holidays count.

Illustration: You registered your drone at your old apartment. On March 1 you move across town. Under this section, you log into the web-based registration system and update your address by March 15 — within 14 calendar days of the change. Later that year you sell the same drone to a friend. That sale is a cancellation event, so you go back into the system and update the registration within 14 calendar days of the sale rather than simply leaving the old record standing.

Common misreading: People often assume this only covers address changes, or that crashing or selling a drone means the record can just be forgotten. The rule treats cancellation events — sale or transfer, destruction, export — as requiring an affirmative update in the system within the same 14 calendar days, and the duty sits with the certificate holder, not the buyer.
