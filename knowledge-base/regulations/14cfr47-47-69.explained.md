This section sets the limits on a Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate — the special registration a dealer uses instead of registering each aircraft it holds for sale. The certificate only covers use of the aircraft when all of these conditions are met. First, on who may use it: the owner the certificate was issued to, that owner's agent or employee, or a prospective buyer. If the dealer is not a manufacturer, the dealer must first have complied with § 47.67 (see that section for what that requires). Second, on where: inside the United States, with one exception — it may be used to deliver an aircraft to a foreign purchaser if the aircraft displays a temporary registration number and carries an airworthiness certificate with that number written on it. Third, a certificate must be carried within the aircraft during the flight. Fourth, on purpose: the flight must be either required flight testing of the aircraft, or necessary for or incident to the sale of the aircraft. There is one added restriction at the end: a prospective buyer may fly the aircraft for demonstration purposes only under the direct supervision of the certificate holder or the holder's agent.

Illustration: A dealer holds several aircraft in inventory under a Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate. A buyer visiting from out of state wants to fly one himself before deciding. The dealer may allow it — a demonstration flight is incident to the sale, and a prospective buyer is among those the certificate covers — but only if the dealer or the dealer's agent directly supervises the buyer during the flight, the certificate is carried in the aircraft, and the flight stays within the United States. If the dealer simply hands over the keys and stays at the office, the flight falls outside what the certificate authorizes.

Common misreading: People read a dealer certificate as a general-purpose registration that lets anyone fly the aircraft for any reason. It is not. Each of the four conditions must hold at once, and the purpose test is narrow: required flight testing, or a flight necessary for or incident to the sale. A pleasure flight, a cross-country trip for the dealer's own convenience, or a buyer flying solo without direct supervision are not covered.
