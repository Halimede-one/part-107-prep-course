This piece of AC 107-2A describes the security side of getting a Remote Pilot Certificate. Once the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) has your application, the Transportation Security Administration (TSA) runs a security vetting of you before any temporary certificate is issued. Three outcomes follow from that vetting. If it succeeds, the FAA issues a permanent Remote Pilot Certificate. If it does not succeed, you are disqualified and you get neither a temporary nor a permanent certificate. And if you think you failed the security threat assessment improperly, you can appeal that decision to the TSA — the appeal goes to TSA, not to the FAA.

Illustration: A pilot passes the aeronautical knowledge test and submits her application. Weeks pass with no temporary certificate in hand, and she is asked to fly a paid roof inspection. She declines the job. Her application is still sitting in TSA vetting, and until that vetting comes back successful no temporary or permanent certificate exists for her to fly under. When the vetting clears, the FAA issues her permanent certificate and she takes the job. Had the vetting come back unsuccessful, she would have been disqualified, and her next step would be an appeal to the TSA rather than a call to the FAA.

Common misreading: People assume passing the knowledge test is the whole gate and that a certificate is now just paperwork in transit. It is not — the TSA vetting is a separate step that must succeed first, and an unsuccessful result means no certificate at all, temporary or permanent.
