This section is the subject outline. It lists the thirteen areas of knowledge that both the initial aeronautical knowledge test and the recurrent training cover. It tells you what you will be tested on and later retrained on, not how to pass or when to retest. The topics are: (a) the regulations that govern small unmanned aircraft system (sUAS) rating privileges, limitations, and flight operation; (b) airspace classification, operating requirements, and flight restrictions affecting small unmanned aircraft operation; (c) aviation weather sources and how weather affects small unmanned aircraft performance; (d) small unmanned aircraft loading; (e) emergency procedures; (f) crew resource management; (g) radio communication procedures; (h) determining the performance of the small unmanned aircraft; (i) the physiological effects of drugs and alcohol; (j) aeronautical decision-making and judgment; (k) airport operations; (l) maintenance and preflight inspection procedures; and (m) operation at night. Note that the same list applies to both the initial test and recurrent training, so the content does not shrink when you move from testing to refresher training. The chunk does not say who must take the test, where it is taken, or how often recurrent training is required; for those points see the sections that cover them.

Illustration: A photographer studying for the initial aeronautical knowledge test decides to skip the chapter on crew resource management because she plans to always fly alone. That is a poor study plan: crew resource management is listed in paragraph (f) as one of the knowledge areas the test covers, so questions drawn from it can appear regardless of how she intends to operate. The same reasoning applies to paragraph (m), operation at night, even if she never intends to fly after dark — if it is on the list, it is fair game on the test and in recurrent training.

Common misreading: People read this list as the full set of rules they must follow, and assume anything not listed here does not apply to them. It is a syllabus of knowledge areas, not a statement of operating limits. Conversely, some assume recurrent training is a lighter, shorter subset of the initial test topics; the chunk applies the same thirteen areas to both.
