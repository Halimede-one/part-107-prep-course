This section sets out who is allowed to fly a small unmanned aircraft system (small UAS, meaning the drone plus its control equipment) and who is allowed to be the remote pilot in command (the person responsible for the flight).

Paragraph (a) covers anyone who touches the flight controls. That person must either (1) hold a remote pilot certificate with a small UAS rating, issued under subpart C of this part, and meet the requirements of § 107.65 (see that section), or (2) be under the direct supervision of a remote pilot in command who can immediately take direct control of the aircraft. So an uncertificated person can legally manipulate the controls, but only with that supervision and that ability to take over at once.

Paragraph (b) is stricter about the role of remote pilot in command. There is no supervision option there. To act as remote pilot in command, the person must personally hold the remote pilot certificate with a small UAS rating issued under subpart C and satisfy § 107.65.

Paragraph (c) is the exception referenced at the start of both (a) and (b). The Administrator may, consistent with international standards, authorize an airman to operate a civil foreign-registered small unmanned aircraft without an FAA-issued remote pilot certificate with a small UAS rating.

Illustration: A certificated remote pilot is hired to shoot footage for a real estate client. The client's marketing employee asks to fly a few shots herself because she knows the look she wants. The remote pilot sets up a controller with a trainer link so he can take over instantly, stands beside her, and keeps watch. Under paragraph (a)(2) this is legal: she is manipulating the controls while under his direct supervision, and he has the ability to immediately take direct control. He remains the remote pilot in command for the whole flight, because under paragraph (b) only a certificate holder who satisfies § 107.65 can hold that role. If his setup did not let him take over immediately, he would have to fly it himself.

Common misreading: People read paragraph (a)(2) as a general permission for an unlicensed person to 'fly under someone's license,' as if being nearby were enough. It is not. The rule requires direct supervision plus the remote pilot in command's ability to immediately take direct control of the flight. And that permission never extends to the remote pilot in command role itself; paragraph (b) has no supervision alternative.
