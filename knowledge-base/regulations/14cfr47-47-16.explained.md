This section covers temporary registration numbers, which the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) issues only to manufacturers, distributors, and dealers who hold a Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate. The numbers are for temporary display on an aircraft during flight allowed under Subpart C of Part 47. A certificate holder asks the Registry in writing for as many numbers as the business needs, and the request must include enough information to justify the need plus the number of each Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate the applicant holds. The numbers cost nothing.

Use is limited. A number may be used and reused only in connection with the holder's Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate, within the limitations of § 47.69 where applicable, including the requirements of § 47.67 (see those sections), and only on aircraft that are not registered under Subpart B of this part or in a foreign country and that display no other identification markings. One temporary number may not be on more than one aircraft in flight at the same time. These numbers may not be used to fly an aircraft into the United States for importation.

The assignment of a number lapses when all of the holder's Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificates expire. If the number is used on a delivery flight outside the United States, the holder must record that the number was assigned to that aircraft and keep the record for at least 1 year after the number is removed from the aircraft. An owner applying for an airworthiness certificate under Part 21 while the aircraft bears a temporary number must furnish that number in the application. The number must come off the aircraft no later than the date title or possession passes to another person.

Illustration: A distributor holds a Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate and has been assigned three temporary registration numbers. Two aircraft are scheduled for demonstration flights the same morning, and a third needs a delivery flight that afternoon. The operations clerk wants to put the same temporary number on all three because paperwork for that number is already filed. That is not allowed for the two morning flights: a temporary registration number may not be used on more than one aircraft in flight at the same time, so each aircraft airborne at that moment needs its own assigned number. The afternoon flight is a different matter — once the morning aircraft is on the ground, the number is reusable, because the rule says these numbers may be used and reused. For the delivery flight, which leaves the United States, the holder writes down that the number was assigned to that aircraft and files the record, keeping it at least 1 year after the number is taken off.

Common misreading: People read this as a general permission slip to fly an unregistered aircraft. It is not. Temporary registration numbers exist only for holders of Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificates, only in connection with that certificate, and only for flight allowed under Subpart C. Two other limits get missed: the aircraft must display no other identification markings and must not already be registered under Subpart B or in a foreign country, and the number cannot be used to fly an aircraft into the United States for importation. Also note the removal deadline is tied to transfer, not to a calendar period — the number comes off no later than the date title or possession passes to someone else, even if the dealer's certificate is still valid.
