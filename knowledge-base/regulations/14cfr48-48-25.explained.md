This section says who may register a small unmanned aircraft and how. To register under Part 48, a person submits the information required by § 48.110 (see that section for what that information is) to the Registry, in the form and manner the Administrator prescribes. Once that information is submitted, the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) issues a Certificate of Aircraft Registration to that person. The aircraft must be registered using the owner's legal name — not a nickname, club name, or handle. There is one exception tied to age: if the owner is less than 13 years of age, someone who is at least 13 years of age must do the registering. The section also makes two clarifications. First, under 49 U.S.C. 44103(c), a registration is not proof of who owns the aircraft if ownership is ever disputed in a proceeding. Second, 'owner' in Part 48 is broader than just a purchaser who paid cash: it includes a buyer in possession, a bailee (someone holding the aircraft for another), a lessee under a contract of conditional sale, and the assignee of any of those people.

Illustration: An 11-year-old receives a small drone as a birthday gift and wants it registered. Because the owner is under 13, the child cannot be the registrant. The child's mother registers the aircraft, supplying the information required by § 48.110 to the Registry under her own legal name, and the FAA issues the Certificate of Aircraft Registration to her. If the child were 13 or older, the child could register it in the child's own legal name.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, people treat the Certificate of Aircraft Registration as a title or proof of ownership — paragraph (c) says plainly it is not evidence of ownership in a dispute. Second, people assume only the person who bought the aircraft can register it; paragraph (d) defines 'owner' to also cover a buyer in possession, a bailee, a lessee under a contract of conditional sale, and an assignee of such a person.
