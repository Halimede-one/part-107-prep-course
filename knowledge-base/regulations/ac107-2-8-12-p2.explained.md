This part of Advisory Circular (AC) 107-2A explains what the manufacturer's "remote pilot operating instructions" are supposed to do, and then shows a made-up example of one. The instructions have to be detailed enough that a remote pilot can clearly understand how to set up the small unmanned aircraft so it complies with the applicable requirements for operating over people. Their job is to inform the pilot and support good decisions. But the AC is explicit that the instructions do not take over the pilot's judgment: it is ultimately the remote pilot in command (PIC) who must decide the safe operational parameters for a given operation. Figure 8-2 is a sample only, illustrating the kind of content a manufacturer might include — a table of contents, an introduction, general information, a system description, required components, and optional components. In the sample, the aircraft has three flight modes selected by a switch above the left control stick: Flight Mode G for any operation not conducted over people, Flight Mode 2 for operations in accordance with Category 2 over people, and Flight Mode 3 for operations in accordance with Category 3 over people. The sample also lists different required component sets for Category 2 and Category 3 (including different batteries and different rotor blades and guards), warns that modifications not provided for in the instructions may affect eligibility to operate over people, says the camera may be replaced only with a pre-approved camera listed in the instructions, says no other payload may be affixed unless it is listed in the instructions, and says any permissible payload must stay securely attached throughout the whole Category 2 or Category 3 operation. Failing to follow those requirements makes the aircraft ineligible to operate over people under Category 2 or Category 3.

Illustration: You fly a Category 2/Category 3 eligible aircraft similar to the sample. A real estate client asks for overhead shots of an open house with guests walking the yard. You check the manufacturer's remote pilot operating instructions and see that your current setup — the larger battery and CAT 3 rotor blades and guards — is the Category 3 configuration, and you set the flight mode switch to Flight Mode 3 accordingly. The client also hands you a small aftermarket spotlight and asks you to mount it. You look it up: it is not listed in the operating instructions as a permissible payload, so you decline to mount it, because attaching an unlisted payload would make the aircraft ineligible to operate over people in Category 2 or Category 3. You also confirm the camera on board is the one that came with the aircraft. Even with the configuration correct, you still walk the site and decide where you will and will not fly — that judgment is yours as remote PIC, not the manual's.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, people read Figure 8-2 as if it were the rule: it is a sample document, so the specific flight mode letters, battery sizes, and component lists are illustrative, not FAA-mandated specifications. Your own aircraft's instructions govern your aircraft. Second, people treat the manual as a shield — "I followed the instructions, so the operation was legal and safe." The AC says the opposite: the instructions aid the pilot, but determining safe operational parameters remains the remote PIC's responsibility.
