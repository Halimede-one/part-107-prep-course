This paragraph covers flying a small unmanned aircraft over an open-air assembly of persons — a crowd gathered outdoors. Under the operations over people categories, a Category 1, 2, or 4 aircraft may not be flown in sustained flight over an open-air assembly unless the operation meets the requirements of 14 CFR part 89, section 89.110 or section 89.115(a). Part 89 is the remote identification rule; this chunk does not spell out what those two sections require, so see those sections. Category 3 operations are never allowed over an open-air assembly of persons, with no exception offered here. The chunk also points you to paragraphs 8.3.2 and 8.3.6.4 of the advisory circular for more on what counts as an open-air assembly; it does not define the term here.

Illustration: You are hired to shoot aerial video of an outdoor music festival with several thousand people packed into a field. Your aircraft is eligible for Category 2 operations over people. Before you accept the job, you check whether your operation meets the requirements of part 89, section 89.110 or section 89.115(a). If it does not, you cannot fly in sustained flight over that crowd — the Category 2 eligibility alone is not enough once an open-air assembly is involved. If your aircraft were only eligible for Category 3, the answer would be no regardless, because Category 3 operations are not allowed over an open-air assembly at all.

Common misreading: The common mistake is assuming that an aircraft eligible to fly over people is automatically cleared to fly over a crowd. Open-air assemblies are treated separately: Categories 1, 2, and 4 need the part 89 section 89.110 or 89.115(a) requirements satisfied, and Category 3 is excluded outright.
