This section sets two different sets of conditions for Category 2 operations. Paragraph (a) is about the aircraft itself: to be eligible for Category 2, the small unmanned aircraft must be designed, produced, or modified so that it (1) will not cause injury to a human being equivalent to or greater than the severity of injury caused by a transfer of 11 foot-pounds of kinetic energy on impact from a rigid object, (2) has no exposed rotating parts that would lacerate human skin on impact with a person, and (3) has no safety defects. Paragraph (b) is about the applicant for a declaration of compliance — the person or company making the declaration, not the remote pilot. That applicant has three obligations. First, a label on the aircraft stating it is eligible for Category 2 operations; the label must be in English, legible, prominent, and permanently affixed. Second, remote pilot operating instructions, which the applicant must make available when the aircraft is sold or transferred, or when someone other than the applicant uses it. Those instructions must cover at least a system description (required components, system limitations, and the declared category or categories), which modifications will not change the system's ability to meet the requirements for its eligible category or categories, and how to verify and change the mode or configuration if those are variable. Third, a product support and notification process so the applicant can notify both the public and the FAA (Federal Aviation Administration) of any defect or condition that makes the aircraft no longer meet this subpart's requirements, and of any identified safety defect that causes the aircraft to exceed a low probability of casualty. The declaration of compliance itself is submitted under § 107.160 — see that section.

Illustration: You are buying a small drone from a reseller because you want to fly it over a crowd at an outdoor market. The listing says "Category 2 capable." When the box arrives, the aircraft has a peel-off sticker that says Category 2, and no operating instructions came with it. You contact the manufacturer and they cannot produce remote pilot operating instructions. Decision: you do not treat this aircraft as Category 2. Paragraph (b)(1) requires the Category 2 eligibility label to be permanently affixed, and (b)(2) requires the applicant for the declaration of compliance to make the remote pilot operating instructions available on sale or transfer. A removable sticker and missing instructions mean the applicant's requirements in this section are not satisfied, so the aircraft is not shown to be usable in Category 2 operations.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, people read the 11 foot-pounds figure as a limit on how much kinetic energy the drone may carry, or as a weight limit. It is neither — it is a severity-of-injury benchmark: the aircraft must not cause injury equivalent to or greater than what a transfer of 11 foot-pounds of kinetic energy from a rigid object would cause. Second, people assume paragraph (b) is a pilot checklist. The labeling, instructions, and product support duties fall on the applicant for the declaration of compliance, not on the remote pilot. The pilot's practical job is to confirm the aircraft actually carries that permanent label and that the instructions exist.
