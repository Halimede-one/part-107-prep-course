This section sets the boundaries of Part 107 — who and what the rule covers. Paragraph (a) says Part 107 governs three things for civil small unmanned aircraft systems (small UAS, meaning the drone plus its control equipment) operated within the United States: registration, airman certification (the remote pilot certificate), and the operation itself. It also covers whether a civil small UAS is eligible to operate over human beings in the United States.

Paragraph (b) lists four categories that Part 107 does not apply to. First, air carrier operations. Second, any aircraft subject to 49 U.S.C. 44809 — see that statute for what it covers. Third, any operation that the holder of an exemption under section 333 of Public Law 112-95 or 49 U.S.C. 44807 chooses to conduct under that exemption, unless the exemption itself says otherwise. Fourth, any operation a person chooses to conduct under part 91 of this chapter using a small UAS that has been issued an airworthiness certificate.

Note the word "civil" in paragraph (a) and the word "elects" in paragraphs (b)(3) and (b)(4). In those two cases the operator makes a choice about which rule set to fly under, and that choice is what removes the operation from Part 107.

Illustration: A company holds an exemption under 49 U.S.C. 44807 that lets it fly a small unmanned aircraft beyond what Part 107 would allow. For a particular job, the chief pilot decides to conduct the flight under the terms of that exemption. Because of paragraph (b)(3), Part 107 does not apply to that operation — unless the exemption document itself specifies that some Part 107 provisions still do. The next week, the same company flies a routine inspection and does not invoke the exemption. That flight is a civil small UAS operation in the United States, so Part 107 applies in full, including its registration, certification, and operating rules.

Common misreading: People read paragraph (b) as a list of permanent carve-outs that automatically exempt certain operators forever. For (b)(3) and (b)(4) it is the operator's election — the decision to conduct a given operation under the exemption or under part 91 — that takes that operation outside Part 107. Holding an exemption or an airworthiness certificate does not by itself remove every flight you make from Part 107.
