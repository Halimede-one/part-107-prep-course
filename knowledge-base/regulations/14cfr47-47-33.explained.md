This section covers first-time registration: an aircraft that has never been registered under 49 U.S.C. 44101-44104, under any other United States law, or under any foreign law. The owner may register it under Part 47 if two things are true. First, the owner complies with §§ 47.3, 47.7, 47.8, 47.9, 47.11, 47.13, 47.15, and 47.17, as applicable — see those sections for what each requires. Second, the owner sends in an Aircraft Registration Application, AC Form 8050-1, together with proof of ownership: an Aircraft Bill of Sale, AC Form 8050-2, signed by the seller, an equivalent bill of sale, or other evidence of ownership that § 47.11 allows.

Paragraph (b) is the escape hatch. If the applicant has a good reason for not being able to produce that evidence, he must submit other evidence the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) finds satisfactory. The chunk gives an example: an affidavit explaining why the required evidence cannot be produced, along with whatever further evidence is available to prove the transaction.

Paragraphs (c) and (d) add description duties for aircraft that were not bought finished from a manufacturer. An amateur-built aircraft owner applying under (a) and (b) must describe the aircraft by class (airplane, rotorcraft, glider, or balloon), serial number, number of seats, type of engine installed (reciprocating, turbopropeller, turbojet, or other), number of engines installed, and the make, model, and serial number of each engine installed, and must state whether the aircraft is built for land or water operation. As evidence of ownership, that owner submits an affidavit giving the U.S. registration number and stating that the aircraft was built from parts and that he is the owner. If it was built from a kit, a bill of sale from the kit manufacturer is also required. Paragraph (d) covers an owner, other than the holder of the type certificate, who assembles an aircraft from parts to conform to the approved type design: that owner gives the same aircraft and engine description required by (c), and also submits evidence of ownership satisfactory to the FAA, such as bills of sale, for all major components of the aircraft.

Illustration: An operator assembles an unmanned aircraft from parts rather than buying a finished one, and the aircraft has never been registered anywhere — not in the United States, not abroad. To register it under this part, the operator files AC Form 8050-1 and, because there is no seller and so no AC Form 8050-2 from a manufacturer, follows paragraph (c): a written description giving class, serial number, number of seats, the type and number of engines installed with each engine's make, model, and serial number, and a statement of whether the aircraft is built for land or water operation. In place of a bill of sale, the operator submits an affidavit giving the U.S. registration number and stating that the aircraft was built from parts and that he owns it. The airframe came from a purchased kit, so the operator also encloses the bill of sale from the kit manufacturer. Decision: file the affidavit plus the kit bill of sale, because paragraph (c) requires both when the aircraft is built from a kit.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, people read paragraph (a)(2) as an absolute barrier — no signed bill of sale, no registration. Paragraph (b) says otherwise: if there is good reason the evidence cannot be produced, other evidence satisfactory to the FAA, such as an explanatory affidavit with whatever supporting proof exists, can take its place. Second, people assume the amateur-built affidavit in paragraph (c) replaces all paperwork. It does not when a kit was used — the bill of sale from the kit manufacturer is still required on top of the affidavit.
