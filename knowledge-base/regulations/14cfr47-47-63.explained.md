This section lists what a manufacturer or dealer has to send in to get a Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate. There are just two items: the application itself, AC Form 8050-5, titled "Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate Application," and the fee set by § 47.17. The chunk does not state the fee amount — see that section for it. This rule applies to manufacturers and dealers, not to an individual registering a single aircraft for personal or business use. A dealer's certificate is a different track from ordinary aircraft registration, intended for businesses that hold aircraft in inventory.

Illustration: A company that builds and sells small unmanned aircraft wants to move demonstration units between customers without registering each one individually to itself. To apply for a Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate, it files AC Form 8050-5 with the FAA and pays the fee required by § 47.17. That is the whole application package under this section. A remote pilot who buys one of those drones for their own survey work does not use this form — they are not a manufacturer or dealer, so they register their aircraft through the ordinary registration process instead.

Common misreading: People sometimes read this as the registration path for anyone who owns several drones. It is not. The dealer's certificate is for manufacturers and dealers, and owning multiple aircraft does not make you either one.
