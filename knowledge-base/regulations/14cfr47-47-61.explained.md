This section sets up the Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate, a special kind of registration the FAA (Federal Aviation Administration) issues only to U.S. manufacturers and dealers. It exists for two stated reasons: so manufacturers can make any required flight tests of aircraft, and so a manufacturer or dealer can operate, demonstrate, and merchandise aircraft without having to get a separate Certificate of Aircraft Registration, AC Form 8050-3, for each aircraft every time ownership transfers under subpart B of this part. The chunk calls it an alternative to the normal Certificate of Aircraft Registration issued under subpart B. A dealer may hold one or more Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificates in addition to his original certificate, and may use such a certificate for any aircraft he owns. Paragraph (c) covers what happens at expiration: if a Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate for an aircraft registered under this subpart expires in accordance with § 47.71, the aircraft owner must submit an application for aircraft registration in accordance with § 47.31, or else the assignment of the registration number will be canceled in accordance with § 47.15(i)(2). This chunk does not state the duration of the certificate or the contents of those other sections — see §§ 47.71, 47.31, and 47.15(i)(2) for those.

Illustration: A U.S. aircraft dealer keeps six aircraft in stock for customer demonstrations. Rather than filing an AC Form 8050-3 application for each one and refiling every time a unit is sold and ownership transfers, the dealer holds Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificates and operates the stock under those for demonstration and sales flights. Months later, one certificate covering an unsold aircraft expires under § 47.71. Because the aircraft is still in inventory, the dealer, as owner, must now submit an application for aircraft registration under § 47.31; if he does not, the registration number assigned to that aircraft will be canceled under § 47.15(i)(2).

Common misreading: Two things trip people up. First, a Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate is not a permanent substitute for registration — the chunk says it expires under § 47.71, and after that the owner has to apply for ordinary registration or lose the registration number. Second, the chunk limits its use to aircraft the dealer owns and to the dealer's or manufacturer's own flight testing, operating, demonstrating, and merchandising; it is not a certificate a buyer inherits or a way for a non-dealer operator to skip registering an aircraft.
