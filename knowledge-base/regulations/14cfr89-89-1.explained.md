This is the definitions section for 14 CFR Part 89, the rules on remote identification (Remote ID) of unmanned aircraft. The opening line limits the reach of these definitions: they apply to this part, meaning Part 89. Two terms are defined in this chunk.

"Declaration of compliance" is a record that a producer submits to the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA). It is the producer's attestation that all the requirements of subpart F of this part have been met. Two kinds of products are named as the subject of such a record: a standard remote identification unmanned aircraft, and a remote identification broadcast module. Note who files it — the producer, not the pilot. The chunk does not spell out what subpart F requires; see that subpart.

"Home-built unmanned aircraft" means an unmanned aircraft that an individual built solely for education or recreation. Three parts matter: an individual built it, and the purpose was education or recreation, and that purpose was the sole purpose. The chunk defines the term only; it does not say what rules attach to a home-built aircraft.

Illustration: A remote pilot shopping for a new aircraft sees a manufacturer's listing that says the model is a "standard remote identification unmanned aircraft" and that a declaration of compliance is on file with the FAA. Reading this section, the pilot understands what that claim means: the producer has submitted a record to the FAA attesting that the requirements of subpart F of Part 89 have been met for that product. The pilot does not need to submit anything of that kind themselves — the declaration of compliance is the producer's record, filed by the producer.

Common misreading: People read "home-built unmanned aircraft" as covering anything they assembled themselves. The definition is narrower than that in one respect: the aircraft must have been built solely for education or recreation. An aircraft an individual assembled with a commercial job in mind does not fit the definition as written. The second trap is treating the declaration of compliance as paperwork the pilot files; the chunk says it is submitted by the producer.
