This section lists who may hold a Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate. An applicant has to meet all three conditions: (1) have an established place of business in the United States, (2) be substantially engaged in manufacturing or selling aircraft, and (3) be a citizen of the United States as that term is defined by 49 U.S.C. 40102(a)(15). The chunk does not reproduce that statutory definition of citizenship, so see that statute for its terms. Note that this is an eligibility rule for dealers, not for ordinary owners registering a single aircraft. The bracketed line at the end is the docket and amendment history for the section, not a requirement.

Illustration: A company assembles and sells small unmanned aircraft out of a leased shop in Ohio and wants a Dealer's Aircraft Registration Certificate so it can move demo units without registering each one individually to a buyer. It has a fixed business address in the United States, its main line of work is building and selling aircraft, and it meets the citizenship definition in 49 U.S.C. 40102(a)(15). All three conditions in this section are satisfied, so the company is eligible to apply. A hobbyist who occasionally resells a used drone from home would fail the 'substantially engaged in manufacturing or selling aircraft' condition and would not be eligible.

Common misreading: People read this as the registration rule that applies to them as a remote pilot or owner. It is not. It only sets who can qualify for the dealer's certificate, and meeting these three conditions is eligibility to apply, not the application procedure or the certificate itself.
