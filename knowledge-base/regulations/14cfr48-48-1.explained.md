This section says who Part 48 covers and which registration path a small unmanned aircraft must follow. Part 48 sets out registration and identification rules for small unmanned aircraft that are part of a small unmanned aircraft system (sUAS), using the definition of that term found in § 1.1 of this chapter — see that section for the definition itself. If a small unmanned aircraft is eligible to be registered in the United States, the owner has two choices: register and identify it under Part 48, or use the older aircraft path — the registration requirements in part 47 plus the identification and registration marking requirements in subparts A and C of part 45. Either route satisfies the rule; they are alternatives, not both. There is one group that does not get the choice. If the aircraft is intended to be operated outside the territorial airspace of the United States, or if it is registered through a trust or voting trust, it must be registered under subparts A and B of part 47 and must meet the identification and registration marking requirements of subparts A and C of part 45. For what each of those parts and subparts actually requires, see those sections.

Illustration: A remote pilot buys a small drone for photography jobs in her home state and wants it registered quickly. Because the aircraft is eligible for registration in the United States, is staying inside U.S. territorial airspace, and is being registered in her own name rather than through a trust, she falls under paragraph (b) and may use the Part 48 route. A colleague at the same company plans to take a similar drone on contract work overseas. His aircraft is intended to be operated outside the territorial airspace of the United States, so paragraph (c) applies and the Part 48 shortcut is not available to him — he must register under subparts A and B of part 47 and meet the marking requirements of subparts A and C of part 45.

Common misreading: People read paragraph (b) as a list of things they must do, and think they need to register under both Part 48 and Part 47. The word is 'either' — these are two alternative paths, and one of them is enough. The opposite error is assuming Part 48 always works; paragraph (c) carves out aircraft meant to fly outside U.S. territorial airspace and aircraft registered through a trust or voting trust, and those must use the part 47 and part 45 route.
