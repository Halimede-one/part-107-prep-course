This section lists what you have to tell the FAA Registry when you apply for a Certificate of Aircraft Registration under Part 48. Every applicant gives: a name (and, if the applicant is not an individual, the name of the authorized representative applying); a physical address (and the representative's physical address for non-individuals), with a mailing address instead only if mail cannot be received at the physical address; an email address (the representative's, for non-individuals); telephone number(s) (the representative's, for non-individuals); and the aircraft manufacturer and model name. Two serial-number rules follow. If the aircraft is a standard remote identification unmanned aircraft, you give the serial number the manufacturer issued under the design and production requirements of part 89 of this chapter. If the aircraft is equipped with a remote identification broadcast module, you give the serial number the module's manufacturer issued under those same part 89 requirements. You may list more than one broadcast module serial number on an application made under § 48.105 (see that section). But neither kind of serial number — the aircraft's or a module's — may appear on more than one Certificate of Aircraft Registration at the same time. The Administrator may also require other information. All of this goes to the Registry through the web-based small unmanned aircraft registration system, in the form and manner the Administrator prescribes. Once the application requirements in paragraph (a) are complete, the FAA issues the Certificate of Aircraft Registration.

Illustration: A survey company registers a drone in the company's name. The office manager, acting as the authorized representative, enters her own name, the company's physical address, her work email, and her desk phone, plus the drone's manufacturer and model. The drone is not a standard remote identification aircraft, so she attaches a remote identification broadcast module and enters that module's manufacturer-issued serial number. She also owns a second module she swaps between airframes, and she lists its serial number on the same application too, which the rule permits. A month later she registers a second drone and tries to put that same spare module's serial number on the new certificate as well. The system will not accept it: a module serial number cannot sit on two Certificates of Aircraft Registration at the same time. She must remove it from the first registration before using it on the second.

Common misreading: People read "an applicant may submit the serial number of more than one remote identification broadcast module" as permission to share one module's serial number across several registrations. It is the opposite: multiple modules may be listed on one certificate, but any single module serial number — like any single standard remote ID aircraft serial number — can appear on only one certificate at a time.
