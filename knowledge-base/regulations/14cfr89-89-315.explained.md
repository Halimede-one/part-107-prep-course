This section lists the seven pieces of information a remote identification broadcast module must be able to broadcast. A broadcast module is an add-on device you attach to a drone so it can transmit remote ID information. The required message elements are: (a) the identity of the unmanned aircraft, which is the serial number that the module's producer assigned to the module itself; (b) the latitude and longitude of the unmanned aircraft; (c) the geometric altitude of the unmanned aircraft; (d) the velocity of the unmanned aircraft; (e) the latitude and longitude of the take-off location; (f) the geometric altitude of the take-off location; and (g) a time mark giving the Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) time that a position source output applies to. Note two things about the identity element. The serial number broadcast is the module's serial number, assigned by whoever produced the module, not a number you create. And the position information covers both where the aircraft is now and where it took off from. This section states what the module must be capable of broadcasting; it does not itself address operating rules, which appear in other sections of this part.

Illustration: A remote pilot owns an older quadcopter with no built-in remote identification. She buys a broadcast module, mounts it on the airframe, and reads the serial number printed on the module's label. When she registers the aircraft, that module serial number is the identity her drone will broadcast in flight, because paragraph (a) ties the identity element to the serial number assigned to the module by its producer, not to the drone's own factory serial number. On a job in a park, she powers up the module on the ground at her launch point so it captures the take-off latitude, longitude, and geometric altitude required by paragraphs (e) and (f), then launches.

Common misreading: People often assume a broadcast module transmits the control station's location, the way some remote ID equipment does. This section does not say that. For a broadcast module the two location elements are the aircraft's current position and the take-off position. Pilots also mix up the identity element, expecting the drone's own serial number to be broadcast; paragraph (a) specifies the module's serial number.
