This part of the advisory circular walks through how to apply for a Remote Pilot Certificate under § 107.63. It splits applicants into two groups.

Paragraph 6.4.1 covers people who do not hold a part 61 pilot certificate, and also part 61 certificate holders who do not meet the flight review requirements in § 61.56 (see that section for what those requirements are). Both of those groups must use this process. A part 61 pilot who does meet the § 61.56 flight review requirements may choose this process too, but is not required to.

The process has three steps: (1) pass an initial aeronautical knowledge test at a Knowledge Testing Center (KTC) — see paragraphs 6.7 and 6.8; (2) complete and submit FAA Form 8710-13, the Remote Pilot Certificate and/or Rating Application; and (3) receive the permanent Remote Pilot Certificate once FAA internal processing finishes.

Step 2 has two options. Option 1 is the online form through the Integrated Airmen Certificate and Rating Application (IACRA) system at https://iacra.faa.gov/iacra/. The AC calls this the fastest and simplest method. You must have already passed the initial Remote Pilot knowledge test. After registering and logging in, you click "Start New Application" and select Application Type "Pilot," Certifications "Initial Remote Pilot," "Other Path Information," then "Start Application." When prompted you can either select the knowledge test information already shown or type in the 17-digit Knowledge Test Exam ID. Knowledge Test Reports upload to IACRA immediately, so the application can be processed without delay after the test. The KTC test proctor verifies the applicant's identity. You sign electronically and submit to the Airman Registry. No FAA representative signature is needed if you could self-certify. The only electronic signature on the IACRA application is the applicant's. The Registry confirms basic airman information with the Transportation Security Administration (TSA) before generating a Temporary Airman Certificate; once that's done you get a confirmation email and can log back into IACRA to print the temporary certificate.

Option 2 is a paper application. If you go this route, the original Remote Pilot initial aeronautical knowledge test report must be mailed with the application to DOT/FAA, Airmen Certification Branch (AFB-720), P.O. Box 25082, Oklahoma City, OK 73125.

The AC adds an important note: a Temporary Airman Certificate will not be provided to a remote pilot applicant who does not hold a part 61 certificate. That is why the AC says it is in the applicant's best interest to use Option 1 (IACRA) rather than paper — so you receive a Temporary Airman Certificate once TSA vetting is complete.

Paragraph 6.4.2 begins to describe an alternative process for individuals who hold a part 61 pilot certificate other than a student pilot certificate and who meet the § 61.56 flight review requirements, but the text of that process is cut off at the end of this chunk.

Illustration: Maya has never held any pilot certificate. She passes the initial aeronautical knowledge test at a KTC on a Tuesday morning and walks out with her test report showing a 17-digit Exam ID. That afternoon she has to choose how to file FAA Form 8710-13. She knows she wants to start flying for her roof-inspection side business as soon as possible. She picks Option 1, IACRA: she registers, logs in, clicks "Start New Application," selects "Pilot," then "Initial Remote Pilot," then "Other Path Information," and starts the application. When IACRA prompts her about the knowledge test, her test result is already uploaded, so she selects it rather than typing the Exam ID. She signs electronically and submits to the Airman Registry. No FAA representative has to sign, because she self-certified. A while later, after the Registry confirms her information with TSA, she gets a confirmation email, logs back into IACRA, and prints her Temporary Airman Certificate — which lets her operate while the permanent certificate works through FAA processing. Had she mailed a paper application with her original test report to the Oklahoma City address instead, she would have gotten no Temporary Airman Certificate at all, because she holds no part 61 certificate, and she would have had to wait for the permanent card.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, people assume the paper route is just slower but otherwise equivalent. It is not: the AC says a Temporary Airman Certificate will not be provided to an applicant who does not hold a part 61 certificate, so a non-pilot who files on paper has nothing to fly on until the permanent certificate arrives. Second, people read 6.4.1 as the "non-pilot path" only. It also applies, mandatorily, to part 61 certificate holders who do not meet the § 61.56 flight review requirements — and it remains an available option for part 61 pilots who do meet them. Also note the knowledge test comes first: you cannot start the IACRA application for an initial Remote Pilot Certificate before you have passed it.
