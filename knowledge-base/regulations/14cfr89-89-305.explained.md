This section lists the eight pieces of information a standard remote identification unmanned aircraft must be able to broadcast. "Standard remote identification" means the identification capability is built into the aircraft itself, rather than added by a separate broadcast module. The required message elements are: (a) the aircraft's identity, which is either a serial number assigned by the person responsible for producing the aircraft or a session ID; (b) the latitude and longitude of the control station; (c) the geometric altitude of the control station; (d) the latitude and longitude of the unmanned aircraft; (e) the geometric altitude of the unmanned aircraft; (f) the aircraft's velocity; (g) a time mark giving the Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) time that a position source output applies to; and (h) an indication of the aircraft's emergency status. Note the wording: the aircraft must be "capable of broadcasting" these elements. This section describes what the aircraft has to be able to send; it does not by itself describe pilot operating duties.

Illustration: A remote pilot buys a drone advertised as having built-in remote identification and wants to confirm what it transmits. Using a receiver app, she sees the broadcast includes a serial number, her own position on the ground, the drone's position, both altitudes, the drone's speed, a UTC time stamp, and an emergency status field. All eight elements in this section are present, so the aircraft is broadcasting the message elements this section requires. If the broadcast had shown only the drone's position and left out the control station location, the aircraft would not be meeting what this section calls for.

Common misreading: Two traps. First, people assume the serial number is always broadcast. Paragraph (a) gives a choice: a serial number assigned by the producer, or a session ID. Either one satisfies the identity element. Second, people forget that the control station's position and altitude are broadcast too, not just the aircraft's. Paragraphs (b) and (c) cover the control station; (d) and (e) cover the aircraft. Both sets are required.
