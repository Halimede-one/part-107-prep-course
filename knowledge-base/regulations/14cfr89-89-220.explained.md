This section covers what happens after someone applies for an FAA-recognized identification area (FRIA) — a defined patch of ground where unmanned aircraft without remote identification broadcast may be flown. Three things happen. First, from the moment the application goes in until the FRIA expires or is terminated, any change to the information in that application must be reported to the FAA within 10 calendar days of the change. The chunk gives examples but says the duty is not limited to them: a new point of contact, or a change in the FRIA's organizational affiliation, both count. Second, if the FRIA holder wants to move or reshape the geographic boundaries, that is not a simple notification — the holder has to submit a request describing the change for FAA review, and the boundaries do not change unless the request is approved under § 89.215 (see that section). Third, the FRIA stays under ongoing review by the Administrator under § 89.215, and that review may lead to termination. The sentence in paragraph (c) is cut off before it names the provision that termination happens under, so the citation for that is not visible in this chunk.

Illustration: A model aircraft club holds a FRIA at its field. In March the club elects a new president, who takes over as the listed point of contact. That is a change to information submitted in the application, so the club has 10 calendar days from the change to submit it to the FAA. In June the club leases an adjacent hayfield and wants to extend the FRIA over it. That is different: the club must submit a request describing the boundary change for FAA review, and until the FAA approves it under § 89.215, the FRIA boundaries are still the old ones — flying a non-broadcasting aircraft over the hayfield would be outside the FRIA.

Common misreading: People assume a boundary change is just another piece of information to report within 10 calendar days. It is not. Paragraph (a) is notification; paragraph (b) requires a request and FAA approval before the boundaries actually change. Also note the 10-day clock starts at the change itself, not at the next renewal or review, and the duty begins at the time of application — not only after the FRIA is granted.
