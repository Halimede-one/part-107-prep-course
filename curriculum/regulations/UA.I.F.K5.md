Objective: know what a Confirmation of Identification is and who needs one.

A Confirmation of Identification is the document a foreign registered civil unmanned aircraft with remote identification needs before it flies in the United States. Under 89.130, the person operating it must first submit a notice of identification in a form acceptable to the Administrator. The notice gives the name, physical address, telephone number, and email address of the operator and of any authorized representative, the aircraft's manufacturer and model, the serial number of the aircraft or of its remote identification broadcast module, the country of registration, and the registration number. On completion the FAA issues a Confirmation of Identification. The filing and the confirmation do not have the effect of United States aircraft registration.

The confirmation does two jobs. It satisfies the serial number condition in 89.110(b)(4) and 89.115(a)(1)(ii), which otherwise require the serial number to be on a United States Certificate of Aircraft Registration. And it must be kept at the aircraft's control station and produced when the FAA or a law enforcement officer asks for it. The holder must keep the information accurate and update it before operating again if anything changes.

Worked example: a Canadian operator brings a standard remote identification aircraft, registered in Canada, for a survey job in the United States. Before the first flight the operator files the notice of identification with the aircraft's serial number and Canadian registration number, receives the confirmation, and keeps a copy at the control station for the inspector who visits the site.

Common misreading: treating the confirmation as a United States registration, or as the only requirement a foreign operator faces. It satisfies the identification notice and nothing more; registration, certification, and authorization rules apply separately.
