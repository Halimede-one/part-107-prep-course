Objective: know the duty to fly only an aircraft that is in a condition for safe operation, before and during each flight.

Section 107.15 has two parts. First, no person may operate a civil small unmanned aircraft system unless it is in a condition for safe operation, and before each flight the remote pilot in command must check the system to determine whether it is. Second, no person may continue a flight when he or she knows or has reason to know that the system is no longer in a condition for safe operation. The first part is a check before every flight. The second part is a duty that continues while the aircraft is in the air.

The rule names the whole system, not only the airframe: the aircraft, the control station, the control link, the batteries, and anything else needed to fly safely. AC 107-2A chapter 7 describes how to meet the rule. Maintain the system by the manufacturer's instructions where they exist. Maintenance covers scheduled and unscheduled overhaul, repair, inspection, modification, replacement, and software upgrades. When a preflight inspection finds a component that needs service, flight should not take place until it is corrected, and a part that cannot be restored should be replaced. The AC's preflight inspection list includes the airframe and control surfaces, the propulsion system, the registration markings, attached equipment, the control link and GPS acquisition, power for the aircraft and the control station, correct movement of the control surfaces from the control station, and the anti-collision light when flying in civil twilight or at night.

Worked example: during the preflight check a pilot finds a hairline crack in one propeller. The aircraft is not in a condition for safe operation, so the pilot replaces the propeller before flying. Later, in flight, a motor begins to stutter and the aircraft wobbles. The pilot now has reason to know the system is no longer safe and lands as soon as possible. Finishing the shot first would break the second part of the rule.

Common misreading: treating the check as something done once at the start of the day. The check is before each flight, and the duty to stop continues for as long as the aircraft is flying.
