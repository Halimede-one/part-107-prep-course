Objective: know the eligibility and operating requirements for Category 2.

A small unmanned aircraft is eligible for Category 2 when it would not cause injury to a person equivalent to or greater than the injury from a transfer of 11 foot-pounds of kinetic energy on impact from a rigid object, has no exposed rotating parts that would lacerate human skin on impact, has no safety defect, is listed on a current FAA-accepted declaration of compliance for Category 2, and is labeled to show its Category 2 eligibility.

The manufacturer, or whoever submitted the declaration, must also provide remote pilot operating instructions, and the remote pilot must follow them. Operating over people under Category 2 is then allowed, with one limit: no sustained flight over an open-air assembly unless the aircraft meets the remote identification requirement.

Worked example: a pilot buys a 1.5-pound aircraft marketed for flight over people. Before flying over a crowd, the pilot checks three things: the model appears on the FAA's list of accepted declarations of compliance for Category 2, the aircraft carries the Category 2 label, and the aircraft is broadcasting remote identification. With all three, sustained flight over the crowd is allowed. Missing the label alone makes the aircraft ineligible.

Common misreading: treating the 11 foot-pound figure as something the pilot measures. The pilot relies on the declaration; the figure is the standard the declarant had to prove.
