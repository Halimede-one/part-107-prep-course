Objective: know what may be added to an aircraft eligible for operations over people without losing its eligibility.

The remote pilot operating instructions decide this. Sections 107.120(b)(2) and 107.130(b)(2) require them to describe the modifications that will not change the aircraft's eligibility, and AC 107-2A says that description must cover the aircraft itself and any payload a person may put on it. Components the instructions list as eligible for inclusion are the optional components. Anything else is a modification that the instructions do not allow, and it ends the aircraft's eligibility for Category 2 or 3 until a new declaration of compliance is accepted.

The AC's sample instructions show how an applicant writes this. The aircraft comes with a camera; for operations over people the camera may be replaced only with a pre-approved camera on the applicant's list; no other payload may be affixed unless it is listed; and any permissible payload must be securely attached throughout the operation. That last point repeats a rule every flight carries: under 107.49(e), the remote pilot in command must ensure before flight that any object attached to or carried by the aircraft is secure and does not adversely affect flight characteristics or controllability. For Category 1 there is no list, but any optional component counts toward the 0.55-pound limit.

Worked example: a pilot wants to add a small spotlight to a Category 2 aircraft for an evening shoot over a crowd. If the operating instructions list that light, or a light of that kind, as an allowable addition, the aircraft stays eligible, provided the light is securely attached. If the instructions do not list it, the aircraft loses its Category 2 eligibility while the light is fitted.

Common misreading: thinking anything that fits is an optional component. Optional means listed in the operating instructions.
