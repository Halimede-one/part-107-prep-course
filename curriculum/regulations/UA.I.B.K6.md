Objective: know the two kinds of hazardous operation that Part 107 prohibits.

Section 107.23 forbids two things. First, no person may operate a small unmanned aircraft system in a careless or reckless manner so as to endanger the life or property of another. Second, no person may allow an object to be dropped from a small unmanned aircraft in a manner that creates an undue hazard to persons or property. Both tests turn on the danger created, not on whether anyone was hurt.

AC 107-2A points out that small unmanned aircraft have operating considerations that manned aircraft do not, so more kinds of conduct can amount to careless or reckless operation. Its example is failing to consider the weather near structures, trees, or rolling terrain when flying in a densely populated area.

This unit has two sub-elements: 2.6a on careless or reckless operation, and 2.6b on dropping an object.

Worked example: a pilot flies low along a busy street in gusty wind to get a dramatic shot. Nothing is hit, but the manner of the flight endangers the people below, which is careless or reckless operation. A second pilot releases a test weight over an empty, cleared field as part of a planned trial. No undue hazard is created. The same release over a car park with people in it would be a violation.

Common misreading: assuming the rule needs an actual collision or injury before it is broken. It is the manner of operation, and the hazard it creates, that the rule addresses.
