Objective: know when a small unmanned aircraft may fly over people inside moving vehicles.

Section 107.145 says that no person may operate a small unmanned aircraft over a human being located inside a moving vehicle unless two conditions are met. First, the operation must meet the requirements of one of the four categories: 107.110 for Category 1, 107.115 for Category 2, 107.125 for Category 3, or 107.140 for Category 4. Second, for Category 1, 2, or 3, throughout the operation the aircraft must either remain within or over a closed- or restricted-access site where everyone inside a moving vehicle is on notice that an aircraft may fly over them, or not maintain sustained flight over moving vehicles. For Category 4, the aircraft must hold its airworthiness certificate under part 21 and be operated within the operating limitations of its approved flight manual, which must not prohibit operations over people inside moving vehicles.

AC 107-2A sets this out in two tables. Within or over a closed- or restricted-access site, flight over people in moving vehicles is allowed in all four categories, with everyone on notice. Outside such a site, Categories 1, 2, and 3 may only transit over moving vehicles, with no sustained flight, and Category 4 depends on its operating limitations. People directly participating in the operation are not restricted in either case. The rule is on the list in 107.205, so a certificate of waiver may permit more.

Worked example: a production company films a car commercial on a closed test track with a Category 2 aircraft. The drivers have been briefed, so the aircraft may follow the cars in sustained flight. The same company filming traffic on a public highway may only cross the road with an eligible aircraft, never hover or track the cars, and may not fly over the traffic at all with an aircraft that is eligible for no category.

Common misreading: thinking a Category 2 aircraft may hover over any traffic. Outside a closed- or restricted-access site it may only transit.
