Objective: know what you must show or allow when the FAA asks.

A remote pilot in command, the owner, or the person manipulating the flight controls must, on request, make available to the FAA the remote pilot certificate with a small UAS rating and any other document, record, or report that the regulations require to be kept. Registration is one such record.

The remote pilot in command, visual observer, owner, operator, or person manipulating the controls must, on request, allow the FAA to test or inspect the small unmanned aircraft system, the remote pilot in command, the person manipulating the controls, and the visual observer, to determine compliance with Part 107.

Since the operations-over-people rule, a person who holds an FAA-accepted declaration of compliance has parallel duties: make the declaration and its supporting records available on request, and allow inspection of the facilities, technical data, and aircraft the declaration covers.

Worked example: an FAA inspector approaches a remote pilot at a construction site and asks to see the pilot's certificate and the aircraft's registration. The pilot must produce both. The inspector then asks to examine the aircraft. The pilot must allow it. Refusing is itself a violation, separate from anything the inspection would have found.

Common misreading: thinking only the remote pilot in command has duties here. The owner, the person on the controls, and the visual observer are all named.
