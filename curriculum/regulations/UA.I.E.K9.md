Objective: know what the remote pilot operating instructions are, what they must contain, and how a remote pilot uses them.

A small unmanned aircraft eligible for Category 2 or Category 3 comes with remote pilot operating instructions. Sections 107.120(b)(2) and 107.130(b)(2) require the applicant for the declaration of compliance to have them and to make them available on sale or transfer of the aircraft, or when someone other than the applicant uses it. At a minimum the instructions must address three things: a system description that includes the required components, any system limitations, and the declared category or categories of operation; the modifications that will not change the aircraft's eligibility; and, if the aircraft has variable modes or configurations, how to verify and change the mode. Category 1 has no applicant requirements, so no instructions are required for it.

AC 107-2A explains the practice. The instructions must be specific to the aircraft design and may come in any format, on paper, with the packaging, or online, as long as the pilot can tell which version is in effect. The FAA encourages manufacturers to add performance limits such as temperature, wind, altitude, range, endurance, airspeed, and maximum weight, normal and emergency procedures, and weight and balance. The AC's sample instructions show a flight mode switch with a mode for Category 2, a mode for Category 3, and a mode for flights not over people, and separate lists of required components for each category. The remote pilot reads the instructions during preflight to put the aircraft in the right configuration, to know which payloads are allowed, and to confirm that the aircraft is in a condition for safe operation. The instructions aid the decision; the remote pilot in command still decides the safe parameters of the flight.

Worked example: a pilot takes delivery of a used Category 2 aircraft. The seller passes on the operating instructions, as the rule requires. The pilot reads that Category 2 needs the listed rotor guards and battery, that the camera may be swapped only for a model on the approved list, and that the mode switch must be set to the Category 2 position, and configures the aircraft that way before flying over people.

Common misreading: treating the operating instructions as an ordinary user manual. They are a required document whose minimum contents are set by rule, and a modification outside them ends the aircraft's eligibility.
