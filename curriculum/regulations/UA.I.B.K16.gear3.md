Objective: know where prior authorization is required before operating, and how it is obtained. This is the step-by-step version.

Step 1, the picture. Think of controlled airspace around an airport as an upside-down wedding cake that reaches all the way to the ground at the airport. Class B is the big cake around a major airport, Class C the medium one, Class D the small one around a towered field. Some airports with no tower have a Class E surface area, a ring drawn on the chart that also reaches the ground. If you stand inside any of these rings at the surface, you are in controlled airspace from the ground up.

Step 2, the rule. Inside those rings, no flight without prior authorization from Air Traffic Control. Outside them, in Class G, this rule does not apply.

Step 3, how to tell. Look at the sectional chart. A solid blue line is Class B. A solid magenta line is Class C. A dashed blue line is Class D. A dashed magenta line is a Class E surface area. If your launch point and your flight path are inside one of those lines, you need authorization.

Step 4, how to get it. Before the flight, request authorization through LAANC, the Low Altitude Authorization and Notification Capability, using an approved app, or through the FAA DroneZone website. The authorization names a maximum altitude for your location. Fly within it.

Step 5, the trap. "Near an airport" is not the test. A faded magenta band on the chart marks Class E that starts at 700 feet above the ground. That is not a surface area, and a flight under 400 feet there needs no authorization. The question is always: what airspace touches the ground where I am?

Memory handle: solid or dashed line around an airport that reaches the ground means ask first.

Worked example, step by step. A pilot is 3 miles from a towered airport. Chart check: the location is inside a dashed blue line. That is Class D at the surface. Authorization is needed. The pilot opens a LAANC app, requests the flight at 200 feet, receives approval, and flies no higher than 200 feet. Same pilot, next day, 8 miles out and outside every line: Class G, no authorization needed.
