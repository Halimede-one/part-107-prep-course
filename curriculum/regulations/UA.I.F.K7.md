Objective: know the remote identification label, and how to tell it apart from the other markings an aircraft may carry.

Section 89.525 requires a label on remote identification equipment. A standard remote identification unmanned aircraft produced under 89.515 must display a label indicating that it meets the requirements of Part 89, and a broadcast module produced under 89.520 must display a label indicating that the module meets them. In both cases the label must be in English and be legible, prominent, and permanently affixed. The label is one of several production duties in subpart F. Under 89.505 the producer assigns each aircraft or module a serial number that complies with the ANSI/CTA-2063-A standard, which is the serial number that goes on the declaration of compliance and on the registration. Producers must submit a declaration of compliance, allow FAA inspection, have independent audits performed, notify the public and the FAA of a defect within 15 calendar days of becoming aware of it, and, for a module, make installation and operating instructions available to anyone operating an aircraft with it.

A Part 107 aircraft may carry three different markings, and a remote pilot should keep them apart. The remote identification label under 89.525 says that the aircraft or module meets Part 89. The registration marking under 48.205 is the aircraft's unique identifier, normally its registration number, displayed legibly on an external surface. The category label under 107.120(b)(1) or 107.130(b)(1) says that the aircraft is eligible for Category 2 or 3 operations over people. Each comes from a different rule, and none substitutes for another. The label is not the pilot's proof of compliance by itself: the aircraft or module must also be on the FAA's list of accepted declarations of compliance, which the pilot can check online.

Worked example: a pilot buys a broadcast module. The pilot looks for the Part 89 label on the module, confirms that the module's serial number is on the FAA's declaration of compliance list, adds that serial number to the aircraft's registration, and separately checks that the aircraft still shows its registration number on the outside.

Common misreading: treating any label on the aircraft as remote identification compliance. The remote identification label, the registration marking, and the category label are three different things, and the declaration of compliance listing matters as much as the label.
