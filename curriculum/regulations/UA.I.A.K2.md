Objective: know the defined terms in Part 107, because the questions use them exactly.

Small unmanned aircraft: an unmanned aircraft weighing less than 55 pounds on takeoff, including everything on board or attached to it. The payload counts. A 50-pound aircraft carrying a 6-pound camera is not a small unmanned aircraft.

Unmanned aircraft: an aircraft operated without the possibility of direct human intervention from within or on the aircraft.

Small unmanned aircraft system (small UAS, or sUAS): the small unmanned aircraft plus the elements needed to operate it safely in the airspace, such as the communication links and the components that control the aircraft. The aircraft alone is not the system.

Control station: the interface the remote pilot uses to control the flight path of the aircraft. A handheld transmitter, a tablet app, or a ground station computer are all control stations.

Visual observer: a person the remote pilot in command designates to help the remote pilot in command and the person manipulating the flight controls to see and avoid other air traffic and objects, in the air or on the ground.

Corrective lenses: spectacles or contact lenses. These are the only vision aids allowed when keeping the aircraft in sight.

Worked example: a question asks who "maintains visual line of sight" during a flight. The defined term is visual observer, and the defined aid is corrective lenses. Binoculars are not corrective lenses, so a visual observer using binoculars is not meeting the rule.

Common misreading: counting the aircraft weight without the payload. The 55-pound limit includes everything attached at takeoff.
