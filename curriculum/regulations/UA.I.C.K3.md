Objective: know who is eligible for a remote pilot certificate with a small UAS rating, and how the certificate is obtained.

Section 107.61 sets four eligibility requirements, subject to the alcohol and drug provisions of 107.57 and 107.59. The applicant must be at least 16 years of age; be able to read, speak, write, and understand the English language, although the FAA may issue the certificate with operating limitations when a medical reason prevents one of these; not know or have reason to know of a physical or mental condition that would interfere with the safe operation of a small unmanned aircraft system; and demonstrate aeronautical knowledge in one of two ways. The first way is to pass an initial aeronautical knowledge test covering the areas in 107.73. The second is open to a person who holds a pilot certificate under part 61, other than a student pilot certificate, and meets the flight review requirement of 61.56: complete training covering the areas in 107.74. No medical certificate is required.

The application under 107.63 is made in a form the Administrator accepts, with evidence of the passed test or the training completion certificate. On the part 61 route the application goes to a Flight Standards office, a designated pilot examiner, an airman certification representative, a certificated flight instructor, or another authorized person, who verifies the applicant's identity, and the applicant shows the flight review by logbook endorsement. FAA guidance adds the practical steps: obtain an FAA Tracking Number in the IACRA system before registering for the knowledge test, take the test at an approved knowledge testing center with government-issued photo identification, and apply on FAA Form 8710-13 through IACRA. The Transportation Security Administration vets the applicant before a temporary certificate is issued. Under 107.64 a temporary certificate is valid for up to 120 calendar days and expires on the date shown, on receipt of the permanent certificate, or on notice that the certificate is denied or revoked. A certificated flight instructor cannot issue a temporary certificate, and a paper application from someone without a part 61 certificate gets none.

Three rules guard the knowledge test. The applicant must bring identification showing a photograph, a signature, a date of birth, and a permanent mailing address, with a residential address if the mailing address is a post office box. An applicant who fails the test may not retake it for 14 calendar days. And an applicant who cheats, for example by copying the test, using unauthorized material, or taking the test for someone else, is barred for 1 year from applying for any certificate, rating, or authorization and from taking any test, and any certificate held may be suspended or revoked.

Worked example: a 16-year-old with no pilot certificate creates an IACRA profile to get a tracking number, books the Unmanned Aircraft General test at a testing center, brings photo identification, passes, and files Form 8710-13 in IACRA with the exam ID. After the security vetting, the temporary certificate can be printed from IACRA, and the permanent one arrives by mail. A private pilot with a flight review within the previous 24 months instead completes the online part 107 initial course and has identity and the flight review validated in person.

Common misreading: assuming the minimum age is 18, or that an FAA medical certificate is needed. The age is 16, and there is no medical certificate; the medical standard is the same self-assessment as in 107.17.
