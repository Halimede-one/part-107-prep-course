Objective: know what a closed- or restricted-access site is, and where the rules depend on one.

Two rules turn on closed- or restricted-access sites. Under 107.125(c)(1), a Category 3 aircraft may fly over people only within or over such a site, where every person inside is on notice that a small unmanned aircraft may fly over them, or else without sustained flight over any non-participant who is not sheltered. Under 107.145(b)(1), an aircraft in Category 1, 2, or 3 may maintain sustained flight over people inside moving vehicles only within or over such a site, with everyone in the vehicles on notice. Outside such a site, the same aircraft may only transit over them.

AC 107-2A describes what the site must be. Everyone within it must be on notice of the flights, and people who are not participating but are working at the site should be advised of any precautions to take. The remote pilot is responsible for ensuring that no one enters the site inadvertently or without authorization. Adequate assurance can come from physical barriers such as barricades and fencing, from monitoring personnel, or from geographic boundaries such as rivers, canals, cliffs, and heavily wooded areas. And the site must not be an open-air assembly, because Category 3 may never fly over one.

Worked example: a Category 3 aircraft inspects a fenced industrial plant. The gates are monitored, every employee has been told that an aircraft will fly overhead, and the plant is not an open-air assembly. Sustained flight over the workers is allowed. A public beach, even one leased for the day, is neither closed nor on notice unless access is controlled and everyone on it has been told.

Common misreading: equating private property with a closed-access site. The test is controlled access plus notice to everyone inside, not ownership.
