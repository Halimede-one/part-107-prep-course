Objective: know who counts as an applicant for a declaration of compliance, and what an applicant must do.

AC 107-2A defines an applicant as any person who produces, designs, or modifies a small unmanned aircraft to be eligible to operate over people. That covers more than factories. A person who manufactures and sells a kit with every part needed to build an operable aircraft must ensure that the assembled aircraft, not only its parts, meets the safety requirements through an FAA-accepted means of compliance, and must declare compliance before the kit is sold. A person who builds an aircraft from separately bought parts is an applicant and must submit a declaration of compliance to conduct Category 2 or 3 operations. And a person who modifies an aircraft listed on a declaration so that it no longer matches the declared configuration is an applicant and must submit a new declaration before flying over people within that category.

The duties come from subpart D. Under 107.160 the applicant submits a declaration of compliance with the required information, declares that the aircraft meets 107.120(a) or 107.130(a) through an accepted means of compliance, verifies that it has no safety defects, maintains a product support and notification process, and agrees to let the FAA inspect its facilities, technical data, and aircraft and witness tests. Under 107.120(b) and 107.130(b) the applicant labels the aircraft and provides remote pilot operating instructions. Under 107.165 the applicant keeps the supporting information: a producer for two years after it stops producing the aircraft, and a designer or modifier for two years after submitting the declaration. Someone who submits a means of compliance keeps its description and justification for as long as the means of compliance remains accepted. Category 1 has no applicant requirements at all.

Worked example: a hobbyist builds a 2-pound aircraft from parts and wants to fly it over guests at a client's event under Category 2. The hobbyist is an applicant: show compliance through an accepted means of compliance, verify no safety defects, submit the declaration through the FAA's online system, label the aircraft, write the operating instructions, and keep the records for two years after the submission.

Common misreading: thinking only manufacturers deal with declarations of compliance. Anyone who builds or modifies an aircraft and wants Category 2 or 3 eligibility is an applicant.
