Objective: know how long a remote pilot's aeronautical knowledge stays current, and what renews it.

Section 107.65 says that a person may not exercise the privileges of a remote pilot in command with a small UAS rating unless, within the previous 24 calendar months, the person has done one of three things in a manner acceptable to the Administrator: passed an initial aeronautical knowledge test covering the areas in 107.73; completed recurrent training covering those same areas; or, as the holder of a part 61 pilot certificate other than a student pilot certificate who meets the flight review requirement of 61.56, completed training covering the areas in 107.74. A recurrent test or training completed before April 6, 2021 within the 24 months counts as well. The certificate itself does not expire. What lapses is the privilege to act as remote pilot in command, until the training is done.

The areas in 107.73 are regulations, airspace and flight restrictions, aviation weather sources and effects, loading, emergency procedures, crew resource management, radio communication procedures, determining performance, physiological effects of drugs and alcohol, aeronautical decision-making and judgment, airport operations, maintenance and preflight inspection procedures, and operation at night. The 107.74 training for part 61 pilots covers eight of these areas and leaves out airspace, weather sources, radio procedures, drugs and alcohol, decision-making, and airport operations, which those pilots covered in their own certification. FAA guidance names the online recurrent course, which is free, and shows how calendar months are counted: a test passed on September 13, 2020 must be followed by recurrent training no later than September 30, 2022, the end of the 24th calendar month. A pilot who completes the training late may not exercise the privileges from the day after the deadline until the training is done, and the next 24 calendar months count from the month of completion. Completing the training early restarts the count from the month of completion. Night operations under 107.29 also require that the test or training was completed after April 6, 2021.

Worked example: a pilot passed the knowledge test on March 10, 2025. The pilot must complete recurrent training by March 31, 2027. Completing it in January 2027 moves the next deadline to January 31, 2029.

Common misreading: counting 24 months from the exact test date rather than to the end of the calendar month, or believing the certificate is revoked when recency lapses. The privilege lapses; the certificate stays, and the privilege returns once recurrent training is completed.
