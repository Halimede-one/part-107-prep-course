Objective: know how an alcohol or drug offense affects a remote pilot certificate, whether the offense happened in the air or not.

Section 107.57 names two kinds of offense and attaches the same consequences to both. The first is a conviction for violating any federal or state statute relating to the growing, processing, manufacture, sale, disposition, possession, transportation, or importation of narcotic drugs, marijuana, or depressant or stimulant drugs or substances. The conviction does not have to involve an aircraft. The second is committing an act prohibited by 91.17(a) or 91.19(a): acting as a crewmember within 8 hours after drinking alcohol, while under the influence, while using a drug that affects the faculties contrary to safety, or with an alcohol concentration of 0.04 or greater; or operating an aircraft with knowledge that narcotic drugs, marijuana, or depressant or stimulant substances are carried in it without authority. No court conviction is needed for the second kind; the act itself is the ground.

The consequences are the same for both. An application for a remote pilot certificate with a small UAS rating may be denied for a period of up to 1 year after the date of the final conviction or of the act. A certificate already held may be suspended or revoked. Unit 2.8 covers the operating rule; this unit covers what the same conduct does to the certificate.

Worked example: a person is convicted of possessing a controlled substance and applies for a remote pilot certificate 6 months later. The FAA may deny the application for up to 1 year after the conviction became final. A certificated remote pilot flies a paid job 5 hours after a drink. That act is prohibited by 91.17(a), so the FAA may suspend or revoke the certificate without any criminal case.

Common misreading: assuming only aviation offenses count, or that a conviction is always required. A drug conviction of any kind is a ground under 107.57(a), and the act alone is a ground under 107.57(b).
