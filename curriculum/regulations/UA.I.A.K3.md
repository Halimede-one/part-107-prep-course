Objective: know what happens when a record, report, or certificate is falsified, reproduced, or altered.

The falsification rule for Part 107 now sits in 14 CFR part 3, subpart D, which applies to everyone subject to Part 107. Section 3.403 forbids three things. First, making, or causing someone else to make, a fraudulent or intentionally false statement in any document submitted under Part 107 (an application, certificate, rating, authorization, declaration, record, or report) or in any document kept, made, or used to show compliance with Part 107. Second, producing, reproducing, or altering any such document for a fraudulent purpose. Third, knowingly omitting a material fact from any such document. "Document in any format" covers electronic and physical documents and tangible items such as data plates or marked parts.

Doing any of these is a basis for two kinds of consequence. The FAA may deny, suspend, modify, revoke, rescind, remove, or withdraw any certificate, rating, authorization, declaration, or similar document it has issued to that person or that the person has applied for, and it may impose a civil penalty. The consequences are not limited to the document involved: a false accident report can cost you your remote pilot certificate. Section 3.405 adds that an incorrect statement or omission of a fact that was material to the issuance or validity of a certificate can by itself be a basis for suspending, revoking, or denying it.

Worked example: a remote pilot damages a parked car during a flight and writes an accident report that understates the repair cost so it looks like no report was needed. That is an intentionally false statement in a report submitted under Part 107, which 3.403(a) forbids. The FAA may revoke the pilot's certificate and impose a civil penalty, on top of whatever the car's owner pursues.

Common misreading: assuming a copied or edited certificate is harmless if the underlying facts are true. Producing, reproducing, or altering a certificate for a fraudulent purpose is itself the violation (3.403(b)).
