Objective: know the eligibility and operating requirements for Category 4.

Category 4 is the certified-aircraft route. The small unmanned aircraft must hold an airworthiness certificate issued under Part 21, and it must be operated in accordance with the operating limitations in its approved flight manual or as the FAA otherwise specifies. Those operating limitations must not prohibit operations over people.

Because the aircraft is certificated, it comes with maintenance duties that other categories do not have: maintenance, preventive maintenance, alterations, and inspections must be performed as required, by the people and using the records the rule names, so the aircraft stays airworthy. The owner or operator is responsible for keeping it that way.

Like the other categories, Category 4 permits sustained flight over an open-air assembly only if the aircraft meets the remote identification requirement.

Worked example: a company operates a 40-pound inspection aircraft that holds an airworthiness certificate. Its flight manual allows flight over people at or below 100 feet. The company may fly over people within that limit, must keep the required maintenance and inspection records, and must have remote identification before sustained flight over a crowd.

Common misreading: thinking Category 4 is for the heaviest aircraft only. It is for any small unmanned aircraft with an airworthiness certificate, whatever its weight.
