Objective: know which changes to an aircraft keep its eligibility for operations over people, and which end it.

For Category 2 and Category 3, the remote pilot operating instructions are the test. Sections 107.120(b)(2)(ii) and 107.130(b)(2)(ii) require the instructions to describe the modifications that will not change the aircraft's ability to meet the requirements of its category. AC 107-2A draws the line from there. A modification that the instructions allow keeps the aircraft eligible. A modification that the instructions do not allow renders the aircraft ineligible for Category 2 or 3 operations over people. A person who makes such a modification and still wants to fly over people takes on the responsibilities of an applicant: show through an FAA-accepted means of compliance that the modified aircraft meets the safety requirements, and submit a new declaration of compliance before flying over people.

Maintenance that does not change the aircraft's configuration or characteristics needs no new declaration. The AC's example is propellers: if replacing them is listed as an allowed modification, the pilot may replace them; if the replacement propellers are not covered by the instructions, a new declaration is required. After a modification that changes the category the aircraft is eligible for, the label must change too, with the old label removed or covered so that only the correct category shows. Section 107.150 adds a related rule: an aircraft may be eligible for more than one category as long as the remote pilot cannot inadvertently switch between modes or configurations, and the instructions must say how to verify and change the mode. Category 1 has no declaration, so for Category 1 the question is only whether the modified aircraft still weighs 0.55 pounds or less and still has no exposed rotating parts that would lacerate skin.

Worked example: a pilot replaces the stock propellers on a Category 2 aircraft with aftermarket carbon-fiber propellers that the operating instructions do not list. The aircraft is no longer eligible for Category 2 until a new declaration covering that configuration is accepted. Fitting a camera from the instructions' list of approved payloads changes nothing.

Common misreading: treating every part swap as maintenance that keeps eligibility. Only the changes the operating instructions allow do.
