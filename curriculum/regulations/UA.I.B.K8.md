Objective: know the alcohol and drug rules that bind the remote pilot in command, the person on the controls, and the visual observer.

Section 107.27 applies 91.17 and 91.19 to anyone manipulating the flight controls, acting as remote pilot in command, or acting as visual observer. Under 91.17(a), no person may act, or attempt to act, as a crewmember within 8 hours after consuming any alcoholic beverage; while under the influence of alcohol; while using any drug that affects the person's faculties in any way contrary to safety; or with an alcohol concentration of 0.04 or greater in a blood or breath specimen. The 8-hour rule and the 0.04 limit are separate tests. A person who waited 8 hours but is still under the influence, or still at 0.04 or above, may not act.

Section 91.17 also covers testing. On request of a law enforcement officer with authority under state or local law, a crewmember must submit to a test of blood or breath alcohol concentration when the officer is investigating conduct of the kind the rule prohibits. When the FAA has a reasonable basis to believe a violation occurred, the person must furnish, or authorize the release of, the results of any test taken within 4 hours after acting as a crewmember. Section 91.19 prohibits operating an aircraft with knowledge that narcotic drugs, marihuana, or depressant or stimulant drugs or substances are carried in it, unless the carriage is authorized by federal or state law. What an alcohol or drug offense, or a refusal to be tested, means for the certificate itself is covered in chapter 3.

AC 107-2A reminds remote pilots that over-the-counter medicines such as some antihistamines and decongestants can cause drowsiness, and that the remote pilot in command is responsible for ensuring that no crewmember is impaired.

Worked example: a visual observer finishes a glass of wine at 7 pm. The night flight is set for 1 am, 6 hours later. The observer may not take part until 3 am, when 8 hours have passed, and then only if no longer under the influence and below 0.04.

Common misreading: using the driving limit of 0.08. For a crewmember the limit is 0.04, and the 8-hour rule applies whatever the concentration.
