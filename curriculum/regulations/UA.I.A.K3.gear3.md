Objective: The ramifications of falsification, reproduction, or alteration of a certificate, rating, authorization, record, or report.

We'll take this rule one small piece at a time. By the end you'll know where the rule lives, the three things it forbids, and what the FAA can do about it.

**Piece 1: where the rule lives.**

For Part 107, the falsification rule is not inside Part 107 itself. It sits in **14 CFR part 3, subpart D** (sections 3.401 through 3.405), and that subpart applies to everyone subject to Part 107. So "it's not in my Part 107 printout" is not a defense — it still binds you.

**Piece 2: the three forbidden acts.**

Section 3.403 forbids exactly three things. A mental hook: **Say it false, Make it fake, Hide a fact.**

- *Say it false.* Making — or causing someone else to make — a fraudulent or intentionally false statement in any document submitted under Part 107 (an application, certificate, rating, authorization, declaration, record, or report), or in any document kept, made, or used to show compliance with Part 107.
- *Make it fake.* Producing, reproducing, or altering any such document for a fraudulent purpose.
- *Hide a fact.* Knowingly omitting a material fact from any such document.

Each one stands on its own. You only have to do one of the three to be in violation.

**Piece 3: what counts as a "document."**

Picture a wide basket, not a filing cabinet. The rule covers a document *in any format*: electronic files, paper, and tangible items such as data plates or marked parts. An edited PDF counts. A screenshot you doctored counts. A marked part counts.

**Piece 4: the two buckets of consequence.**

Doing any of the three acts is a basis for two kinds of consequence, and the FAA can reach for either or both.

- *Bucket one, your paperwork.* The FAA may **deny, suspend, modify, revoke, rescind, remove, or withdraw** any certificate, rating, authorization, declaration, or similar document it has issued to you, or that you have applied for.
- *Bucket two, your wallet.* The FAA may impose a **civil penalty**.

**Piece 5: the consequence is not confined to the document you faked.**

This is the part people miss, so slow down here. The FAA is not limited to acting on the one paper you lied on. A false *accident report* can cost you your *remote pilot certificate*. Think of it less like a parking ticket tied to one car, and more like a judgment about whether you can be trusted with anything the FAA issues you.

**Piece 6: a second, separate hook.**

Section 3.405 adds that an incorrect statement, or an omission of a fact that was material to the issuance or validity of a certificate, can by itself be a basis for suspending, revoking, or denying that certificate.

**Work an example step by step.**

A remote pilot damages a parked car during a flight and writes an accident report that understates the repair cost, so it looks like no report was needed.

1. Is there a document under Part 107? Yes — an accident report.
2. Which of the three acts? *Say it false* — an intentionally false statement, forbidden by 3.403(a).
3. What can follow? The FAA may revoke the pilot's certificate and impose a civil penalty — on top of whatever the car's owner pursues.

**The misreading to guard against.**

People assume a copied or edited certificate is harmless as long as the underlying facts are true — "I really do hold this certificate, I just reprinted it to look nicer." That reasoning fails. Producing, reproducing, or altering a certificate *for a fraudulent purpose* is itself the violation under 3.403(b). The act is the offense; no separate lie is needed.
