Objective: know when and how an accident must be reported.

A remote pilot in command must report an operation to the FAA within 10 calendar days when either of two things happened. First, serious injury to any person, or any loss of consciousness. Second, damage to any property other than the small unmanned aircraft itself, unless the cost of repair, including materials and labor, does not exceed $500, or the fair market value of the property does not exceed $500 if it is a total loss.

The report goes to the FAA in a manner acceptable to the Administrator. In practice that is the FAA DroneZone website, or the nearest FAA Flight Standards District Office. The report describes the operation and what happened.

Two details carry questions. Damage to the drone itself never triggers a report, however expensive. And "serious injury" follows the Abbreviated Injury Scale: level 3 or higher, which means injuries such as a fracture or a wound needing surgery, not a scrape or a bruise. Loss of consciousness of any length triggers a report on its own.

Worked example: a drone clips a fence and falls onto a garden shed. The shed roof needs a $350 repair. No report is required, because the cost does not exceed $500. If the same fall had cracked a $1,200 window, a report would be due within 10 calendar days.

Common misreading: counting the cost of the drone. Only property other than the small unmanned aircraft counts toward the $500 threshold.
