Objective: know the one exception in Part 89 for research and compliance testing, and how it is obtained.

Section 89.120 allows the Administrator to authorize operations without remote identification where the operation is solely for the purpose of aeronautical research or to show compliance with regulations. Section 89.105 names 89.120 as the exception to the general requirement. Two words carry the rule. The operation must be solely for research or compliance showing, and it must be authorized: the exception does not apply on its own. Subpart F has a matching provision for production: its design and production requirements do not apply to an unmanned aircraft designed or produced exclusively for aeronautical research or to show compliance with regulations.

FAA guidance describes the mechanism. The FAA issues a Remote ID Letter of Authorization to operators for aeronautical research or to show compliance with regulations, and says that other operations, such as drone light shows conducted under an FAA-issued waiver, may also be eligible for one. A request to operate without remote identification, or to deviate from its requirements, is made to the FAA by email.

Worked example: a university laboratory tests a new airframe that has no remote identification equipment. Before the first flight it requests and receives a letter of authorization for the research program. Without the letter, the flights would need a broadcast module or a FRIA like any other.

Common misreading: thinking that calling a flight research exempts it. The operation must be solely research or compliance showing, and the Administrator must have authorized it.
