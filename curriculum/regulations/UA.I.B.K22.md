Objective: know what a person must hold and maintain to act as remote pilot in command.

Section 107.12(b) says that no person may act as remote pilot in command unless that person has a remote pilot certificate with a small UAS rating issued under subpart C and satisfies 107.65. Section 107.12(a) says the same for manipulating the flight controls, with the one exception of a person flying under the direct supervision of a remote pilot in command who can immediately take direct control. Section 107.12(c) allows the Administrator to authorize an airman to operate a civil foreign-registered small unmanned aircraft without an FAA certificate, consistent with international standards.

Section 107.65 is the recency requirement. A person may not exercise the privileges of a remote pilot in command unless, within the previous 24 calendar months, he or she has passed an initial aeronautical knowledge test; completed recurrent training; or, as the holder of a part 61 pilot certificate other than a student pilot certificate who meets the flight review requirement, completed the training in 107.74. Holding the certificate is therefore only half the requirement. The other half is renewed every 24 calendar months. AC 107-2A adds that during flight the remote pilot in command must have the certificate and personal identification in physical possession and readily accessible, and 107.7 requires presenting them to the FAA on request. How to obtain the certificate, and what can disqualify an applicant, is the subject of chapter 3 (Task C).

Worked example: a pilot passed the knowledge test 30 months ago and has done no recurrent training since. The certificate is still valid, but the pilot may not act as remote pilot in command until completing recurrent training. Until then the pilot may fly only under the direct supervision of a current remote pilot in command.

Common misreading: thinking the certificate alone is enough. Recency under 107.65 is part of the requirement to act as remote pilot in command.
