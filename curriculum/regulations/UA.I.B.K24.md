Objective: know when civil twilight is and what a flight during civil twilight requires.

Section 107.29(c) defines civil twilight for Part 107. Outside Alaska it is the period that begins 30 minutes before official sunrise and ends at official sunrise, and the period that begins at official sunset and ends 30 minutes after official sunset. In Alaska it is the period of civil twilight defined in the Air Almanac.

Under 107.29(b), no person may operate a small unmanned aircraft system during civil twilight unless the aircraft has lighted anti-collision lighting visible for at least 3 statute miles, with a flash rate sufficient to avoid a collision. The remote pilot in command may reduce the intensity of the lighting, but may not extinguish it, on determining that operating conditions make the reduction in the interest of safety. Civil twilight carries no training condition; that belongs to night. AC 107-2A adds that the preflight check before a twilight flight must confirm that the light works, and that the light's power draw may shorten the planned flight time. The lighting requirement can be waived under 107.205.

Worked example: official sunrise is 6:30 am. From 6:00 to 6:30 am the flight is in civil twilight, so the anti-collision light must be on and visible for 3 statute miles. A pilot flying at 6:15 am with the light off is in violation. From 6:30 am no lighting is required.

Common misreading: assuming civil twilight brings the night rules with it. Civil twilight requires the lighting only. Night requires the lighting and the knowledge test or training completed after April 6, 2021.
