Objective: know the limits on carrying property for compensation or hire under Part 107.

Part 107 permits the transportation of property by small unmanned aircraft for compensation or hire, within limits that AC 107-2A sets out. The transport must occur wholly within the bounds of a single state. The operation must comply with all the operating restrictions of Part 107 and be conducted within a confined area. The flight must stay within visual line of sight of the remote pilot, and this cannot be waived for transportation of property. The aircraft may not be operated from a moving vehicle or aircraft when carrying property for compensation or hire, which 107.25 states and which 107.205 confirms cannot be waived. The maximum total weight of the aircraft, including the property carried, is under 55 pounds. And under 107.36 the property may not be hazardous material.

The other operating rules apply as in any flight: the pilot must know the aircraft's location, attitude, altitude, and direction, yield the right of way, and keep the ability to see and avoid other aircraft.

Worked example: a farm supply store flies a replacement part 1 mile across a customer's land, in sight the whole way, for a fee. That is allowed. Delivering the same part across a state line, or beyond visual line of sight, or launching it from a moving truck, is not allowed under Part 107.

Common misreading: thinking Part 107 bans paid delivery. It permits it, within a single state, in visual line of sight, under 55 pounds all up, and never from a moving vehicle.
