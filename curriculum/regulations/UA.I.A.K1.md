Objective: know when Part 107 applies, and when it does not.

Part 107 is the rule for flying a civil small unmanned aircraft system in the United States. "Small" means the aircraft weighs less than 55 pounds at takeoff, counting everything on board or attached to it. "Civil" means it is not a public (government) aircraft. The rule covers three things: registration, the remote pilot certificate, and how the aircraft may be operated. Since 2021 it also covers which aircraft may fly over people.

Section 107.1(b) names four things Part 107 does not apply to: air carrier operations; any aircraft flown under the recreational exception in 49 U.S.C. 44809; an operation that the holder of a Section 333 or 49 U.S.C. 44807 exemption elects to conduct under that exemption; and an operation a person elects to conduct under Part 91 with a small unmanned aircraft system that has an airworthiness certificate. AC 107-2A also lists amateur rockets, moored balloons, unmanned free balloons, kites, public aircraft operations (such as a city fire department's), and operations outside the United States as outside Part 107.

Worked example: a real-estate photographer flies a 2-pound quadcopter to photograph a house for a listing. The aircraft is under 55 pounds and the flight is for a business purpose, so Part 107 applies in full. The same person flying the same aircraft on a weekend purely for fun could instead fly under the recreational exception, with its own rules. The purpose of the flight, not the aircraft, decides which rules apply.

Common misreading: thinking Part 107 is only for "commercial" flights. Part 107 is the default for any civil small unmanned aircraft flight that is not purely recreational. Flying for a nonprofit, a school, or your own farm is still a Part 107 flight.
