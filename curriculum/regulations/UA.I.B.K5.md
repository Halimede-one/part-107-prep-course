Objective: know what a remote pilot in command may do in an in-flight emergency, and what reporting follows.

Section 107.21 gives the remote pilot in command emergency authority. In an in-flight emergency that requires immediate action, the remote pilot in command may deviate from any rule of Part 107 to the extent necessary to meet that emergency. The authority is limited to the emergency and to the extent necessary: it is not a licence to deviate further or for longer than the situation demands. A remote pilot in command who deviates must, on request of the Administrator, send a written report of the deviation to the FAA. There is no report unless the FAA asks for one.

AC 107-2A describes an in-flight emergency as an unexpected and unforeseen serious occurrence or situation that requires urgent, prompt action, and says the emergency action should be taken in a way that minimizes injury or damage to property.

Keep this rule separate from accident reporting under 107.9. A deviation report is only on request. An accident report is required within 10 calendar days whenever an operation causes serious injury or loss of consciousness, or property damage above the 107.9 threshold, whether or not any rule was deviated from.

Worked example: an aircraft loses its control link and begins drifting toward a crowd. The pilot climbs briefly above 400 feet to clear a tree line and regain the link, then lands. The deviation was necessary to meet the emergency, so it is permitted. No report is due unless the FAA requests one. If the aircraft had struck someone and caused a serious injury, a 107.9 accident report would be due within 10 calendar days regardless.

Common misreading: thinking a deviation must always be reported within 10 days. The 10-day rule is for accidents under 107.9. The emergency deviation report is sent only when the FAA asks.
