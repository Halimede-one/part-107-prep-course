Objective: know the eligibility and operating requirements for Category 3.

Eligibility for Category 3 mirrors Category 2 with a higher impact limit: the aircraft would not cause injury equivalent to or greater than a transfer of 25 foot-pounds of kinetic energy on impact from a rigid object, has no exposed rotating parts that would lacerate skin, has no safety defect, is listed on a current FAA-accepted declaration of compliance for Category 3, and carries a Category 3 label. Remote pilot operating instructions apply as for Category 2.

The operating limits are the tighter part. Under Category 3 the remote pilot may not operate over an open-air assembly of people at all. The pilot may operate over people only in one of two ways: within or over a closed- or restricted-access site where every person inside has been notified that a small unmanned aircraft may fly over them, or without sustained flight over any person who is not directly participating in the operation or sheltered under a covered structure or inside a stationary vehicle.

Worked example: a surveying company uses a 4-pound Category 3 aircraft over an active construction site fenced off from the public. Every worker has been briefed that drones fly overhead. That is a closed-access site with notice, so sustained flight over the workers is allowed. The same aircraft over the public sidewalk next to the site may only transit over people, not hover.

Common misreading: assuming Category 3 is "Category 2 but more." It permits a heavier impact and in exchange forbids open-air assemblies and restricts sustained flight to controlled sites.
