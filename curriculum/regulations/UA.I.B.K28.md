Objective: know the rule on ADS-B Out in Part 107, and its relation to remote identification.

Section 107.53 says that unless otherwise authorized by the Administrator, no person may operate a small unmanned aircraft system under Part 107 with ADS-B Out equipment in transmit mode. ADS-B Out, Automatic Dependent Surveillance-Broadcast Out, is the equipment manned aircraft use to broadcast their position to air traffic control and to other aircraft. The rule names the equipment in transmit mode. It says nothing about receiving.

Part 89 closes the other door. Under 89.125, ADS-B Out equipment cannot be used to comply with the remote identification requirements of Part 89. Remote identification, covered in chapter 6, is its own broadcast with its own message elements. An ADS-B Out transmitter is prohibited under 107.53 and does not count as remote identification under 89.125.

Worked example: a pilot reads that ADS-B Out makes aircraft visible to everyone and installs a transmitter to "satisfy remote ID." The installation fails on both counts: transmitting breaks 107.53 unless authorized, and it does not meet Part 89.

Common misreading: confusing ADS-B Out with remote identification. They are different systems under different parts, and remote identification is the one Part 89 requires.
