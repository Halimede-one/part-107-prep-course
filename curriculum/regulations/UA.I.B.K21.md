Objective: know the four operating limitations in 107.51 and how they fit together.

Section 107.51 binds the remote pilot in command and the person manipulating the flight controls to four limits. The groundspeed of the aircraft may not exceed 87 knots, which is 100 miles per hour. The altitude may not be higher than 400 feet above ground level, unless the aircraft is flown within a 400-foot radius of a structure and no higher than 400 feet above the structure's immediate uppermost limit. The minimum flight visibility, observed from the location of the control station, is 3 statute miles. The minimum distance from clouds is 500 feet below the cloud and 2,000 feet horizontally from it.

AC 107-2A notes that these limits exist, among other reasons, to support the remote pilot's ability to identify an encroaching aircraft or a person on the ground and to act in time. All four can be waived under 107.205 when the applicant shows that the operation can be conducted safely under the terms of a certificate of waiver. Without a waiver they apply together, and they apply on top of every other rule, such as visual line of sight and airspace authorization. An airspace authorization may set a ceiling lower than 400 feet.

The four limits are taken one at a time in units 2.21a to 2.21d.

Worked example: a pilot plans a racing flight at 110 miles per hour at 300 feet. The altitude is fine, but the speed exceeds 100 miles per hour, so the flight needs a waiver of 107.51 or a slower aircraft.

Common misreading: treating 107.51 as the complete list of rules for a flight. It is the list of numerical limits. Line of sight, airspace, people on the ground, and the rest of Part 107 apply as well.
