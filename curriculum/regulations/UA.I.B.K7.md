Objective: know when a small unmanned aircraft may be operated from a moving vehicle, and when it may not.

Section 107.25 draws two lines. Operating a small unmanned aircraft system from a moving aircraft is prohibited outright. Operating from a moving land or water-borne vehicle is allowed only if the small unmanned aircraft is flown over a sparsely populated area and is not transporting another person's property for compensation or hire.

AC 107-2A adds the surrounding rules. The sparsely populated area condition can be waived under 107.200 and 107.205, but no waiver will be issued to allow carrying another person's property for compensation or hire from a moving vehicle. Everything else in Part 107 still applies: the remote pilot in command must keep visual line of sight, must ensure that no one is exposed to undue risk if control is lost, and must keep effective communication with a visual observer who rides in a different vehicle. State and local traffic laws apply to the people in the vehicle as well.

Worked example: a surveyor films a pipeline from a truck driving along a ranch road in open country. The area is sparsely populated, nothing is being carried for hire, and the aircraft stays in sight, so the operation is allowed. The same work from a boat moving through a busy harbor over a built-up waterfront is not, because the area is not sparsely populated. A courier launching a paid delivery from a moving van is not allowed anywhere.

Common misreading: thinking the rule bans all operations from vehicles. Only moving aircraft are banned outright. Moving land and water vehicles are allowed over sparsely populated areas, with the exception for property carried for hire.
