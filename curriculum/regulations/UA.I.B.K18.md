Objective: know what prohibited and restricted areas are, and what Part 107 requires before flying in one.

Section 107.45 says that no person may operate a small unmanned aircraft in a prohibited or restricted area unless that person has permission from the using or controlling agency, as appropriate. Both kinds of area are regulatory special use airspace, established in 14 CFR part 73 and depicted on aeronautical charts with their hours of operation, altitudes, and controlling agency.

The Aeronautical Information Manual describes them. A prohibited area is airspace within which the flight of aircraft is prohibited, established for security or other reasons associated with the national welfare. A restricted area is airspace within which flight is not wholly prohibited but is subject to restrictions, because of unusual and often invisible hazards such as artillery firing, aerial gunnery, or guided missiles. Entering a restricted area without authorization from the using or controlling agency may be extremely hazardous. The status of special use airspace can be obtained from the using or controlling agency, and an airspace NOTAM is issued when an area requires a NOTAM for activation. Other special use airspace, such as warning areas, military operations areas, alert areas, and national security areas, is not covered by 107.45, but a remote pilot should know what it is and fly with care in it. A national security area can be temporarily closed by NOTAM under 99.7, which unit 2.19 covers.

Worked example: a pilot plans to map a parcel of land that the sectional chart shows inside a restricted area. The pilot must obtain permission from the agency named on the chart before flying there. A restricted area that is not active may have been released to the controlling agency, which is why the status is worth asking for. A prohibited area is off limits without permission, whatever its status.

Common misreading: assuming that a restricted area is only a concern for aircraft at high altitude. The area reaches whatever floor the chart gives, often the surface, and 107.45 applies to small unmanned aircraft at any height inside it.
