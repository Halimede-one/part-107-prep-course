Objective: know the minimum distances from clouds and how they limit altitude on a cloudy day.

Under 107.51(d), the minimum distance of a small unmanned aircraft from clouds must be no less than 500 feet below the cloud and 2,000 feet horizontally from the cloud. AC 107-2A adds that the aircraft cannot be operated above any cloud, and that there can be no obstruction to visibility, such as a cloud, between the aircraft and the remote pilot in command. The practical effect on a day with a low cloud base is a ceiling on altitude: the aircraft must stay at least 500 feet below the base, so a base at 700 feet above the ground means a maximum altitude of 200 feet above the ground, even though 107.51(b) would otherwise allow 400 feet.

Local aviation weather reports give the cloud base, and the remote pilot in command should use the closest station that represents the operating area. Without a reliable way to determine the cloud clearance, there is no flight.

Worked example: the weather report gives a cloud base of 700 feet above ground level. The pilot limits the flight to 200 feet above ground level, which keeps the aircraft 500 feet below the base. A patch of low cloud drifting in 1,500 feet to the west would also require the pilot to move the aircraft or land, because 2,000 feet of horizontal clearance is required.

Common misreading: thinking that clouds do not matter below 400 feet, or applying the manned-aircraft idea of simply staying clear of clouds. Part 107 requires 500 feet below and 2,000 feet horizontally, and those numbers can lower the usable altitude well below 400 feet.
