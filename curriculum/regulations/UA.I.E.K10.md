Objective: know what "required components" and the category declaration mean for the aircraft a remote pilot flies over people.

An aircraft's eligibility for Category 2 or 3 is declared for a particular configuration. The declaration of compliance under 107.160 states the make, model, and serial numbers, the FAA-accepted means of compliance used, and the applicant's declaration that the aircraft, or specific configurations of it, satisfies 107.120(a) or 107.130(a) or both, has no safety defects, is covered by a product support and notification process, and may be inspected by the FAA. The aircraft must be listed on that accepted declaration and labeled for the category before it may be flown over people under 107.115 or 107.125.

The required components are how the configuration reaches the pilot. Under 107.120(b)(2)(i) and 107.130(b)(2)(i), the remote pilot operating instructions must include a system description with the required components, the system limitations, and the declared categories. AC 107-2A explains that if the applicant designed the aircraft with a component such as a parachute system that is supplied separately, the instructions must clearly identify the component that must be attached, and must list the components that are necessary or eligible for inclusion. The AC's sample instructions list, for each category, the airframe, the controller and ground station, a specific battery, and category-specific rotor blades and guards. An aircraft flown without a required component is not in its declared configuration, and so is not the aircraft the declaration covers.

Worked example: the operating instructions for a pilot's aircraft list "CAT 2 Rotor Blades & Guards" and a particular battery as required components for Category 2. The pilot flies with the guards removed to save weight. The aircraft is no longer in the configuration declared for Category 2, so the flight over people is not a Category 2 operation, whatever the label says.

Common misreading: thinking the label alone makes the aircraft eligible. The aircraft must also be in its declared configuration, with every required component in place.
