Objective: know who must keep which records, and for how long, under the operations-over-people rule.

Record retention in Part 107 falls on the people who make the aircraft eligible for operations over people, not on the everyday remote pilot. A person who submits a declaration of compliance must retain the records that support it, such as the test data and the means of compliance it relied on, for two years after that person stops producing the small unmanned aircraft system the declaration lists. A person who submits a means of compliance must retain its supporting records for as long as the means of compliance is accepted by the FAA plus two years.

These records must be made available to the FAA on request, which is the inspection duty in 107.7.

For the remote pilot the practical point is different: the records a pilot must carry or produce are the certificate, registration, and any waiver or authorization in effect, plus the remote pilot operating instructions for a Category 2 or 3 aircraft.

Worked example: a small company designs a 3-pound aircraft, tests it to the Category 2 impact limit, and submits a declaration of compliance. Four years later it stops making the model. It must keep the test records for two more years after that, and produce them if the FAA asks.

Common misreading: thinking the remote pilot must keep flight logs under Part 107. Part 107 does not require flight logs, although a waiver may.
