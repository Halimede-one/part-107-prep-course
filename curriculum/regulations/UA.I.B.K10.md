Objective: know what visual line of sight requires, who must keep it, and what does not count.

Section 107.31(a) requires that, with vision unaided by any device other than corrective lenses, the remote pilot in command, the visual observer if one is used, and the person manipulating the flight controls must be able to see the unmanned aircraft throughout the entire flight, in order to know its location; determine its attitude, altitude, and direction of flight; observe the airspace for other air traffic or hazards; and determine that it does not endanger the life or property of another. Under 107.31(b), that ability must be exercised throughout the flight either by the remote pilot in command and the person manipulating the controls, or by a visual observer.

AC 107-2A explains how the rule works in practice. The person keeping line of sight may look away briefly, for example to check the controller for battery life, as long as the capability to see the aircraft, or to maneuver it back into sight quickly, is retained. If sight is lost, it must be regained as soon as practicable, and there is no fixed time interval for which an interruption is acceptable. A pilot who cannot regain sight should follow predetermined procedures, such as landing, hovering, or return to home. Vision aids such as binoculars may be used only momentarily to enhance situational awareness. First-person-view devices may be used, but they do not satisfy the line-of-sight requirement. At night, the pilot cannot rely on the anti-collision light or telemetry displays alone. The requirement can be waived under 107.205, except for carrying another person's property for compensation or hire.

Worked example: a pilot flies in first-person-view goggles for a cinematic shot while a visual observer beside the pilot keeps the aircraft in unaided sight and reports its position. The observer satisfies 107.31(b)(2), so the flight is lawful. Without the observer, the pilot in goggles is not keeping visual line of sight.

Common misreading: thinking the camera feed or a map display counts as seeing the aircraft. It does not. The eye, with glasses or contact lenses at most, must be able to do it.
