Objective: know the right-of-way rule for small unmanned aircraft and what "yielding" means.

Section 107.37(a) says that each small unmanned aircraft must yield the right of way to all aircraft, airborne vehicles, and launch and reentry vehicles. Yielding means that the small unmanned aircraft must give way to the other aircraft or vehicle, and may not pass over, under, or ahead of it unless well clear. Section 107.37(b) adds that no person may operate a small unmanned aircraft so close to another aircraft as to create a collision hazard. There is no case in which a small unmanned aircraft has the right of way over a manned aircraft.

AC 107-2A calls this the "see and avoid" responsibility. To meet it, the remote pilot in command must know the location and flight path of the aircraft at all times, must be aware of other aircraft, persons, and property near the operating area, and must maneuver to avoid a collision. The remote pilot in command must act so that other aircraft do not need to maneuver to avoid the small unmanned aircraft. The yielding rule in 107.37(a) can be waived under 107.205; the collision hazard rule in 107.37(b) cannot.

This unit has one sub-element, 2.14a, on the see-and-avoid technique and the other hazards a remote pilot in command must consider.

Worked example: a helicopter approaches at low level while a pilot is inspecting a cell tower. The pilot descends and moves the aircraft away from the helicopter's path before the helicopter pilot has to do anything. Passing under the helicopter would be allowed only if well clear.

Common misreading: thinking a small unmanned aircraft has the right of way when it was there first, or over a slower aircraft. It yields to everything.
