Objective: know that a small unmanned aircraft may not carry hazardous material, and how that rule differs from the ones that can be waived.

Section 107.36 is one sentence: a small unmanned aircraft may not carry hazardous material. The term has the meaning given in the Department of Transportation's hazardous materials regulations, at 49 CFR 171.8. A remote pilot who is asked to carry a substance or article should check that definition before agreeing, because the Part 107 rule has no weight limit, no small-quantity allowance, and no exception for short flights.

Section 107.36 is not on the list of waivable rules in 107.205. AC 107-2A says that the carriage of any hazardous material on a small unmanned aircraft may only occur if the operator holds an exemption that permits it, and that petitions for exemption are governed by 14 CFR part 11. A certificate of waiver under 107.200 cannot do it.

Worked example: a client asks a pilot to fly a small container across a site. The container's shipping label marks the contents as hazardous material. The pilot may not carry it under Part 107. If the client's business needs such flights, the route is a petition for exemption under part 11, not a waiver request.

Common misreading: assuming a waiver is available for hazardous material, as it is for the altitude limit or operations at night. It is not. The only path is an exemption.
