Objective: know when an object may and may not be dropped from a small unmanned aircraft.

Section 107.23(b) prohibits allowing an object to be dropped from a small unmanned aircraft in a manner that creates an undue hazard to persons or property. The rule does not ban dropping objects. It bans dropping them in a way that creates an undue hazard. Where an object is released, what it is, and who is below all go into that judgment.

Two other rules frame this one. Under 107.49(e), before flight the remote pilot in command must ensure that any object attached to or carried by the aircraft is secure and does not adversely affect the aircraft's flight characteristics or controllability, so a release must be planned, not accidental. Under 107.36, the aircraft may not carry hazardous material at all, so a drop can never involve hazardous material.

Worked example: an agricultural operator releases seed from a small unmanned aircraft over a field with no one in it. The drop creates no undue hazard, so it is permitted. A promoter wants to drop coupons over a parade crowd from the same aircraft. Objects falling onto people create an undue hazard, so that drop is prohibited.

Common misreading: reading the rule as "nothing may ever be dropped." The rule is about the hazard a drop creates, not about dropping as such.
