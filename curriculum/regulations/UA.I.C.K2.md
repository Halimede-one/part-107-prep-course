Objective: know what a refusal to be tested for alcohol, or to release test results, does to a remote pilot certificate.

Section 107.59 makes two refusals grounds for action against the certificate. The first is refusing to submit to a test to indicate the percentage by weight of alcohol in the blood, when a law enforcement officer requests it in accordance with 91.17(c). Under that section, the officer must be authorized under state or local law to conduct the test, and must be investigating a suspected violation of a state or local law that prohibits the same kind of conduct as the federal rule: acting within 8 hours of drinking, under the influence, or at 0.04 or greater. The second is refusing to furnish, or to authorize the release of, test results that the Administrator requests under 91.17(c) or (d). The FAA may ask for the results of any alcohol or drug test taken within 4 hours after the person acted as a crewmember, when it has a reasonable basis to believe a violation occurred.

The consequences match those for the offense itself: denial of an application for a remote pilot certificate with a small UAS rating for up to 1 year after the date of the refusal, or suspension or revocation of a certificate already held. The refusal is a separate ground. It does not depend on what the test would have shown.

Worked example: after a drone strikes a parked car, a police officer with authority under state law suspects the remote pilot was drinking and asks for a breath test. The pilot refuses. The refusal is itself a ground for suspending or revoking the pilot's certificate, whatever the test would have shown.

Common misreading: thinking that refusing a test is safe because no result exists. The refusal carries the same consequences as the offense.
