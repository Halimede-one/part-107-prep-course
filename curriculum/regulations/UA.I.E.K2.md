Objective: know how the night rules and the operations-over-people rules combine.

AC 107-2A states the principle: the categories of operation over people and their restrictions do not change because of night. The tests, analyses, and inspections a manufacturer uses to show that an aircraft meets the safety requirements are neutral as to the time of day, and the risk mitigations apply equally by day and by night. So a flight over people at night must satisfy two sets of rules at once. From 107.29, the remote pilot in command must have completed the initial knowledge test or training after April 6, 2021, and the aircraft must have lighted anti-collision lighting visible for at least 3 statute miles with a flash rate sufficient to avoid a collision. From 107.39 and subpart D, the aircraft must be eligible for the category used and the flight must respect that category's limits, including the rule on sustained flight over open-air assemblies.

The light itself is part of the aircraft. For Category 1 that matters directly: the 0.55-pound limit in 107.110 includes everything on board or attached, so an anti-collision light adds to the weight the pilot must check. The AC notes that manufacturers declaring an aircraft eligible for Category 2 or 3 will most likely need to consider the mass of an anti-collision light in showing that the aircraft meets the injury-severity limit, which is why the remote pilot operating instructions may name the light as a listed component. The preflight check before any twilight or night flight must confirm that the light works and allow for its power draw, and the AC warns that reduced light and contrast make it harder to keep the aircraft in visual line of sight, so the operating area may need to be smaller.

Worked example: a pilot flies a 0.53-pound Category 1 aircraft over guests at an evening garden party. Clipping on a 0.05-pound strobe for the night flight takes the aircraft to 0.58 pounds, so it is no longer Category 1. The pilot needs a lighter light or an aircraft eligible for another category, as well as the night training.

Common misreading: thinking that night flight over people needs a waiver, or that night changes the category rules. No waiver is needed when both sets of rules are met, and neither set relaxes the other.
