Objective: know the general rule on flight over people and where the detailed rules are.

Section 107.39 says that no person may operate a small unmanned aircraft over a human being unless one of three things is true: the person is directly participating in the operation of the aircraft; the person is under a covered structure or inside a stationary vehicle that can provide reasonable protection from a falling small unmanned aircraft; or the operation meets the requirements of at least one of the operational categories in subpart D of Part 107. The first two are the general exceptions. The third points to the four categories of operation over people.

The categories, their eligibility rules, and their limits are the subject of chapter 5 (Task E), which this unit does not repeat. Two points from the operating rules are worth holding here. Under 107.49(f), when a flight will be over people under subpart D, the preflight check must confirm that the aircraft meets the requirements of the category being used. And AC 107-2A summarizes the open-air assembly rule: a Category 1, 2, or 4 operation may not sustain flight over an open-air assembly of people unless the aircraft meets the remote identification requirement of Part 89, and a Category 3 operation may never fly over an open-air assembly.

Worked example: a crew films a soccer match. The players and the spectators in the open stands are not participants, so no flight over them is allowed unless the aircraft and the operation meet one of the categories. A crew member holding a reflector beside the pilot is directly participating, so flight over that person is allowed.

Common misreading: thinking that anyone who agreed to be filmed is "directly participating." Participating means taking part in the operation of the aircraft, as the crew does.
