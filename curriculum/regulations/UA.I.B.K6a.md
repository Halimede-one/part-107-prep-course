Objective: know the standard for careless or reckless operation under Part 107.

Section 107.23(a) prohibits operating a small unmanned aircraft system in a careless or reckless manner so as to endanger the life or property of another. The words are the same ones used for manned aircraft. The test is whether the manner of operation endangers someone else's life or property. A flight can stay inside every numerical limit, such as 400 feet and 3 statute miles of visibility, and still be careless or reckless in how it is flown.

AC 107-2A notes that small unmanned aircraft bring additional considerations, so additional kinds of conduct can be careless or reckless. Its example is failing to consider the weather near structures, trees, or rolling terrain when operating in a densely populated area. The rule sits beside 107.19(c), which makes the remote pilot in command responsible for ensuring that the aircraft poses no undue hazard to other people, aircraft, or property if control is lost for any reason. A flight that assumes nothing will go wrong is the kind of flight 107.23(a) is aimed at.

Worked example: a pilot flies at 20 feet over a crowded beach to film swimmers. The flight is below 400 feet and in good visibility, but a motor failure would put the aircraft on a person. That endangers life, so it is careless or reckless operation. The same pilot filming from over the water at the edge of the beach, after assessing the wind and where people are, is operating with care.

Common misreading: believing that keeping to the numerical limits makes a flight lawful. The limits are necessary, not sufficient. The manner of the flight is judged on its own.
