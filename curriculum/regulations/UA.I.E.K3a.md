Objective: know the eligibility and operating requirements for Category 1.

To operate under Category 1, the small unmanned aircraft must weigh 0.55 pounds or less on takeoff and throughout the operation, including everything on board or attached to it, and it must have no exposed rotating parts that would lacerate human skin on impact with a person. There is no declaration of compliance and no label; the weight and the rotating-part protection are the whole test.

One operating limit applies on top: the remote pilot may not maintain sustained flight over an open-air assembly of people unless the aircraft meets the remote identification requirement in Part 89, either with standard remote identification or a remote identification broadcast module.

Worked example: a 249-gram aircraft with prop guards weighs 0.55 pounds exactly and has no exposed blade that can cut. It is eligible for Category 1 as sold. The pilot may fly it over people, and may hover over a crowd at a park concert only if the aircraft is broadcasting remote identification. Add a 20-gram accessory and it is over 0.55 pounds, and no longer Category 1.

Common misreading: assuming "under 250 grams" alone is enough. The rotating-part condition is separate, and the weight includes everything attached during the whole flight.
