Objective: know where prior authorization is required before operating, and how it is obtained.

No person may operate a small unmanned aircraft in Class B, Class C, or Class D airspace, or within the lateral boundaries of the surface area of Class E airspace designated for an airport, unless that person has prior authorization from Air Traffic Control. These are the controlled airspace classes that reach the ground around airports. Class G airspace, which is uncontrolled, needs no authorization under this rule.

The authorization is obtained before the flight, not by calling the tower from the field. The two routes are the Low Altitude Authorization and Notification Capability, known as LAANC, which gives near-real-time authorization through approved apps for many airports, and the FAA DroneZone website for airports LAANC does not cover or for requests outside the pre-approved altitudes. An authorization carries conditions, such as a maximum altitude in a given grid cell, and the pilot must comply with them.

Worked example: a pilot wants to photograph a building 3 miles from a mid-size airport with a control tower. The sectional chart shows the building inside the airport's Class D surface area. The pilot must obtain authorization first, for example through LAANC, and fly no higher than the authorized ceiling for that location. If the building were outside the Class D boundary in Class G airspace, no authorization would be needed, although the other rules such as not interfering with airport traffic still apply.

Common misreading: treating "near an airport" as the test. The test is the airspace class at the surface where you fly. Class E that begins at 700 or 1,200 feet above the ground is not a surface area, and does not require authorization for a flight at or below 400 feet.
