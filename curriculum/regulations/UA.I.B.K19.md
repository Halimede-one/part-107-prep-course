Objective: know which NOTAM-based flight restrictions bind a remote pilot in command, and the duty to check for them.

Section 107.47 requires a person acting as remote pilot in command to comply with 91.137 through 91.145 and 99.7. These sections are written for all aircraft, and 107.47 makes them apply to small unmanned aircraft. Each works through a notice to airmen, a NOTAM, which designates the area, the period, and the conditions. AC 107-2A says the remote pilot in command must check for NOTAMs before each flight to determine whether any airspace restriction applies.

The sections cover these situations. Section 91.137 is temporary flight restrictions in the vicinity of disaster or hazard areas, issued to protect persons and property from a hazard on the surface, to provide a safe environment for disaster relief aircraft, or to prevent unsafe congestion of sightseeing aircraft over an incident of public interest. When a restriction is issued for the first purpose, no aircraft may operate in the area unless it is participating in the relief activities under the direction of the official in charge. Section 91.138 is restrictions in national disaster areas in Hawaii. Section 91.139 is emergency air traffic rules. Section 91.141 is restrictions near the President, the Vice President, and other public figures. Section 91.143 is areas designated for space flight operations, closed except when authorized by ATC. Section 91.144 is restrictions during abnormally high barometric pressure, above 31 inches of mercury. Section 91.145 is aerial demonstrations and major sporting events; the restricted airspace for a sporting event is normally a 3 nautical mile radius and 2,500 feet above the surface, and for an aerial demonstration normally a 5 nautical mile radius, with the NOTAM issued at least 30 days in advance unless the FAA has good cause for less. Section 99.7 requires compliance with special security instructions in an ADIZ or Defense Area. FAA guidance adds that flying over any emergency or rescue operation, such as a wildfire or a hurricane response, is prohibited.

Worked example: a wildfire is burning 10 miles away and a pilot wants footage. A NOTAM under 91.137 covers the fire area. Only aircraft taking part in the relief effort under the official in charge may fly inside it, so the pilot may not. On another day the same pilot checks NOTAMs before a flight near a stadium and finds a sporting event restriction, so the flight waits until the event ends.

Common misreading: assuming that flight restrictions are for manned aircraft, or that staying below 400 feet avoids them. Section 107.47 makes them binding on remote pilots, and a restriction applies from the surface up to the altitude the NOTAM gives.
