Objective: know how Part 89 treats ADS-B Out, and why it is not a route to remote identification for a Part 107 flight.

Section 89.125 is direct: ADS-B Out equipment cannot be used to comply with the remote identification requirements of Part 89. Automatic Dependent Surveillance-Broadcast Out is the equipment manned aircraft use to broadcast their position to air traffic control and other aircraft. Remote identification is a separate broadcast with its own message elements, and only remote identification satisfies Part 89.

Part 89 mentions ADS-B in two other places, and both concern aircraft outside Part 107. Under 89.101(b), subpart B does not apply to unmanned aircraft operations under part 91 that are transmitting ADS-B Out under 91.225. Under 89.510, an aircraft produced under a part 21 design or production approval may, as an alternative to standard remote identification, be equipped with ADS-B Out that meets 91.225. Neither helps a Part 107 operation, because 107.53 prohibits operating under Part 107 with ADS-B Out in transmit mode unless the Administrator authorizes it.

Worked example: a pilot who also flies manned aircraft fits an ADS-B Out transmitter to a drone, expecting it to serve as remote identification for Part 107 work. It does not meet Part 89 under 89.125, and transmitting during a Part 107 flight breaks 107.53.

Common misreading: assuming that any position broadcast counts. Only remote identification counts under Part 89, and ADS-B Out is prohibited under Part 107.
