Objective: know what the remote pilot in command is responsible for, and how that responsibility is assigned and transferred.

Section 107.19 sets out the role. A remote pilot in command must be designated before or during the flight. The remote pilot in command is directly responsible for, and is the final authority as to, the operation of the small unmanned aircraft system. He or she must ensure that the aircraft will pose no undue hazard to other people, other aircraft, or other property if control is lost for any reason; must ensure that the operation complies with all applicable regulations; and must have the ability to direct the aircraft to ensure that compliance.

To act as remote pilot in command, a person must hold a remote pilot certificate with a small UAS rating and meet the recency requirement of 107.65. AC 107-2A adds that the certificate and personal identification must be in the pilot's physical possession and readily accessible during the flight. Control may pass between certificated remote pilots during an operation, provided both can keep the aircraft in visual line of sight without losing control. The transfer is communicated positively, and the pilot who takes over carries the responsibility from that moment.

This unit has one sub-element, 2.4a, which covers the one case in which someone other than the remote pilot in command may manipulate the flight controls.

Worked example: a two-person crew surveys a solar farm. Pilot A is designated remote pilot in command for the first battery. For the second battery, Pilot B says "I have control", Pilot A answers "you have control", and Pilot B becomes remote pilot in command. Both hold certificates and both can see the aircraft. Responsibility moves with the designation.

Common misreading: thinking the aircraft's owner, or the client who ordered the flight, is responsible for the operation. The remote pilot in command is, whoever owns the aircraft or pays for the work.
