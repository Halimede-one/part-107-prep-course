Objective: know the one-aircraft-at-a-time rule and what it covers.

Section 107.35 says that a person may not manipulate flight controls, or act as remote pilot in command or visual observer, in the operation of more than one unmanned aircraft at the same time. The rule attaches to the person, in any of the three roles. It applies whatever the aircraft are doing. AC 107-2A states that the use of automation does not allow a person to operate more than one small unmanned aircraft simultaneously: an aircraft holding a programmed hover or flying a programmed route is still being operated.

The rule is on the list in 107.205, so a certificate of waiver may authorize one person to operate more than one aircraft, for example for a drone light show. Without a waiver, each aircraft needs its own remote pilot in command, person on the controls, and, if used, visual observer.

Worked example: a pilot launches a first aircraft and leaves it hovering on autopilot, then launches a second to film it. The pilot is acting as remote pilot in command for two aircraft at the same time, which 107.35 prohibits. A company that flies a 50-aircraft light show does so under a waiver of 107.35.

Common misreading: believing that an aircraft on autopilot does not count. It counts. The rule looks at how many aircraft the person is involved in operating, not at who is moving the sticks.
