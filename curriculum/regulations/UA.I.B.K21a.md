Objective: know the maximum groundspeed and how to stay within it.

Under 107.51(a), the groundspeed of a small unmanned aircraft may not exceed 87 knots, which is 100 miles per hour. The limit is on groundspeed, the aircraft's speed over the ground, not on airspeed. With a tailwind the groundspeed is higher than the airspeed, so an aircraft can break the limit while its airspeed stays below it.

AC 107-2A gives three ways to know the groundspeed: a GPS device on the aircraft that reports groundspeed to the remote pilot, from which the pilot can also work out the wind and the airspeed for a given direction; timing the aircraft between two or more fixed points while allowing for the wind, and noting the power settings that keep it at or below 87 knots; or relying on the manufacturer's design limitations, such as an installed groundspeed limiter.

Worked example: a fixed-wing mapping aircraft cruises at 70 knots airspeed. On the downwind leg a 20-knot tailwind gives a groundspeed of 90 knots, over the limit. The pilot slows down on that leg or turns across the wind.

Common misreading: reading the limit as an airspeed. It is a groundspeed, and the wind counts.
